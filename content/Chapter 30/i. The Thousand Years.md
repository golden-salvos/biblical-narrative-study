## Overview

Revelation 20 is the only passage in Scripture that gives the messianic kingdom a length: a thousand years. Satan is bound in the Abyss, the saints reign with Christ, Satan is released for a final rebellion that ends in fire from heaven, and the dead stand before the great white throne. Revelation says comparatively little about the kingdom itself, so this session turns to the prophets who describe it at length: Isaiah's mountain of the Lord and the shoot of Jesse, Ezekiel's new heart, dry bones, and two sticks made one, Daniel's nations whose lives are prolonged, and Zechariah's yearly pilgrimage to the Feast of Tabernacles. Along the way it asks why the kingdom exists at all, why Satan must be released, and how a just God judges every human heart.

## 1. Recap

The previous session walked through Revelation 19. The [[Glossary#Great Multitude|great multitude]] in heaven celebrates God's judgment on Babylon, and the church watches that justice carried out from heaven, as rulers in training who are about to take the place of the rebellious powers that governed the nations. The marriage of the Lamb comes, the [[Glossary#Bride of Christ|Bride]] is clothed in fine linen, the righteous deeds of the saints, and those invited to the [[Glossary#Marriage Supper of the Lamb|marriage supper of the Lamb]] are blessed.

Then heaven opens, and the true rider on the white horse comes out: Faithful and True, the Word of God, [[Glossary#King of Kings and Lord of Lords|King of kings and Lord of lords]]. The [[Glossary#Antichrist|Antichrist]] brings his armies against Jerusalem and the mountains of Israel, and the Lord destroys that army where it stands. The beast is captured, and with him the [[Glossary#False Prophet|false prophet]], and the two of them are thrown alive into the [[Glossary#Lake of Fire|lake of fire]] that burns with sulfur (Revelation 19:20). Everyone else on the battlefield is killed. With Armageddon, the seven years are over.

That removes the human leadership of evil: the man who led the army, his religious mouthpiece, and every follower who took the [[Glossary#Mark of the Beast|mark of the beast]]. The session closed on a question: why a thousand years, and why doesn't the Lord go straight to the new heaven and the new earth? The answer begins with the covenant promises to Israel that remain unfulfilled, and with the one figure Revelation 19 left untouched. Revelation 20 deals with him first.

## 2. Satan Bound

*"Then I saw an angel coming down from heaven, holding in his hand the key to the bottomless pit and a great chain. And he seized the dragon, that ancient serpent, who is the devil and Satan, and bound him for a thousand years, and threw him into the pit, and shut it and sealed it over him, so that he might not deceive the nations any longer, until the thousand years were ended. After that he must be released for a little while."* (Revelation 20:1–3)

The beast and the false prophet are thrown into the lake of fire and are done away with, to rise no more. The [[Glossary#Dragon|dragon]] is treated differently. He is not destroyed. He is bound, thrown into the [[Glossary#Abyss|pit]], and sealed in for a thousand years, and the text says plainly that afterward he *must* be released for a little while.

That raises a large question. Satan has been free to deceive the nations for the entire span of human history. Why does the Lord chain him for this specific thousand years, and why let him out again at the end?

Two observations frame the answer. First, this is unprecedented. In this study's reading, Satan had access to heaven for all of his history until he was thrown out at the midpoint of the seven years, right before the [[Glossary#Abomination of Desolation|Abomination of Desolation]]. Now, for the first time in history, he is chained. Second, the Lord does nothing by accident. A thousand years with no Satan, followed by his release, is a deliberate design, and the rest of Revelation 20 and the prophets that describe this period explain it.

## 3. The Sabbath Rest

When Israel wandered in the wilderness, what did they picture when they thought of the Promised Land? A land flowing with milk and honey. The Lord spoke of it again and again as entering the land and entering *his rest*.

That language goes back to creation. God worked six days, and on the seventh he rested. On the traditional reckoning of biblical chronology, roughly six thousand years of human history lie behind us. In this study's reading, the [[Glossary#Millennial Kingdom|Millennial Kingdom]] is the seventh thousand-year period, and it is the [[Glossary#Sabbath Millennium|Sabbath rest]] for God's people ("with the Lord a day is like a thousand years," 2 Peter 3:8; compare Hebrews 4:9).

No passage of Scripture says outright that the millennium is the Sabbath rest. The pattern, though, matches everything else in the biblical story: six days of work and a seventh of rest, six years of planting and a seventh of rest for the land, a wilderness journey that ends in rest in the land. Early Christian writers read history this way (the *Epistle of Barnabas* 15, from the early second century, is the earliest), and so does Jewish tradition: the Talmud records a teaching that the world will exist for six thousand years (Sanhedrin 97a). The traditional Jewish calendar counts from creation and reached the year 5787 in the fall of 2026, so on its reckoning the six-thousandth year is still two centuries off; the various chronologies differ.

> **Key Principle:** The thousand years are the seventh "day" of human history. As God rested on the seventh day of creation, the Millennial Kingdom is the Sabbath rest he has prepared for his people.

To understand who is on the earth during those thousand years, and why, the place to begin is the Old Testament. The prophets describe this period at much greater length than Revelation does. The sequence is: what the Lord has planned for the Jews in the kingdom, who else is there, and then what happens at the end and why.

## 4. The Mountain of the House of the Lord

*"The word that Isaiah the son of Amoz saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem. It shall come to pass in the latter days that the mountain of the house of the Lord shall be established as the highest of the mountains, and shall be lifted up above the hills; and all the nations shall flow to it, and many peoples shall come, and say: 'Come, let us go up to the mountain of the Lord, to the house of the God of Jacob, that he may teach us his ways and that we may walk in his paths.' For out of Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem. He shall judge between the nations, and shall decide disputes for many peoples; and they shall beat their swords into plowshares, and their spears into pruning hooks; nation shall not lift up sword against nation, neither shall they learn war anymore. O house of Jacob, come, let us walk in the light of the Lord."* (Isaiah 2:1–5)

The prophecy concerns Judah and Jerusalem, and it describes the Millennial Kingdom. Notice what it requires. All the nations flow to Jerusalem. Many peoples come up to the house of the Lord and ask him to teach them his ways. That cannot happen if there are no nations besides Israel. So the Gentile nations, those left alive at the end, are present in the kingdom.

It is a time of peace, not war. Swords become plowshares, and no one learns war anymore. That detail reaches back to the spiritual history this study traced in Genesis. Who taught mankind war? In 1 Enoch's account, the [[Glossary#Watchers|Watchers]]: weaponry and metallurgy were among the forbidden knowledge they handed to humanity (1 Enoch 8:1), and it was one of the things the Lord held against them. In the kingdom, that instruction ends.

## 5. The Righteous Reign of the Branch

*"There shall come forth a shoot from the stump of Jesse, and a branch from his roots shall bear fruit. And the Spirit of the Lord shall rest upon him, the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the Spirit of counsel and might, the Spirit of knowledge and the fear of the Lord. And his delight shall be in the fear of the Lord. He shall not judge by what his eyes see, or decide disputes by what his ears hear, but with righteousness he shall judge the poor, and decide with equity for the meek of the earth; and he shall strike the earth with the rod of his mouth, and with the breath of his lips he shall kill the wicked. Righteousness shall be the belt of his waist, and faithfulness the belt of his loins."* (Isaiah 11:1–5)

Jesse is David's father. The [[Glossary#Branch (Shoot of Jesse)|shoot from the stump of Jesse]] is the [[Glossary#Messiah|Messiah]], Jesus. Does this describe the world today? No. There is no such justice on the earth, and the Lord is not yet ruling it. In this study's reading, this is a description of his reign in the Millennial Kingdom.

*"The wolf shall dwell with the lamb, and the leopard shall lie down with the young goat, and the calf and the lion and the fattened calf together; and a little child shall lead them... The nursing child shall play over the hole of the cobra, and the weaned child shall put his hand on the adder's den. They shall not hurt or destroy in all my holy mountain; for the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the Lord as the waters cover the sea. In that day the root of Jesse, who shall stand as a signal for the peoples—of him shall the nations inquire, and his resting place shall be glorious."* (Isaiah 11:6–10)

His resting place shall be glorious. Where is it? Jerusalem.

### The Second Gathering

*"In that day the Lord will extend his hand yet a second time to recover the remnant that remains of his people, from Assyria, from Egypt, from Pathros, from Cush, from Elam, from Shinar, from Hamath, and from the coastlands of the sea. He will raise a signal for the nations and will assemble the banished of Israel, and gather the dispersed of Judah from the four corners of the earth. The jealousy of Ephraim shall depart, and those who harass Judah shall be cut off; Ephraim shall not be jealous of Judah, and Judah shall not harass Ephraim."* (Isaiah 11:11–13)

The Lord is gathering his people, the Jews, home. They have been scattered to the four winds, and he brings them back to the land of Israel. Why single out Judah and Ephraim? They are the two halves of the [[Glossary#Divided Kingdom|divided kingdom]]: Judah in the south, and Ephraim, the leading tribe of the north. The Lord will not leave them divided. He unites them as one nation for the first time since the kingdom split after Solomon, and since Assyria carried the northern tribes away.

The chapter closes with a highway: *"And there will be a highway from Assyria for the remnant that remains of his people, as there was for Israel when they came up from the land of Egypt"* (Isaiah 11:16). Assyria is the land to the north and east, in northern Mesopotamia (today's northern Iraq and the neighboring parts of Syria and southeastern Turkey), the region the study has connected throughout with the Antichrist's kingdom. The return will be a second exodus.

## 6. Ezekiel: For the Sake of My Holy Name

Ezekiel 36 and 37 describe the same restoration in more detail.

*"Therefore say to the house of Israel, Thus says the Lord God: It is not for your sake, O house of Israel, that I am about to act, but for the sake of my holy name, which you have profaned among the nations to which you came... I will take you from the nations and gather you from all the countries and bring you into your own land. I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you shall be clean from all your uncleannesses, and from all your idols I will cleanse you. And I will give you a new heart, and a new spirit I will put within you. And I will remove the heart of stone from your flesh and give you a heart of flesh. And I will put my Spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes and be careful to obey my rules. You shall dwell in the land that I gave to your fathers, and you shall be my people, and I will be your God."* (Ezekiel 36:22, 24–28)

The Lord says it twice: it is not for Israel's sake that he acts, but for his own name. He promises abundant grain and fruit, no more famine, and a land so transformed that those passing by will say, *"This land that was desolate has become like the garden of Eden"* (Ezekiel 36:35). The nations that are left around Israel will know that he is the Lord.

### The Valley of Dry Bones

Ezekiel 37 opens with the famous vision of the [[Glossary#Valley of Dry Bones|valley of dry bones]]. The Spirit sets Ezekiel down in a valley full of bones, very many and very dry. *"Son of man, can these bones live?"* Ezekiel prophesies, and the bones come together, bone to its bone; sinews and flesh come upon them and skin covers them. Then he prophesies to the breath, and breath comes into them, and they stand on their feet, an exceedingly great army.

Then the Lord interprets it:

*"Son of man, these bones are the whole house of Israel. Behold, they say, 'Our bones are dried up, and our hope is lost; we are indeed cut off.' Therefore prophesy, and say to them, Thus says the Lord God: Behold, I will open your graves and raise you from your graves, O my people. And I will bring you into the land of Israel. And you shall know that I am the Lord, when I open your graves, and raise you from your graves, O my people. And I will put my Spirit within you, and you shall live, and I will place you in your own land."* (Ezekiel 37:11–14)

Not part of the house of Israel, but the *whole* house of Israel. The Lord says he will open their graves, raise them, and bring them into the land. Many interpreters read this as a picture of national restoration: the exiles say "our bones are dried up, and our hope is lost," and the graves are the graves of exile from which God brings the nation back to life. The study reads it more literally. The Lord explains the vision himself, and his explanation is a gathering, a raising from the graves, and a return to the land. In this study's reading, it describes an actual raising of the dead of Israel, though, as the previous session noted, this is not a point to stand on with full certainty.

What kind of raising is it? Not the kind the church receives. At the Lord's return the church is resurrected into its [[Glossary#Resurrection Body|resurrection bodies]], perfected and no longer subject to death. On this reading, this is the kind of raising Lazarus experienced: brought back to life in a mortal body, a man who would die again. Every person raised from the dead in the Old Testament and the Gospels was raised that way. Only Christ, the firstfruits, was resurrected into a glorified body. The house of Israel is brought back to life, into the land, as mortal people.

Jewish burial practice reflects a related hope. For centuries Jews have sought burial on the Mount of Olives, facing the Temple Mount, in the expectation that the resurrection of the dead will begin there when the Messiah comes.

### Two Sticks, One King

*"Son of man, take a stick and write on it, 'For Judah, and the people of Israel associated with him'; then take another stick and write on it, 'For Joseph (the stick of Ephraim) and all the house of Israel associated with him.' And join them one to another into one stick, that they may become one in your hand."* (Ezekiel 37:16–17)

Again the northern and southern kingdoms. The Lord explains:

*"I will make them one nation in the land, on the mountains of Israel. And one king shall be king over them all, and they shall be no longer two nations, and no longer divided into two kingdoms... My servant David shall be king over them, and they shall all have one shepherd. They shall walk in my rules and be careful to obey my statutes. They shall dwell in the land that I gave to my servant Jacob, where your fathers lived. They and their children and their children's children shall dwell there forever, and David my servant shall be their prince forever. I will make a covenant of peace with them. It shall be an everlasting covenant with them. And I will set them in their land and multiply them, and will set my sanctuary in their midst forevermore. My dwelling place shall be with them, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people. Then the nations will know that I am the Lord who sanctifies Israel, when my sanctuary is in their midst forevermore."* (Ezekiel 37:22, 24–28)

In this study's reading, "my servant David" is not David raised to the throne. It is the son of David, the root of Jesse of Isaiah 11: Jesus. (Some interpreters expect David himself to be raised as a prince under the Messiah.) What did the Lord promise David? That a descendant of his would sit on the throne of Israel forever. This is the fulfillment of the [[Glossary#Davidic Covenant|Davidic Covenant]]. Has any of this happened on the earth? No. In this study's reading, it happens in the Millennial Kingdom, and the reign it describes does not end when the thousand years do. *(What follows the thousand years is the subject of the next session.)*

## 7. After Armageddon: Every Promise Kept

Ezekiel 38 and 39 come immediately after this: the prophecy against [[Glossary#Gog|Gog]], which this study reads as describing the Battle of [[Glossary#Armageddon|Armageddon]]. At the end of chapter 39, after the Lord has called every carnivorous bird to feast on the bodies in the valley, he says what comes next:

*"And I will set my glory among the nations, and all the nations shall see my judgment that I have executed, and my hand that I have laid on them. The house of Israel shall know that I am the Lord their God, from that day forward... Therefore thus says the Lord God: Now I will restore the fortunes of Jacob and have mercy on the whole house of Israel, and I will be jealous for my holy name. They shall forget their shame and all the treachery they have practiced against me, when they dwell securely in their land with none to make them afraid... Then they shall know that I am the Lord their God, because I sent them into exile among the nations and then assembled them into their own land. I will leave none of them remaining among the nations anymore. And I will not hide my face anymore from them, when I pour out my Spirit upon the house of Israel, declares the Lord God."* (Ezekiel 39:21–22, 25–26, 28–29)

Have all the promises of God's covenants been fulfilled to Israel? In this study's reading, no. Each of the covenants carries promises that remain unfulfilled: the land, the nation, the throne, the Spirit. When does the Lord keep them? Here, in the Millennial Kingdom. He fulfills every covenant promise he made to the patriarchs and to the Jews.

> **Key Principle:** In this study's reading, the purpose of the thousand years is to fulfill the covenant promises God made to Israel. Every unfulfilled promise of the [[Glossary#Abrahamic Covenant|Abrahamic]], Davidic, and [[Glossary#New Covenant|New]] Covenants reaches its completion in the Millennial Kingdom.

## 8. A Kingdom With No One to Worship but God

Who is no longer on the earth? Satan. And with him, the rest of the fallen angels and the [[Glossary#Demons|demons]]. When Israel worshiped false gods throughout its history, whom was it worshiping? Demons and fallen angels, the beings behind the idols. They have lost their authority and their place. They are not around to govern.

So in the kingdom there is no false god to worship. Once the idols are destroyed, there is no one left to deceive. The Lord has taken away Satan, the false gods, the demons, and all those who practiced evil. For a thousand years, there is no Satan.

### The Spirit Poured Out and the Choice That Remains

On top of that, the Lord pours out his Spirit on the house of Israel. They have no Satan to tempt them. They have no evil king or empire to deal with. They serve under their Messiah in a perfectly just kingdom, governed perfectly, and they see their King every day in Jerusalem.

Does that make them all believers? Do they still have a choice? The Lord has removed many of the options for evil, but he has not removed choice. The pouring out of the Spirit and the presence of the Lord do not take a person's choice away.

David received the Spirit of the Lord (1 Samuel 16:13), and still sinned with Bathsheba, and came back to God. Saul also had the Spirit come upon him, and he rejected God (1 Samuel 10:10; 15:23; 16:14). Satan himself, before he rebelled, had everything Israel is about to be given. He knew the Lord as he is. There was nothing evil in the world to draw him away. He chose to rebel anyway.

Pouring out the Spirit is not the same thing as being born again. When the Lord pours out his Spirit, it is still his Spirit, offered; it has not yet been received by the individual. When a person is born again, their spirit is joined with his and they become a [[Glossary#New Creation|new creation]], and in this study's reading that cannot happen without that person's choice. To read Ezekiel's promise as God overriding Israel's [[Glossary#Free Will|free will]] is to assume something the text does not say. What the text says is that they are given an ideal situation to live in, far better than anything they were given before, with his presence among them. Nothing more should be read into it.

> **Key Principle:** God never removes free choice. In the Millennial Kingdom he removes the deceiver, the false gods, and the evil empires, pours out his Spirit, and lives among his people in person, and every person still chooses.

## 9. The Nations in the Kingdom

What about the Gentiles? Isaiah 2 already showed the nations streaming to Jerusalem. Daniel and Zechariah fill in the picture.

### Dominion Taken, Lives Prolonged

Daniel 7:11–12 sits in the middle of the vision of the [[Glossary#Four Kingdoms of Daniel|four beasts]], after the thrones are set and the [[Glossary#Ancient of Days|Ancient of Days]] takes his seat, and before the Son of Man receives dominion:

*"I looked then because of the sound of the great words that the horn was speaking. And as I looked, the beast was killed and its body destroyed and given over to be burned with fire. As for the rest of the beasts, their dominion was taken away, but their lives were prolonged for a season and a time."*

In this study's reading, the beast killed and given over to the fire is the Antichrist and his kingdom, exactly what Revelation 19 describes. The rest of the beasts are the other kingdoms of the vision: Babylon, Medo-Persia, and Greece. Their dominion is taken away, but their lives are prolonged. On this reading, those nations continue to exist, stripped of all authority. They are not destroyed as the Antichrist's kingdom is. They are allowed to remain.

### Zechariah 14

Zechariah 14 is the longest description of the Millennial Kingdom in the Bible. It opens on the battle the Antichrist brings against Israel:

*"Behold, a day is coming for the Lord, when the spoil taken from you will be divided in your midst. For I will gather all the nations against Jerusalem to battle, and the city shall be taken and the houses plundered and the women raped. Half of the city shall go out into exile, but the rest of the people shall not be cut off from the city. Then the Lord will go out and fight against those nations as when he fights on a day of battle. On that day his feet shall stand on the Mount of Olives that lies before Jerusalem on the east, and the Mount of Olives shall be split in two from east to west by a very wide valley... And you shall flee to the valley of my mountains... Then the Lord my God will come, and all the holy ones with him."* (Zechariah 14:1–5)

In this study's reading, that is a synopsis of the end: the Antichrist coming against Jerusalem and taking the city, the Lord telling his people to flee, and the Lord coming in his time with all his holy ones to fight for them and destroy their enemies.

Then the kingdom. On that day living waters flow out from Jerusalem, half to the eastern sea and half to the western sea, in summer and winter alike. *"And the Lord will be king over all the earth. On that day the Lord will be one and his name one"* (Zechariah 14:9). Jerusalem is lifted up and inhabited, *"for there shall never again be a decree of utter destruction. Jerusalem shall dwell in security"* (14:11). The armies that came against the city are struck with a plague: their flesh rots while they are still standing (14:12).

Then:

*"Then everyone who survives of all the nations that have come against Jerusalem shall go up year after year to worship the King, the Lord of hosts, and to keep the Feast of Booths. And if any of the families of the earth do not go up to Jerusalem to worship the King, the Lord of hosts, there will be no rain on them... This shall be the punishment to Egypt and the punishment to all the nations that do not go up to keep the Feast of Booths."* (Zechariah 14:16–17, 19)

The [[Glossary#Feast of Tabernacles|Feast of Booths]], or Tabernacles, is one of the seven [[Glossary#Appointed Times|appointed times]] of Leviticus 23, celebrated every year, and its theme is God dwelling among his people. In the kingdom, the peoples outside Israel are required to make a pilgrimage to Jerusalem each year to celebrate the Lord's presence among men. If they do not, there are consequences: no rain on their land. The text does not say for how long, but because the feast comes every year, the natural reading is that the drought lasts until the next one.

The chapter closes with holiness reaching into ordinary things. *"Holy to the Lord"* is inscribed on the bells of the horses, and every pot in Jerusalem and Judah becomes as holy as the bowls before the altar. *"And there shall no longer be a trader in the house of the Lord of hosts on that day"* (14:21).

This, in this study's reading, is the Millennial Kingdom as the Old Testament describes it. It is primarily for the Jews. The Lord is king in Jerusalem. There is a temple, and there are sacrifices. And he rules with absolute authority. This is not a democracy or a democratic republic. It is a King with absolute authority over the whole earth, governing all peoples from Jerusalem.

## 10. The Saints Reign With Him

Back in Revelation 20:

*"Then I saw thrones, and seated on them were those to whom the authority to judge was committed. Also I saw the souls of those who had been beheaded for the testimony of Jesus and for the word of God, and those who had not worshiped the beast or its image and had not received its mark on their foreheads or their hands. They came to life and reigned with Christ for a thousand years. The rest of the dead did not come to life until the thousand years were ended. This is the first resurrection. Blessed and holy is the one who shares in the first resurrection! Over such the second death has no power, but they will be priests of God and of Christ, and they will reign with him for a thousand years."* (Revelation 20:4–6)

Who sits on the thrones? The text says only those to whom authority to judge was given. To whom does the Lord give the kingdom? Daniel answers: *"the time came when the saints possessed the kingdom"* (Daniel 7:22). Whether each believer has a throne of their own is beside the point. What the text says is that the saints reign and rule with him.

Why the martyrs are named specifically: martyrdom is the defining characteristic of the [[Glossary#Great Tribulation|Great Tribulation]]. Those living in the last days who refuse the mark will be beheaded. That does not mean only martyrs reign. Daniel places no qualification on the saints who receive the kingdom (they are those who believe in him), and Revelation 20:6 extends the blessing to everyone who shares in the first resurrection.

### Two Resurrections

In this study's reading, the [[Glossary#First Resurrection|first resurrection]] is the Lord's return for the church: the [[Glossary#Rapture|rapture]]. The dead in Christ rise first, and those who are still alive are caught up together with them to meet the Lord in the air (1 Thessalonians 4:16–17). That is when believers receive their resurrection bodies, their white robes. (Amillennial interpreters, by contrast, read the first resurrection as spiritual: the new birth, or the believer's soul living and reigning with Christ in heaven after death.) The church is his bride. She goes to heaven, experiences the wedding feast, and comes back down with him when he comes to judge, because she is with him wherever he is.

Christ's own resurrection is not counted in this sequence; Scripture calls him the firstfruits of the resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:20, 23). "The first resurrection" here is the resurrection of the saints. The rest of the dead, everyone else, do not come to life until the thousand years are ended. That second resurrection takes place at the [[Glossary#Great White Throne Judgment|Great White Throne]].

Over those who share in the first resurrection, the [[Glossary#Second Death|second death]] has no power. They are priests of God and of Christ, and they reign with him for a thousand years.

### Doing Freely What the Angels Refused

Look at what has happened. Satan is out of the picture. The rest of the fallen angels and all the demons are removed. And, in this study's reading, the saints take their place. The [[Glossary#Divine Council|divine council]] was given authority over the nations and corrupted it. Now the church does what the angels refused to do, and does it freely.

This is the scene God was picturing from the beginning. He wants people who love him freely to serve him and reign with him. He created an order of beings, and all of them had a choice. He knew some would rebel. But the only way to arrive here, at a kingdom where everyone who has chosen him reigns with him, runs through all the rebellion, all the sin, and all the death that came with it. Otherwise, what would creation be? Robots. No one would have a choice, and without choice there is no love.

> **Key Principle:** The saints reigning with Christ is the goal God had in mind when he gave his creatures free will. The church takes the place the rebellious powers abandoned, and it serves freely, out of love.

## 11. Satan Released

*"And when the thousand years are ended, Satan will be released from his prison and will come out to deceive the nations that are at the four corners of the earth, Gog and Magog, to gather them for battle; their number is like the sand of the sea."* (Revelation 20:7–8)

Here is the part that confuses people. Satan has been locked up for a thousand years. Why release him?

Because mankind in the Millennial Kingdom still has a choice. Consider what everyone on the earth during those thousand years experiences: peace, rest, justice, the presence of God, and God himself ruling in person. Anyone who rebels against that is no different from Satan. Satan knew God as he is, entirely, and rejected him. That is why there is no repentance or forgiveness for Satan. He saw God as he was and chose against him. The people of the millennium, Jews and Gentiles alike, will know God too.

### The One Requirement

What was the one requirement in the Garden of Eden? One tree. What is the one requirement placed on the nations in the Millennial Kingdom? Go up to Jerusalem for the Feast of Tabernacles.

Will some refuse? Picture the nations that do not show up, and the drought that follows. Who brings the drought? The Lord. Who gets blamed? The Lord. It is the pattern of Cain. The Lord told him that if he did what was right he would be accepted, and Cain killed his brother instead. Some will not repent of their ways and will not follow the Lord. They will complain, and they will blame God.

So when Satan comes out to deceive, he finds a ripe crop. There will be people who are not happy, people who have chosen otherwise, and Satan will find them and give them a platform for rebellion. It is the whole story all over again, and that is the point.

Gog and Magog here names the peoples Satan gathers from the four corners of the earth. The names come from Ezekiel 38, where Gog is prince of Meshech and Tubal, peoples of Asia Minor, and Magog is his land. Why the names reappear here, a thousand years after the battle Ezekiel describes (as this study reads it), Scripture does not explain; it may simply be that "Gog and Magog" had become a name for the final enemies of God's people.

### Fire From Heaven

*"And they marched up over the broad plain of the earth and surrounded the camp of the saints and the beloved city, but fire came down from heaven and consumed them, and the devil who had deceived them was thrown into the lake of fire and sulfur where the beast and the false prophet were, and they will be tormented day and night forever and ever."* (Revelation 20:9–10)

Satan gathers everyone who has rejected God and rebelled against him and brings them against Israel. This battle does not even last as long as the last one. Fire comes down from heaven and consumes them.

Does the Lord show them mercy at that point? No. He gives them exactly what they want.

And then the devil is thrown into the lake of fire with the beast and the false prophet, to be tormented day and night forever and ever. People ask what hell is. This is it. If that does not make it clear, nothing will.

## 12. The Great White Throne

*"Then I saw a great white throne and him who was seated on it. From his presence earth and sky fled away, and no place was found for them. And I saw the dead, great and small, standing before the throne, and books were opened. Then another book was opened, which is the book of life. And the dead were judged by what was written in the books, according to what they had done. And the sea gave up the dead who were in it, Death and Hades gave up the dead who were in them, and they were judged, each one of them, according to what they had done. Then Death and Hades were thrown into the lake of fire. This is the second death, the lake of fire. And if anyone's name was not found written in the book of life, he was thrown into the lake of fire."* (Revelation 20:11–15)

All the dead stand before this throne. In this study's reading, the only ones who do not are those who have already been resurrected: the church, raised at the Lord's return. This is the second resurrection. The books are opened, and another book with them: the [[Glossary#Book of Life|book of life]].

Where is everyone destined to go whose name is not written in the book of life? To the same place as Satan, the Antichrist, the false prophet, and the rebel angels. The lake of fire is not reserved for them alone.

Death has never been the end. It ends the physical body, but it is only a transition. The spirit lives on, both good and bad, either with God or without him. And it is our choice.

This is also why the [[Glossary#Seven Trumpets|trumpets]] and the [[Glossary#Seven Bowls of Wrath|bowls of wrath]] look the way they do. God allows hell to be unleashed on the earth temporarily, to show people what is coming: a temporary glimpse of the destiny they are choosing.

## 13. A Just Judge

People ask how a loving God could throw all these people into the lake of fire. In this study's reading, the answer is that he gives them exactly what they asked for. He gave them a choice, the same as Satan, the same as the Watchers. They had their chance.

What about those who never heard the name of Jesus? Paul says God is visible in creation (Romans 1:19–20). The heavens declare his glory (Psalm 19:1). That testimony to a Creator does not take a book or a religion; it takes looking up at the stars, as David did and as Abraham did. Every person either glorifies him as Creator or does not, and so no one is without excuse. This is [[Glossary#General Revelation|general revelation]], and everyone has had it.

Before Christ, was anyone a Christian? Was Abraham? Was Moses? No. But Abraham believed God's promise, *"and he counted it to him as righteousness"* (Genesis 15:6). Abraham did not know the name of Jesus Christ. God knows the heart. He made us, and he knows us inside and out. The book of life has been written since the foundation of the world (Revelation 13:8; 17:8): before a person lives a single day, they are already in it or not. In this study's reading, that is not because God chose for them, but because he knows what they will choose. That is his [[Glossary#Foreknowledge|foreknowledge]]. He can fairly judge one heart from another. We cannot, and we should never try.

There is no hard-and-fast rule that says "do X, Y, and Z, and you are saved." The moment a qualifier is added, that only those who were preached to have had a chance, the argument collapses, because not every person in every era has heard the whole of God's plan. God judges the heart, and he is just. And he gives every person the ability to know him. In the end, no one goes to the lake of fire for any reason except their own choice: they were given every chance and still chose to rebel.

### Why Israel Receives What the Nations Do Not

The hardest part of this for many people is that Israel receives something the Gentiles do not: the whole house of Israel brought back into the land under its King. Is that fair?

What did God promise them? Something very different from what he promised the Gentiles. Apart from the covenant with Noah, made with all humanity and every living creature (Genesis 9:8–17), God made no covenants with the Gentile nations; the Gentiles are brought into the blessings of the New Covenant through Israel's Messiah (Ephesians 2:11–13). Every element of the Millennial Kingdom rests on a covenant promise God made directly to Abraham and his descendants. The Gentiles have had their own season, the [[Glossary#Time of the Gentiles|time of the Gentiles]], and God used Israel as the vessel to bring Christ to the world. And no people on the earth has been punished more in this life than the Jews. The Holocaust is only the modern tip of the iceberg.

The book of Job is the warning against judging this from a human standpoint. Job's friends tried, with good intentions, to explain why Job suffered. Their explanations sounded logical and helpful, and every one of them was wrong. Job's suffering had nothing to do with what Job had done and everything to do with the war in heaven and Satan's test. What happens on this earth is seen by God in a way we cannot see it.

A saying that is not from Scripture but communicates something true: *the same sun that hardens the clay softens the wax.* The sun is not responsible for how each responds. Like every analogy it breaks down if pressed too far, but its point holds. God's goodness and presence are the same; the response belongs to the one receiving them.

The philosophical question underneath all of this has one answer. If God wants, at the end, a people who freely love him, how does he get there? Of all the ways it could be done, there is only one. He has to create everything, give every being the ability to choose, place them in an environment where they can freely choose one way or the other, and let the choices fall where they may. That is the only way to arrive at a people who love him freely.

This is why Scripture says so much about not judging (Matthew 7:1). God does not want his people in the seat of judgment. That is not their role. The church will rule and reign with him, but it will not decide anyone's destiny. No believer sends anyone to hell at the Great White Throne.

> **Key Principle:** God judges the heart, not the circumstances of a person's birth. He has made himself known to every person through creation, he knows every choice before it is made, and he gives every person exactly what they choose.

## 14. What It Means to Reign

What does it look like to rule and reign with Christ? Look at what the Watchers were supposed to do: watch over, protect, and guide. They were judged in part because they interfered with the affairs of humans for their own ends. Authority in the kingdom is the opposite: the posture of a leader who asks what Jesus would do if he were in this position (Mark 10:42–45).

Christ remains the ultimate authority. The saints have made their choice to be there, but they do not decide what happens to other human beings. And the one requirement for ruling and reigning effectively is love. *"By this all people will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another"* (John 13:35). Anyone without love, love that includes everyone else, has no right to rule or reign, and never will.

## 15. Looking Ahead: The End of the Story

Revelation 21 and 22 are the subject of the next session: the new heaven and the new earth, the New Jerusalem, and the last words of Jesus. *(This will be developed in the next session.)* The story began in Genesis in the garden, in paradise. It ends when everything is finished and God's people are back in paradise. What that looks like is very different from most people's perception of heaven.

Some of the details of Revelation are confusing, but the story is simple. It is a love story, an epic, a story of returning home, with all the elements of a story, which the Lord tells from the beginning to the end: what happened, the conflict, the enemies, what he is doing to destroy them, and how he will restore all things. It is not broadcast plainly; the enemy is not advertising the plan. That is why the Lord says *"seek, and you will find"* (Matthew 7:7). It takes curiosity and a desire to know.

And where the details are not clear, the relationship fills the gaps. When we do not understand where we are, we trust the one who does, and he shows us more.

For a narrative walk through the whole story from Jesus' perspective, beginning, as this study does, with Genesis 3:15, Joel Richardson's *The Gospel of the Skull Crusher* is a short, well-illustrated companion.
