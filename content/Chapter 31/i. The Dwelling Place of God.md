## Overview

The story that began in a garden ends in a city. Revelation 21 and 22 show a new heaven and a new earth with no more sea, the New Jerusalem coming down out of heaven as a bride, a city shaped like the Holy of Holies with no temple in it, because "its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb," and the river and tree of life restored. This session reads those chapters through the last of Israel's seven feasts, the Feast of Tabernacles, God dwelling with his people, and asks why it is the last to be fulfilled. Along the way it considers where sin lives and why the new creation is the first time there is none, what "let the evildoer still do evil" teaches about free will and the limits of human control, and why the end of the story is not a place but a presence.

## 1. Recap

The previous session walked through Revelation 20 and the thousand-year reign of Christ. The [[Glossary#Millennial Kingdom|Millennial Kingdom]] is sometimes described as a second chance for the Jews, but that is not its main purpose. Its purpose is to fulfill the covenant promises.

That is why the study spent so much time on the four covenants God made with Israel. In this study's reading, covenants are different from prophecies. They are promises, sworn by God, and they are non-negotiable. If God promised something to Israel that has not yet happened in history, it is going to happen. The Millennial Kingdom is where it happens. The whole book is centered on the Jews because they are the one nation out of all the nations that belongs to God as his own, and everything in the thousand years is aimed at fulfilling what he promised to Abraham, to Moses, to David, and through Jeremiah in the [[Glossary#New Covenant|New Covenant]].

The Gentiles benefit too. There will be other nations on the earth during the Millennial Kingdom, and they will need to find God where he is: reigning from Jerusalem, enthroned in the temple. So they are required to come to him. Every year, the nations must make a pilgrimage to Jerusalem during the [[Glossary#Feast of Tabernacles|Feast of Tabernacles]] to worship the King, and there are consequences for those who stay home:

*"Then everyone who survives of all the nations that have come against Jerusalem shall go up year after year to worship the King, the Lord of hosts, and to keep the Feast of Booths. And if any of the families of the earth do not go up to Jerusalem to worship the King, the Lord of hosts, there will be no rain on them."* (Zechariah 14:16–17)

At the end of the thousand years, Satan was released, gathered the nations against Jerusalem one last time, and the rebels were consumed by fire from heaven. Satan was thrown into the [[Glossary#Lake of Fire|lake of fire]], the [[Glossary#Great White Throne Judgment|Great White Throne Judgment]] was held, and the first earth passed away. That is where Revelation 21 begins.

## 2. The Seven Feasts and the Last One

Before reading Revelation 21, it helps to go back to Leviticus. There are two chapters in Leviticus worth remembering by number. Leviticus 26 is where Moses lays out the blessings for keeping the Law and the curses for breaking it. Leviticus 23 is the calendar: the weekly Sabbath, and then the seven annual feasts of the Lord, each fixed to its own time of year, the [[Glossary#Appointed Times|appointed times]].

The first four are the spring feasts, and Jesus has already fulfilled every one of them. [[Glossary#Passover|Passover]] is the Passover lamb (1 Corinthians 5:7). Unleavened Bread pictures the sinless sacrifice, his body without the leaven of sin laid in the tomb; it is listed with Passover in the same paragraph and kept at the same time, but it was fulfilled separately. [[Glossary#First Fruits|First Fruits]] is the resurrection: Jesus is the first of all the people who will one day be raised. And the Feast of Weeks, Pentecost, came fifty days later, when the Holy Spirit was poured out and three thousand were added to the church in a single day. His death, his burial, his resurrection, and the coming of the Spirit: the first four feasts, each fulfilled on the day Israel was already keeping.

Three remain: the Feast of Trumpets, the Day of Atonement, and the Feast of Booths, or Tabernacles. The feasts were always remembrance feasts that also looked forward. Israel kept them anticipating a day when each one would be fulfilled, whether or not they recognized the fulfillment when it came.

In this study's reading, the **Feast of Trumpets** is fulfilled at the last trumpet, the seventh, when the Lord takes control of the earth and begins to reign. The trumpets and the bowls are a progressive revelation of his wrath, and the seventh trumpet is where his reign begins. That is why a feast of trumpets is a celebration and not a dread: the trumpets bring judgment, but the last trumpet announces the King.

The **Day of Atonement**, on this reading, is fulfilled at the end of the Millennial Kingdom. After Satan is thrown into the lake of fire, the Lord sets up the Great White Throne Judgment, and every person who has ever lived stands before it. How is anyone atoned for at that throne? Only through Jesus. The only way to be justified is to be found in the [[Glossary#Book of Life|book of life]].

The **Feast of Tabernacles** is the last. During the Millennial Kingdom the nations come up to Jerusalem every year to keep it. Jesus is on the earth, and they come to him there, but they are still keeping a feast, still observing a reminder of something. Something different happens after the Day of Atonement, in the new heaven and the new earth, and that difference is the key to this whole session.

### What the Tabernacle Meant

Why would Tabernacles be saved for last? Start with what the [[Glossary#Tabernacle|tabernacle]] meant to Israel. It was the tent of meeting, and more than a gathering place, it was where God dwelt. He was tabernacling: present with his people. But that presence came with severe rules. The Holy of Holies could be entered once a year, only by the high priest, and only with sacrifice. God lived among them, and a curtain stood between him and everyone else.

The Feast of Tabernacles itself is still celebrated today. Jewish families build booths outside and eat in them, and some sleep in them, remembering the years Israel lived in tents in the wilderness while God dwelt among them in his own tent. The question to carry into Revelation 21 is this: why is the feast about God dwelling with his people the last one to be fulfilled?

## 3. A New Heaven and a New Earth

*"Then I saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth had passed away, and the sea was no more."* (Revelation 21:1)

Everything is new from here on. The first earth has passed away (Peter describes the heavens and earth being dissolved by fire, 2 Peter 3:10–13), and what John sees is the [[Glossary#New Heaven and New Earth|new heaven and new earth]]: a new creation.

The disappearance of the sea is about more than oceans and salt water. Throughout the Bible the [[Glossary#Sea (Biblical Symbol of Chaos)|sea]] represents chaos. Daniel's four beasts rose out of it. The beast of Revelation 13 rose out of it. Even in the flood, Genesis says only that everything that breathed on dry land died (Genesis 7:22); the sea and its creatures came through. The sea carried over from the old world as a kind of throwback to chaos. In the new heaven and new earth there is no sea, because there is no chaos. There is no sin, and there is no Satan.

## 4. The Dwelling Place of God Is With Man

*"And I saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband. And I heard a loud voice from the throne saying, 'Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man. He will dwell with them, and they will be his people, and God himself will be with them as their God. He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more, neither shall there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain anymore, for the former things have passed away.'"* (Revelation 21:2–4)

This is the Feast of Tabernacles. *Behold, the dwelling place of God*: where? *With man.* No one needs to keep the feast any longer, because the thing the feast anticipated has arrived. There is nothing left to look forward to; his dwelling is here, with his people.

Now notice what is being said. Who was on the earth during the Millennial Kingdom? Jesus, the Jews, and whoever was left of the Gentiles. Jesus was already on the earth. He was already reigning from Jerusalem as the King who will reign forever. So what is different about this? Something is very different about this time compared to the Millennial Kingdom, and the rest of the chapter builds toward it.

## 5. The Alpha and the Omega

*"And he who was seated on the throne said, 'Behold, I am making all things new.' Also he said, 'Write this down, for these words are trustworthy and true.' And he said to me, 'It is done! I am the Alpha and the Omega, the beginning and the end. To the thirsty I will give from the spring of the water of life without payment.'"* (Revelation 21:5–6)

The promise continues: the one who conquers will inherit these things, and God will be his God and he will be his son. But the cowardly, the faithless, the detestable, murderers, the sexually immoral, sorcerers, idolaters, and all liars have their portion in the lake that burns with fire and sulfur, which is the [[Glossary#Second Death|second death]] (Revelation 21:7–8).

The one seated on the throne calls himself the [[Glossary#Alpha and Omega|Alpha and the Omega]] here, as "the Lord God" did at the opening of the book (1:8), and in the next chapter Jesus claims the same title for himself without ambiguity (22:13, 16). That raises a question. John 1:1 says that in the beginning was the Word, the Word was with God, and the Word was God, and that nothing was made without him. If he is God, he was never created and he will never die. How can he have a beginning and an end?

The answer is in what this book is. In the purest sense of the word, history is his story. In this study's reading, the beginning he speaks of is Genesis 1:1: creation. Without him there is no us, no earth, no creation. He is our beginning. And from the standpoint of the story that runs from Genesis to Revelation, he is also its end.

Think about everything he has seen and done between the two. He created. He witnessed the corruption of mankind, sin and death entering the world. He came to the earth to bring salvation in the only way it could be brought, and he redeemed mankind. And now he has rid creation of all corruption: every being, every entity, every human who brought evil into this world has been destroyed and put in the lake of fire. He has already begun to reign; he did that in the Millennial Kingdom. So what is the last thing left for him to do? Hold the question a little longer. Even in the Garden of Eden, before Adam and Eve sinned, someone had already sinned. Something that has never once been true in all of history is about to be true.

## 6. The Bride, the Wife of the Lamb

*"Then came one of the seven angels who had the seven bowls full of the seven last plagues and spoke to me, saying, 'Come, I will show you the Bride, the wife of the Lamb.' And he carried me away in the Spirit to a great, high mountain, and showed me the holy city Jerusalem coming down out of heaven from God, having the glory of God, its radiance like a most rare jewel, like a jasper, clear as crystal."* (Revelation 21:9–11)

The city has a great, high wall with twelve gates, and at the gates twelve angels. On the gates are inscribed the names of the twelve tribes of Israel: three gates on the east, three on the north, three on the south, and three on the west. The wall has twelve foundations, and on them are the names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb (Revelation 21:12–14). Israel and the church are written into the same structure: the tribes on the gates, the apostles on the foundations. The city is the [[Glossary#Bride of Christ|Bride]].

### A City Four Square

The angel measures the city with a golden rod, and *"the city lies foursquare, its length the same as its width. And he measured the city with his rod, 12,000 stadia. Its length and width and height are equal"* (Revelation 21:16).

We tend to picture things in two dimensions: a footprint on the ground. But this is not just a square. It is a cube, as high as it is long and wide.

An audience member saw the connection immediately: that is the shape of the Holy of Holies. The innermost room of the temple, the one place God's presence dwelt and the high priest could enter once a year, was a perfect cube. The whole city now has that shape.

Twelve thousand stadia is about 1,380 miles. For a sense of scale, the United States is roughly 3,000 miles from coast to coast; this city would span almost half of it. Set the same footprint down over the Middle East and it reaches from the Nile all the way to the Euphrates and up into Turkey. River to river. In this study's reading that matters, because it is the original dimension of the land God promised to Abraham: *"from the river of Egypt to the great river, the river Euphrates"* (Genesis 15:18). Israel has never possessed that land as its own; even Solomon's empire held much of it only as tribute-paying kingdoms (1 Kings 4:21). Here the promise is fulfilled. (Many interpreters read the city's dimensions symbolically instead, as twelve times a thousand, the number of God's people multiplied to perfection.)

The city is not being set down on top of present-day Jerusalem. This is a new heaven and a new earth; everything is new. The New Jerusalem represents everything Jerusalem was, and now it completely replaces it.

### I Go to Prepare a Place for You

Before he ascended, Jesus told his disciples what he was going to do:

*"In my Father's house are many rooms. If it were not so, would I have told you that I go to prepare a place for you? And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again and will take you to myself, that where I am you may be also."* (John 14:2–3)

This is that place: the [[Glossary#New Jerusalem|New Jerusalem]], prepared for the Bride.

Earlier in the session an audience member raised a Jewish wedding custom that fits the picture, which she called "flying the bride": the bride is lifted up on a chair while the guests dance. It is often explained as recalling the way a groom came for his bride. In the popular retelling of the ancient custom (some of whose details come from later sources and are less certain than often presented; see the Context note for Chapter 29), the groom's father would tell him when he had added on to the house enough, when he had made a place for his bride and could go and get her. The groom would go through the streets, calling his friends to come with him, and people would gather and join the procession. The bride had to be ready at a moment's notice. They would put her in the chair and carry her to wherever the wedding feast was going to be, and the feast would last for about a week.

When Jesus comes back for his Bride, he catches his people up in the air with him. The dead in Christ rise first, and then those who are still alive are caught up together with them to meet the Lord (1 Thessalonians 4:16–17). There are practices in Jewish culture whose significance is not always understood even by those who keep them, and the picture they paint is beautiful once it is seen.

### Walls, Gold, and Pearl

The angel also measures the wall: 144 cubits, *"by human measurement, which is also an angel's measurement"* (Revelation 21:17), something over two hundred feet. The wall is built of jasper, and the city itself is pure gold, *"like clear glass"* (Revelation 21:18). Not only is the city enormous; it is made of gold so pure it is transparent. From a human standpoint it boggles the mind. What exactly that means, no one can say, and the colors in the description are not the point.

The foundations of the wall are adorned with every kind of jewel, one for each foundation: jasper, sapphire, agate, emerald, onyx, carnelian, chrysolite, beryl, topaz, chrysoprase, jacinth, and amethyst. The twelve gates are twelve pearls, each gate made of a single pearl: an entire gateway, for people to go in and out, cut from one pearl. And the street of the city is pure gold, like transparent glass (Revelation 21:19–21).

## 7. No Temple in the City

*"And I saw no temple in the city, for its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb."* (Revelation 21:22)

During the Millennial Kingdom there was a temple, and there were sacrifices. Ezekiel 40–48 describes it in detail: exactly what the temple looks like, the Lord coming to the temple, the sacrifices, and where each of the twelve tribes is located in the land. In the New Jerusalem there is no temple at all. The temple is God himself.

In this study's reading, this is the key. Who was present in the Millennial Kingdom? Jesus. Who has been present from Genesis to Revelation as the representative of God on the earth? Jesus (John 1:18). So why is this the first time we see God the Father descending to live with man?

Because of what Jesus had to accomplish before Revelation 21 could happen. To make it possible for his Father to dwell on the earth with mankind, he first had to perfect humanity. He first had to rid creation of sin and death and everything evil. At the close of Revelation 20, all of it has been put away. The new heaven and new earth are perfect, because there is no sin.

> **Key Principle:** In this study's reading, Jesus has been the presence of God on the earth from Genesis to the Millennial Kingdom. The Father descends to dwell with mankind only once sin itself is gone. That is the fulfillment of the Feast of Tabernacles, and the reason it is the last feast to be fulfilled.

### Where Sin Lives

An audience member pressed the point. Believers sin every day. Won't they still sin in the new creation? How is that any different from now?

The answer turns on a question theologians debate: what is the sin nature, and where does it live? The instinctive answer is the heart. Some teach that the sin nature is embedded in our essential identity, part of our spirit. If that were true, it would never die, because our spirit never dies. What does die is the flesh. Paul is clear in Romans that sin dwells in the flesh: *"For I know that nothing good dwells in me, that is, in my flesh"* (Romans 7:18). In this study's reading, before a person is saved, they are caught in exactly what Paul describes: the thing they do not want to do, they do, and the thing they want to do, they do not (Romans 7:15–20). (Interpreters have long debated whether Paul is describing life before conversion or the ongoing struggle of a believer.) The flesh is tied to the spirit, and they cannot disobey it. And the flesh is not only the physical body. It includes the mind and the will as they operate on their own.

What does baptism represent? Not only washing, and not only new birth, but, in this study's reading, what has to happen before new birth: death. The flesh has to die so that we can be reborn (Romans 6:3–6). What happens at that moment is that the flesh is separated from the spirit, so that the spirit can be in union with the Holy Spirit. Could the Holy Spirit be in union with us if our spirit were still bound to our flesh? No. As Christians we are separated, even though we still live in this body. That is why Paul urges believers to offer their bodies as a living sacrifice (Romans 12:1) and let the Spirit quicken them to use those bodies for good works.

This is the conversation Jesus has with Nicodemus. You must be born again (John 3:3–7), and to be born again, you have to die to this life. Before, we had no choice with sin; the flesh had control. Now, separated from it and joined to the Holy Spirit, we have a choice. We can still choose to go after the flesh, but we are no longer bound to it. We are, as Paul says, a [[Glossary#New Creation|new creation]]: *"the old has passed away; behold, the new has come"* (2 Corinthians 5:17).

The picture is like the conception of a child. A man and a woman come together, a part of each joins, and there is a new creation. The same is true when the Holy Spirit comes to us. We have been separated from the flesh in order to be united with him, and what results is new.

At that point a transformation has occurred. Can it be reversed? Can the butterfly crawl back into the cocoon and turn back into a caterpillar? In this study's reading, no. (Christians have long disagreed about whether a true believer can fall away, and the question is taken up separately.) The transformation is done. In this life the flesh is still with us. It still has influence on us, and we have influence over it, and whichever one we feed determines the direction we go. But the new creation never dies, and nothing can separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord (Romans 8:38–39). When we die physically, the only thing that is lost is the flesh. We are the same. What we are is wrapped in a new body, the [[Glossary#Resurrection Body|resurrection body]]. And this is eternal life, Jesus said: *"that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent"* (John 17:3), a life that begins the moment we are born again.

### The First Time Without Sin

So when believers stand in their resurrection bodies, what have they been completely separated from? In this study's reading, the flesh. That is the difference between the new creation and the Millennial Kingdom. During the thousand years, when Satan was released, the flesh was still there for him to appeal to, and the nations followed him. Now Satan is never coming back, and there is no flesh for anything to appeal to.

For the first time in human history, at the point when the Great White Throne Judgment has happened and all sin has been put away, God's people stand in resurrected bodies on a new earth, in the New Jerusalem. It is the first time God, in all his holiness, can be in the presence of humanity. It is the first time there is no sin anywhere to separate him from us. That is the fulfillment of the Feast of Tabernacles: God dwelling on the earth with his people. It is something human beings will probably never be able to fully grasp.

## 8. The Light of the City

*"And the city has no need of sun or moon to shine on it, for the glory of God gives it light, and its lamp is the Lamb."* (Revelation 21:23)

By its light the nations walk, and the kings of the earth bring their glory into it. Its gates are never shut by day, and there is no night there. The glory and honor of the nations are brought into it. But nothing unclean will ever enter it, nor anyone who does what is detestable or false, only those who are written in the Lamb's book of life (Revelation 21:24–27).

## 9. The River and the Tree of Life

*"Then the angel showed me the river of the water of life, bright as crystal, flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb through the middle of the street of the city; also, on either side of the river, the tree of life with its twelve kinds of fruit, yielding its fruit each month. The leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations."* (Revelation 22:1–2)

No longer will there be anything accursed. The throne of God and of the Lamb will be in the city, and his servants will worship him. They will see his face, and his name will be on their foreheads. Night will be no more; they will need no lamp or sun, because the Lord God will be their light, and they will reign forever and ever (Revelation 22:3–5).

The [[Glossary#Tree of Life|tree of life]] was last seen in Eden, guarded by cherubim after the Fall. Now it stands in the open, beside the river, in the middle of the city. What was lost in the garden is restored.

## 10. Do Not Seal Up the Words

The angel tells John that these words are trustworthy and true, that the Lord has sent his angel to show his servants what must soon take place, and that Jesus is coming soon. When John hears and sees these things, he falls down to worship at the feet of the angel who showed them to him, and the angel stops him: *"You must not do that! I am a fellow servant with you and your brothers the prophets, and with those who keep the words of this book. Worship God"* (Revelation 22:6–9).

Then: *"Do not seal up the words of the prophecy of this book, for the time is near"* (Revelation 22:10).

That is the opposite of what Daniel was told. At the end of Daniel, Daniel kept asking questions, and the answer was to shut up the words and seal the book until the time of the end (Daniel 12:4, 9). Revelation is the revealing of everything Daniel spoke of and saw.

## 11. Let the Evildoer Still Do Evil

*"Let the evildoer still do evil, and the filthy still be filthy, and the righteous still do right, and the holy still be holy."* (Revelation 22:11)

Recall what the study said about [[Glossary#Hardening|hardening]] and [[Glossary#Free Will|free will]]. This verse is a picture, from God's perspective, of how it all works. There are going to be people who, despite everything that is written and everything that is in creation, still make their choice. And God says: let them. That is the way he created the world.

So can morality be dictated? Can people be made to do certain things? Is that what God wants his people to do? If God chose not to control humanity and its choices, what gives anyone else the right to take that control?

An audience member pushed back that this sounds fatalistic, as though believers should sit back and let evil go. We are still responsible for our own sins and actions, she said; we are still supposed to take a stand and make a difference. All of that is true, and it is not in conflict with the verse. People will make their own choices, and believers can still do everything they can to make things as good as possible for the people around them. The point is that influence is not control.

The American system offers a useful comparison. In this study's view, its laws were founded on Judeo-Christian values: moral law from God, through the Bible, translated into civil law, much as the Levitical law was. The way the system works is freedom: people have the freedom to do what they want, until it infringes on the rights of another. The law is enforced because everyone agrees that it is the law. When people begin to challenge the foundation beneath it, when they no longer agree with the values that underlie the law, the system develops a fundamental problem. At what point do you force them?

That is where the question of the minority comes in. Does might make right? That is how many authoritarian systems, in the Middle East and elsewhere, work. The founders understood that if you negate and marginalize the minority, you will eventually find yourself marginalized. If you do not treat with respect the people who disagree with you, the system stops working. For many of the early settlers, the whole point of America was religious freedom; it is why they crossed from Europe. Religious freedom means people may choose what they believe and live by it, up to the point where the exercise of their beliefs infringes on the rights of others.

That is a constitutional standard. God has a higher one: every human being makes their own choices. This is where it gets hard, because people take issue with God when bad things happen to good people. But if God has established that all humans have free choice, bad things are going to happen to good people, because people freely exercise their will, and God has allowed that to stand for our time on this earth. When we start playing God and dictating how people shall behave, we take the rules he set up into our own hands. That calls for great care. None of this means Christians should not do good. They should protect the fatherless and the widows and anyone who cannot protect themselves from predators. But sometimes we take it too far, trying to legislate and dictate how people will behave from our own human perspective. There is only one Judge, which is why Jesus said, *"Judge not, that you be not judged"* (Matthew 7:1).

That command needs to be read carefully, and the same audience member drew the line. Jesus is talking about judging a person, whether they stand right with God. He is not forbidding anyone to call a wrong act wrong. Saying "you robbed the bank, and that is wrong" is a different thing from deciding someone's eternal standing. The first is ordinary moral clarity. The second belongs to God alone.

### Justice Comes at the End

When is justice for evil carried out? At the end. Will we see justice on this earth in our lifetimes? Bits and pieces, but overall, no. If we expect justice on this earth, we will be disappointed, and that is not God's fault. If our whole platform, everything about us, is justice in this lifetime, we will eventually end up disillusioned.

That is the whole point of Reinhold Niebuhr's Serenity Prayer: we cannot control everything. Are we supposed to take vengeance? No. *"Vengeance is mine, I will repay, says the Lord"* (Romans 12:19).

And that goes back to the point of prophecy and the point of Revelation. In this study's reading, the Lord came for his church at the end of the seals, way back in chapter 6, and from there until it returns with him in chapter 19 the church is in heaven. So why does he spend so much time, in the prophets and in this book, telling his people what is going on on the earth? To help them [[Glossary#Patient Endurance|patiently endure]]. That is why John says it over and over, why the Lord says it over and over, and why Daniel emphasized it. The believer's part is to patiently endure, because he has it all under control.

We can influence where we can. We can make situations better and improve the lives of those around us. But the fight is not against institutions, and it is not against men or women. Paul was clear: *"We do not wrestle against flesh and blood"* (Ephesians 6:12). As soon as we start fighting against other people, we are playing into Satan's hands.

> **Key Principle:** The believer's job is not to change the world or to bring justice to this earth. It is to love, to serve, and to bring heaven to earth in our own small sphere as far as we can, and to leave justice in the hands of the one who has promised to bring it.

## 12. The First and the Last

*"Behold, I am coming soon, bringing my recompense with me, to repay each one for what he has done. I am the Alpha and the Omega, the first and the last, the beginning and the end."* (Revelation 22:12–13)

He is the first, from Genesis 1, and the last, in Revelation 22. He starts the story by creating the beginning, and he concludes it and finishes the end.

When did the author choose to begin his story? With the creation of the world. There is a whole spiritual war going on in the midst of it, as the [[Glossary#Parable of the Wheat and Weeds|parable of the wheat and the weeds]] shows, and it is important to understand the reality of that war. But the story starts with us. It is not about them; it is about us. The beginning and the end are both about us.

Ironically enough, there is no mention of Satan anywhere in Revelation 21 and 22. He is irrelevant. Everyone in the lake of fire is irrelevant at this point. They do not matter.

## 13. Outside the City

*"Blessed are those who wash their robes, so that they may have the right to the tree of life and that they may enter the city by the gates. Outside are the dogs and sorcerers and the sexually immoral and murderers and idolaters, and everyone who loves and practices falsehood."* (Revelation 22:14–15)

What does *outside* mean? Not in the city, outside the city walls. In this study's reading, Satan, the Antichrist, and every human who rebelled have not ceased to exist. They are still being tormented forever, and they have to be somewhere. They are outside the city, because the city is the Bride. Many people imagine that when you die you go to heaven and are separated from the earth, but Satan and the lake of fire have to be physically somewhere in the created universe, however we understand it. Wherever that is, it is outside the city.

The "dogs" are not animals. In Scripture the word is an insult for the impure and the shameless, those who live by the flesh (Deuteronomy 23:18; Philippians 3:2).

## 14. Come, Lord Jesus

Jesus speaks in his own name: he has sent his angel to testify these things for the churches, and he is *"the root and the descendant of David, the bright morning star"* (Revelation 22:16). The [[Glossary#Morning Star|morning star]] promised to the overcomer in Thyatira is himself.

The Spirit and the Bride say, *Come.* Let the one who hears say, *Come.* Let the one who is thirsty come, and let the one who desires take the water of life without price (Revelation 22:17). Then comes the warning: anyone who adds to the words of this prophecy will have the plagues of this book added to him, and anyone who takes away from them will have his share in the tree of life and the holy city taken away (Revelation 22:18–19).

*"He who testifies to these things says, 'Surely I am coming soon.' Amen. Come, Lord Jesus! The grace of the Lord Jesus be with all. Amen."* (Revelation 22:20–21)

And with that, the greatest story ever told ends.

## 15. Face to Face

There are questions about the new creation that Scripture does not answer, and there will still be questions when we get there. It will not matter as much then. What the new creation looks like matters less than the fact that believers will be in the presence of God and see him face to face. What we see now we see dimly, as in a mirror (1 Corinthians 13:12); then it will be face to face. The intimacy of that relationship will be greater and more precious than anything on this earth can show us.

Most believers have had some kind of encounter with the Lord, and anyone who has been in his presence, even for a split second, never forgets it. The feeling is unlike anything else in the world. When we are in his presence, everything else is secondary. It is all about the relationship.

Many people get caught up in everything in Revelation: all the bad things, all the judgments. But Christians have the privilege of a relationship with Almighty God right now, and it is easy to set that aside for everything else in this life. It is the most precious treasure we have on this earth. A time is coming when we will no longer care about everything that seems so important to us now, because when we are in his presence face to face, that will be everything, forever.

> **Key Principle:** The end of the story is not a place but a presence. The new heaven, the new earth, and the New Jerusalem exist for one purpose: that God may dwell with his people, and they may see his face.

### Not a Revelation Class

This study is often called the Revelation class, but it is not a class on Revelation. It is the Bible: the story of Jesus, his story, from the beginning to the end. Revelation happens to be the end, the climax. And the emphases in this study are not imposed on the text from outside. They are the points Jesus himself emphasized throughout Scripture, the places where he put the pieces of the story. Following a book means following its author's line of thought. He puts things in for a purpose and guides the reader through them, and the arcs of all its characters lead to the conclusion of the matter, the resolution of the conflict. Everything in the book applies to Revelation, because Revelation is how he fulfills all things.

## 16. Looking Back

With Revelation 22, the story is complete. The natural next step is to walk back through the whole of it from the beginning, tracing the thread from Genesis 3:15 through the covenants, the prophets, and the Gospels to the throne of God and the Lamb, and seeing how it all fits together. Some larger questions raised along the way, including whether a person who is saved is always saved, are left for further study.
