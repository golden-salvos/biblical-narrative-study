*A record of genuine questions raised during this session, with the answers given in the room. This is a companion to the main chapter note, not a replacement for it.*

### Q: The 144,000 are protected from the wrath — but could any of them die of something ordinary, like a heart attack, during that period?

No. The Lord is sustaining them. The seal is providential protection, not only a shield against the locusts and the cavalry of the fifth and sixth trumpets. The proof is in Revelation 14:1: when John sees them standing with the Lamb on Mount Zion, the number is still 144,000. Not one has died and not one is missing.

### Q: Was the celibacy of the 144,000 something the Law required of Jews?

No. Nothing in Scripture requires celibacy of Israel. Marriage was the norm and was regarded as part of obeying the command to be fruitful and multiply. The 144,000 chose it as an act of dedication to God — a level of devotion beyond what the Law demanded, kept while they waited for the Messiah. That devotion, together with the integrity described in verse 5 ("in their mouth no lie was found"), is what set them apart as firstfruits.

### Q: The third angel's message sounds final. If you willingly take the mark, is there no coming back?

That is correct. Anyone who worships the beast and receives the mark drinks the wine of God's wrath, and Revelation 14:11 says the smoke of their torment rises forever. The bowl judgments confirm it from the other side: those who bear the mark curse God under the plagues and do not repent (Revelation 16:9, 11, 21). The choice is final because it is made with full knowledge — the church, the angels, and the Two Witnesses are all proclaiming exactly what the mark means before anyone can take it.

### Q: If everyone worships the beast except those whose names are in the book of life, does that include people who aren't Christians yet?

Yes. The book of life is written from God's vantage point, "from the foundation of the world." It includes everyone who will come to faith at any point before the final judgment — not only those who are believers at the moment the Antichrist rises. The 144,000 are the example: they were not believers in Jesus when they were sealed. God knows who will choose him, and some people will refuse the mark before they have fully come to faith. That is also why Christians have no business writing off someone who is not a believer today.

### Q: Who are the Two Witnesses — Elijah and Moses, or Elijah and Enoch?

Elijah is certain, because his return is prophesied (Malachi 4:5). The other is most likely Enoch. Enoch and Elijah are the only two people in Scripture who left the earth without dying, which makes them the two who can still die in Jerusalem at the end of their ministry. Moses is the leading alternative, on the strength of the water-into-blood and plague miracles, but Scripture records that Moses died and was buried (Deuteronomy 34:5–6).

### Q: How high would the blood be if it reached a horse's bridle?

The bridle sits on the horse's head, where the bit goes into its mouth — on most horses that is roughly the height of a man's shoulder or higher, somewhere around four to five feet. The image is of blood flowing that deep for 1,600 stadia, roughly 180 miles. Ancient Jewish writers used the same picture to describe catastrophic slaughter (see the Context note for this session).

### Q: So we don't have to be on the earth to witness the bowls of wrath?

No. Revelation 15 places the church in heaven, standing beside the sea of glass with harps, while the angels come out of the sanctuary with the bowls. By then believers have their resurrection bodies, and a resurrection body is not bound by the physical limits we live with now. After his resurrection, Jesus vanished from the disciples at Emmaus and appeared inside a locked room. Believers will be in heaven and still able to see what is happening on the earth.

### Q: Is the "great city" that splits into three parts at the seventh bowl Babylon?

Yes. The great city in Revelation 16:19 is Babylon the Great, the center of the Antichrist's kingdom. Its fall at the seventh bowl is the fulfillment of what the second angel announced in Revelation 14:8. Chapters 17 and 18 describe Babylon at length, and they are the subject of the next session.

### Q: Revelation has called God the one "who is and who was and who is to come." Why does Revelation 16:5 drop "who is to come"?

Because the coming has happened. An audience member pointed this out in the words of the angel in charge of the waters at the third bowl: *"Just are you, O Holy One, who is and who was."* The same change appears earlier, at the seventh trumpet, when the elders give thanks to the Lord God Almighty *"who is and who was, for you have taken your great power and begun to reign"* (Revelation 11:17). Once the reign has begun on the earth, "who is to come" is no longer a future hope. It has arrived.
