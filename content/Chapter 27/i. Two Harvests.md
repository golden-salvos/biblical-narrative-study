## Recap

The previous session walked through Revelation 12 and 13. At the midpoint of the seven years, [[Glossary#Michael|Michael]] rises and wins a war in heaven; the [[Glossary#Dragon|dragon]] — Satan — loses his standing in the divine court and is thrown down to the earth. With heaven closed to him, his only remaining avenue is a human agent, and he takes full possession of the [[Glossary#Antichrist|Antichrist]]. That is why the [[Glossary#Abomination of Desolation|Abomination of Desolation]] happens when it does.

The session closed on the second beast — the [[Glossary#False Prophet|false prophet]], the Antichrist's religious voice — and on the [[Glossary#Mark of the Beast|mark of the beast]] and the number 666. The mark cannot be slipped into anyone unawares. It requires worshiping the beast and his image, and it is a choice.

That leaves the timeline parked at a very specific moment: the Abomination of Desolation, the threshold of the [[Glossary#Great Tribulation|Great Tribulation]]. Revelation 14 opens a new series of visions, and none of them carries a timestamp. Each one has to be placed by its clues.

## Reading Visions Without Timestamps

Revelation is not a single film running from start to finish. It is a series of visions, and John often moves from one to the next with nothing more than "then I looked" or "then I saw another." Chapter 14 is a string of separate visions — the Lamb and the 144,000, three angels, a grain harvest, a grape harvest — connected to one another by theme rather than by sequence.

The discipline is to ask of each vision: when does this take place? The text almost never answers directly. It answers through clues — who is present, where they are standing, what has already happened to them, and what has not happened yet. Read that way, the visions of chapter 14 do not all fall at the same point. Some belong at the Abomination of Desolation, some at the return of Christ, some at the very end. They have been placed side by side for a reason, and the reason is usually contrast.

> **Key Principle:** Revelation's visions are not always in chronological order. Each one has to be located on the timeline by its own clues before its meaning can be read correctly.

## The Lamb and the 144,000 on Mount Zion

*"Then I looked, and behold, on Mount Zion stood the Lamb, and with him 144,000 who had his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads. And I heard a voice from heaven like the roar of many waters and like the sound of loud thunder. The voice I heard was like the sound of harpists playing on their harps, and they were singing a new song before the throne and before the four living creatures and before the elders. No one could learn that song except the 144,000 who had been redeemed from the earth. It is these who have not defiled themselves with women, for they are virgins. It is these who follow the Lamb wherever he goes. These have been redeemed from mankind as firstfruits for God and the Lamb, and in their mouth no lie was found, for they are blameless."* (Revelation 14:1–5)

### Placing the Vision

The first clue is the [[Glossary#144,000|144,000]] themselves. They were sealed between the sixth and seventh seals, before the first trumpet sounded. So this vision cannot come earlier than that sealing.

The second clue is who is standing with them. The Lamb is on Mount Zion — in Jerusalem, on the earth. Christ does not stand on the earth until he returns. So this scene has to be after his return, which in this study's sequence falls between the sixth and seventh seals.

The third clue is what they have come through. The 144,000 were sealed for a specific purpose: protection from the [[Glossary#Wrath of God|wrath of God]] in the trumpet judgments. When the demonic army of the fifth trumpet and the cavalry of the sixth are released on the earth, they are forbidden to touch those who bear God's seal (Revelation 9:4). Here, John sees the full number — all 144,000. Not one has died. Not one is missing. They have been providentially sustained through everything the trumpets brought. The Lord has not permitted any of them to be harmed during this period, by judgment or by anything else.

Fourth, notice where John is. As far as the text has shown, the vantage point of the visions is still heaven. The voice and the new song come from before the throne. But the 144,000 are on the earth, and Jesus is on the earth with them.

Put together, the vision fits after the seven trumpets and before the seven bowls of wrath. The 144,000 have witnessed every trumpet. They are about to witness the bowls. And the passages that follow in chapters 15 and 16 move straight into those bowls, which confirms the placement.

### Firstfruits of the Kingdom

What do the 144,000 represent? The text names it: [[Glossary#First Fruits|firstfruits]] for God and the Lamb. Firstfruits are the first of a harvest — the first portion gathered, with much more to follow. The 144,000 are the first redeemed of the kingdom that is about to be established on the earth. They are the firstfruits of the [[Glossary#Millennial Kingdom|Millennial Kingdom]], the nucleus of Israel standing with the King in Jerusalem as he begins to reign.

They are singled out in two ways. They learn a song no one else in humanity can learn. And they follow the Lamb wherever he goes.

### What Set Them Apart

They are singled out because of choices they made before they ever believed. Remember that they were not believers in Jesus when they were sealed. They were Jews looking for the Messiah, not yet recognizing that he had already come. But they waited for him with a devotion that went beyond what was required.

The text names one of those choices: they did not defile themselves with women; they are virgins. That was not a requirement of the Law. Nothing in Scripture requires celibacy of Israel. They chose it, as an act of dedication to God. Alongside it, the text says no lie was found in their mouths and that they are blameless. These are men who kept themselves ritually and ceremonially pure, following the Mosaic and Levitical law as observant Jews, and preparing themselves for the coming of the Messiah. God saw that devotion and set them apart.

## Three Angels, Three Messages

Then the vision switches. John sees [[Glossary#Three Angels' Messages|three angels]] in succession, each with a message for the earth (Revelation 14:6–13). Again there is no timestamp, so the messages themselves have to supply one.

### The First Angel: The Eternal Gospel

*"Then I saw another angel flying directly overhead, with an eternal gospel to proclaim to those who dwell on earth, to every nation and tribe and language and people. And he said with a loud voice, 'Fear God and give him glory, because the hour of his judgment has come, and worship him who made heaven and earth, the sea and the springs of water.'"* (Revelation 14:6–7)

Listen to the specific language. Worship him who made heaven and earth, the sea, and the springs of water. That is not a generic description of creation. It is a list of exactly what the trumpet judgments strike. When the trumpets sound, the earth is struck — the grass and the trees. The sea is struck and its creatures die. The rivers and springs are turned bitter. The heavens are darkened.

So this message is a warning given in advance. Fear God, give him glory, worship him as the Creator — because the hour of his judgment has come. If you will not acknowledge him as the one who made all these things, he is going to judge the very things you have refused to credit him for. He is going to take them away from you.

That places the message before the first trumpet. And the eternal gospel is being proclaimed to every nation, tribe, language, and people at the moment it is most needed — after the Antichrist has revealed himself and before the wrath begins.

### The Second Angel: Fallen Is Babylon

*"Another angel, a second, followed, saying, 'Fallen, fallen is Babylon the great, she who made all nations drink the wine of the passion of her sexual immorality.'"* (Revelation 14:8)

This message is announced almost completely out of context. Revelation devotes two full chapters to [[Glossary#Babylon the Great|Babylon the Great]] later on, and its identity has not yet been developed. *(The identity, location, and characteristics of Babylon the Great are the subject of the next session.)* What the passage says is short and clear, and its purpose can be read from what the Antichrist offers the world.

What is the incentive to take the mark? It is not the torment; that is the consequence. The incentive is participation in the economy — the ability to buy and sell, to share in the wealth. And that offer is only a temptation if there is something worth having. If the Antichrist ruled from an empty desert with no trade, no city, and no luxury, no one would take the deal. What makes his kingdom appealing to people who want riches, comfort, and luxury is what they can see and taste. His city is going to be glorious — a city of glamour, luxury, and trade. Babylon is that great city: the center of the Antichrist's kingdom, the great prostitute that has drawn the kings of the earth into her riches and her idolatry.

The second angel's message is that the city is finished. Everything you are putting your trust in — everything you see, everything you think you are getting from this economy — is going to burn. It is the same message Jesus gave when the disciples pointed out the grandeur of the temple: *"Truly, I say to you, there will not be left here one stone upon another that will not be thrown down"* (Matthew 24:2). When is that message most needed? At the same moment as the first — right after the Abomination of Desolation, before people take the mark.

### The Third Angel: The Wine of God's Wrath

*"And another angel, a third, followed them, saying with a loud voice, 'If anyone worships the beast and its image and receives a mark on his forehead or on his hand, he also will drink the wine of God's wrath, poured full strength into the cup of his anger, and he will be tormented with fire and sulfur in the presence of the holy angels and in the presence of the Lamb. And the smoke of their torment goes up forever and ever, and they have no rest, day or night, these worshipers of the beast and its image, and whoever receives the mark of its name.'"* (Revelation 14:9–11)

This is as clear as Scripture gets. Anyone who willingly takes the mark is done. There is no coming back from it.

Revelation 13:8 already said that everyone on the earth will worship the beast — except those whose names are written in the [[Glossary#Book of Life|book of life]] from the foundation of the world. That is the verbiage, and the verbiage matters. Those names include people who are not yet Christians when the Antichrist rises. The 144,000 were not believers when they were sealed. God knows who will choose him, and somewhere in the back of their minds, some people are going to hear this message and refuse the mark before they have fully come to faith.

That should correct an arrogant habit among Christians: the assumption that someone who is not a believer today never will be. Only the Lord knows how much time a person has, and he knows when they are going to make that choice.

The third message clearly reaches beyond the window of the first two — its consequences run forever. But its decision point does not. Its decision is *don't take the mark*. When is the first moment anyone can take the mark? Right after the Abomination of Desolation, when the opportunity to worship the beast first opens. God would not allow a single person to take that mark without this message being spoken first.

So all three messages belong at the same point: after the Abomination of Desolation, before the Great Tribulation runs its course and before the first trumpet.

### Three Witnesses

God does not leave this choice to chance, and he does not rely on a single messenger. During the same window — from the Abomination of Desolation to the death of the Antichrist — three witnesses are testifying the same thing to the entire world.

The first is the church, which is still on the earth. The second is the angels, carrying the eternal gospel and its warnings to every nation and language. The third is the [[Glossary#Two Witnesses|Two Witnesses]], two supernatural prophets ministering in Jerusalem through the same period. One of them is certainly Elijah, because that is prophesied; the other is most likely Enoch.

This is not a choice God takes lightly, and it is not one that can be entered casually or imposed secretly. It follows the same rule that governs the whole universe: every person is going to know the truth and make a choice based on it. Whether they believe it or not, they will know.

> **Key Principle:** No one will take the mark of the beast without being warned. God makes the consequences of that choice unmistakably clear through the church, the angels, and the Two Witnesses before the first person can make it.

### The Endurance of the Saints

*"Here is a call for the endurance of the saints, those who keep the commandments of God and their faith in Jesus. And I heard a voice from heaven saying, 'Write this: Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord from now on.' 'Blessed indeed,' says the Spirit, 'that they may rest from their labors, for their deeds follow them!'"* (Revelation 14:12–13)

Once again the saints are called to [[Glossary#Patient Endurance|patient endurance]]. Prayer and the help of God are how a believer gets through it. But it is possible to endure while grumbling the whole way. What does it take to endure *patiently*? Knowing how the story ends.

That is why the Lord tells his people all of this. Believers know exactly how it ends. They know their inheritance. They know what is on the other side. They know that he is their great reward, and that he is going through it with them, comforting them along the way.

And so the blessing on those who die in the Lord from now on. If Christians are to endure patiently, they cannot be afraid of death. Death is a transition — the doorway to the inheritance. In the middle of everything this period brings, those who die in the Lord get to rest.

The job of a believer in the Great Tribulation is not to survive. It is not to hide, to prep, or to avoid everything and make it to the end. The job is to witness, to love even in the face of great wickedness, and to endure patiently as a testimony of faith in the Lord.

The Roman persecutions are the precedent. Christians died in the arenas of the empire with all of Rome watching. The empire could not deny the way they died. It could not deny the faith they held, or the power and joy they had in the middle of it. It was too real. That witness is what eventually brought the Roman Empire to its knees.

## The Harvest of the Earth

Then the vision switches again.

*"Then I looked, and behold, a white cloud, and seated on the cloud one like a son of man, with a golden crown on his head, and a sharp sickle in his hand. And another angel came out of the temple, calling with a loud voice to him who sat on the cloud, 'Put in your sickle, and reap, for the hour to reap has come, for the harvest of the earth is fully ripe.' So he who sat on the cloud swung his sickle across the earth, and the earth was reaped."* (Revelation 14:14–16)

A white cloud, and one like a [[Glossary#Son of Man|son of man]] seated on it. That is Daniel 7:13 almost word for word. This is the second coming of Christ.

Right after the blessing on those who die in the Lord, the vision shows what they have to look forward to. The harvest of the earth is coming. This is the great hope of those dying in the Great Tribulation.

It is not the same point on the timeline as the three messages. Those were spoken at the beginning of the Great Tribulation. The harvest comes later — before the end of the seven years, and, as established last session, no later than roughly six months before the end, leaving room for the trumpets and bowls that follow it.

The language reaches all the way back to the first parable this study read, in its opening weeks: the [[Glossary#Parable of the Wheat and Weeds|parable of the wheat and the weeds]] (Matthew 13:24–30, 36–43). The servants want to pull up the weeds, and the master tells them not to — the harvest is not ready. He is waiting for the whole field to ripen. Here an angel announces that the harvest of the earth is fully ripe, and the Son of Man reaps it. This is the gathering of the church — the [[Glossary#Rapture|rapture]], the Lord's return for his people.

## The Grape Harvest

Then another vision.

*"Then another angel came out of the temple in heaven, and he too had a sharp sickle. And another angel came out from the altar, the angel who has authority over the fire, and he called with a loud voice to the one who had the sharp sickle, 'Put in your sickle and gather the clusters from the vine of the earth, for its grapes are ripe.' So the angel swung his sickle across the earth and gathered the grape harvest of the earth and threw it into the great winepress of the wrath of God. And the winepress was trodden outside the city, and blood flowed from the winepress, as high as a horse's bridle, for 1,600 stadia."* (Revelation 14:17–20)

This vision is tied to the previous one by the sickle, but the symbolism is completely different. The reaper is an angel, not the Lord. And this is a different harvest: not grain but grapes, thrown into the [[Glossary#Winepress of the Wrath of God|winepress of the wrath of God]].

When is the grape harvest? Certainly after the Lord returns, but it can be marked more precisely. It is the moment God's wrath is fully poured out: the seventh bowl, the Battle of [[Glossary#Armageddon|Armageddon]]. The Antichrist, the false prophet, and the entire army gathered with them are completely wiped out, and the blood flows for 1,600 stadia — roughly 180 miles.

The aftermath is described elsewhere. Ezekiel records God summoning every bird of prey to a great feast on the bodies of those who fell in the battle (Ezekiel 39:17–20), and Revelation 19 repeats the summons. When the Lord comes riding on his horse — a scene the study will read when it reaches Revelation 19 — the horse is in blood up to its bridle. Where has he come from? From the winepress.

### Choose Wisely

These two visions are joined because they are both harvests with sickles, and they are placed side by side to contrast. One is the fate of believers — those who have followed the Lord. The other is the fate of those who take the mark of the beast. They represent two different points on the timeline, but they are connected because they are the two fates of mankind, determined by one choice. It is the grail scene from *Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade*: choose wisely.

> **Key Principle:** The grain harvest and the grape harvest are not the same event, and they do not happen at the same time. They are set side by side to show the two outcomes of the choice every person faces — gathered by the Son of Man, or trodden in the winepress of God's wrath.

## The Last Plagues and the Song of Moses

Revelation 15 and 16 belong together. Chapter 15 introduces seven angels and what they are about to do; chapter 16 pours out the [[Glossary#Seven Bowls of Wrath|seven bowls of wrath]].

*"Then I saw another sign in heaven, great and amazing, seven angels with seven plagues, which are the last, for with them the wrath of God is finished. And I saw what appeared to be a sea of glass mingled with fire — and also those who had conquered the beast and its image and the number of its name, standing beside the sea of glass with harps of God in their hands. And they sing the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb, saying, 'Great and amazing are your deeds, O Lord God the Almighty! Just and true are your ways, O King of the nations! Who will not fear, O Lord, and glorify your name? For you alone are holy. All nations will come and worship you, for your righteous acts have been revealed.'"* (Revelation 15:1–4)

John keeps receiving one sign after another, each a separate vision. This one takes place in heaven, at the sea of glass before the throne.

Who is the multitude that has conquered the beast, its image, and the number of its name? It is the church — believers, standing in heaven with harps in their hands, singing the [[Glossary#Song of Moses|song of Moses]] and the song of the Lamb.

*"After this I looked, and the sanctuary of the tent of witness in heaven was opened, and out of the sanctuary came the seven angels with the seven plagues, clothed in pure, bright linen, with golden sashes around their chests. And one of the four living creatures gave to the seven angels seven golden bowls full of the wrath of God who lives forever and ever, and the sanctuary was filled with smoke from the glory of God and from his power, and no one could enter the sanctuary until the seven plagues of the seven angels were finished."* (Revelation 15:5–8)

So two things are happening in heaven at once: the seven angels coming out of the sanctuary with the bowls, and the church standing beside the sea of glass, worshiping God. The bowls are about to be poured out on the earth, and the church is watching it from heaven.

The church does not need to be on the earth to witness what happens there. By this point believers have their [[Glossary#Resurrection Body|resurrection bodies]], and a resurrected body is not governed by the physical limitations we live under now. When Jesus had his resurrected body, he was talking with the disciples on the road to Emmaus one moment and gone the next (Luke 24:31). The disciples were in a locked room, and suddenly the Lord was standing among them (John 20:19, 26). Believers will be in heaven, and still able to see what is happening on the earth.

## The Seven Bowls

*"Then I heard a loud voice from the temple telling the seven angels, 'Go and pour out on the earth the seven bowls of the wrath of God.'"* (Revelation 16:1)

The **first bowl** is poured on the earth, and harmful and painful sores come upon the people who bear the mark of the beast and worship its image.

The **second bowl** is poured into the sea, and it becomes like the blood of a corpse. Every living thing in the sea dies.

The **third bowl** is poured into the rivers and springs, and they become blood. Then the angel in charge of the waters speaks: *"Just are you, O Holy One, who is and who was, for you brought these judgments. For they have shed the blood of saints and prophets, and you have given them blood to drink. It is what they deserve!"* And the altar answers: *"Yes, Lord God the Almighty, true and just are your judgments!"* (Revelation 16:5–7)

An audience member noted a detail in that verse. Up to this point in Revelation, the one on the throne has been praised as the one *"who is and who was and who is to come"* (Revelation 1:4, 8; 4:8). Here the angel says only *"who is and who was."* The third phrase is gone, because he has come. The coming is no longer future.

The **fourth bowl** is poured on the sun, and it scorches people with fire. They curse the name of God who has power over these plagues, and they do not repent and give him glory.

The **fifth bowl** is poured on the throne of the beast, and his kingdom is plunged into darkness. People gnaw their tongues in anguish and curse the God of heaven for their pain and sores. They do not repent of their deeds.

### The Sixth Bowl: The Euphrates and the Kings of the East

*"The sixth angel poured out his bowl on the great river Euphrates, and its water was dried up, to prepare the way for the kings from the east."* (Revelation 16:12)

Videos regularly appear online announcing that the Euphrates is drying up and that the Battle of Armageddon is therefore near. It is not time. The river's flow may well be dropping — upstream damming has a great deal to do with that — but that is not this. This bowl comes at the very end of the seven years, after the trumpets and in the middle of the bowls. None of the events that must come before it have happened.

The Euphrates rises in the mountains of Turkey and runs down through Syria and Iraq. Throughout biblical history it marked a frontier: the eastern limit of the land promised to Abraham (Genesis 15:18), and the line between the lands to its west and the empires of the east. The [[Glossary#Kings of the East|kings of the east]] are the rulers of the lands beyond it — Iraq, Afghanistan, Pakistan, and on toward China. Drying the river removes the barrier for their armies.

*"And I saw, coming out of the mouth of the dragon and out of the mouth of the beast and out of the mouth of the false prophet, three unclean spirits like frogs. For they are demonic spirits, performing signs, who go abroad to the kings of the whole world, to assemble them for battle on the great day of God the Almighty. ('Behold, I am coming like a thief! Blessed is the one who stays awake, keeping his garments on, that he may not go about naked and be seen exposed!') And they assembled them at the place that in Hebrew is called Armageddon."* (Revelation 16:13–16)

The dragon, the beast, and the false prophet — the [[Glossary#Unholy Trinity|unholy trinity]] — are each inhabited by evil spirits, and they send those spirits out as messengers to gather the kings of the whole world.

### Where Is Armageddon?

There is endless speculation about Armageddon — the Plain of Megiddo, the Jezreel Valley, and so on. Does any of it matter? Not much. Where is the last battle actually fought?

When the study read Ezekiel, God said to Gog, *"I will turn you about and put hooks into your jaws"* (Ezekiel 38:4), and he brought him against the mountains of Israel. The final battle is fought just outside Jerusalem. Exactly where outside the city is not something Scripture requires us to pin down. But it will be near Jerusalem, because Jerusalem is the center of the world in God's plan, and that is where the battle is.

### The Seventh Bowl: It Is Done

*"The seventh angel poured out his bowl into the air, and a loud voice came out of the temple, from the throne, saying, 'It is done!' And there were flashes of lightning, rumblings, peals of thunder, and a great earthquake such as there had never been since man was on the earth, so great was that earthquake. The great city was split into three parts, and the cities of the nations fell, and God remembered Babylon the great, to make her drain the cup of the wine of the fury of his wrath. And every island fled away, and no mountains were to be found. And great hailstones, about one hundred pounds each, fell from heaven on people; and they cursed God for the plague of the hail, because the plague was so severe."* (Revelation 16:17–21)

The great city split into three parts is Babylon the Great. This is the fall of Babylon that the second angel announced, and chapters 17 and 18 will say much more about it.

### No Repentance

Compare this earthquake with the one at the end of the Two Witnesses' ministry. That earthquake struck Jerusalem, and the survivors were terrified and gave glory to the God of heaven (Revelation 11:13). Those were Jews who had seen the Messiah they had rejected come back, had seen his power and his wrath, and were repenting of their error.

This is different. These are the ones who worshiped the beast and took the mark, and no matter what they see or experience, they do not repent. They curse God instead.

It is the same thing that happened with Pharaoh. Again and again he wavered and said, *go, worship the Lord* — and then changed his mind. His time was done. He had already made his decision, and the Lord [[Glossary#Hardening|hardened]] him in it; he was judged as he was, right then and there. It is the same for those who bear the mark. It is too late for them. They have already made their choice.

## The Sequence So Far

With the seven bowls, the study has now walked sequentially through twenty-one events in Revelation.

The seven seals came first — the seals on the scroll that only the Lamb could open. Sometime between the sixth and seventh seals the Lord returns. No one knows the day or the hour, but he comes in that window, because he comes back before his wrath is poured out in the trumpets.

The seventh seal opens the seven trumpets. The trumpets are wrath upon the earth, concentrated on a portion of it — most likely the Middle East, the heart of the Antichrist's kingdom — which is why the Lord seals his 144,000 before they begin, and why he tells his people to flee east, away from Jerusalem.

At the seventh trumpet, there is no more delay, and the Lord begins to reign. That is most likely when he rejoins the 144,000 on the earth, because he reigns from Jerusalem — and in this vision he stands with them on Mount Zion.

Following the seventh trumpet, as he begins his reign, he pours out the seven bowls. At the end he judges the Antichrist and the false prophet, throws them into the lake of fire, and kills everyone who has come against Jerusalem fighting alongside the Antichrist. Everyone who fights with the Antichrist dies. Anyone who comes against Jerusalem dies.

After the Antichrist and false prophet are gone, Satan is chained in the abyss for a thousand years while Christ reigns from Jerusalem. There will be a temple and sacrifices, and the Lord will be enthroned there. Gentile nations will exist outside Israel and will be required to come up to Jerusalem once a year. At the end of the thousand years Satan is released, raises another army, and comes against Jerusalem again — and this time there is no battle. Fire comes down from heaven and consumes them. Then the earth as it exists now ceases to exist, there is a new heaven and a new earth, and the New Jerusalem comes down out of heaven: the city Jesus said he was going to prepare for his people (John 14:2–3). *(The millennial kingdom, the role of the nations, the new heaven and new earth, the New Jerusalem, and when and why the Father joins his people there will each be taken up as the study reaches Revelation 19–22.)*

Much of what people imagine about heaven — souls as winged cherubs drifting among the clouds — is not what Scripture describes. The eternal home of God's people is a real city in a renewed creation: the New Jerusalem.
