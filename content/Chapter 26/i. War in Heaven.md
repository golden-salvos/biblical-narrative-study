## Recap

The previous session closed on the seventh trumpet. Loud voices in heaven announced that the kingdom of the world had become the kingdom of the Lord and of his Christ, and the reign that had been exercised from heaven turned toward the earth itself. The session also reviewed the timestamps Revelation uses to mark the second half of the seven-year period — forty-two months, 1,260 days, and "time, times, and half a time" — all naming the same three-and-a-half-year window that runs from the [[Glossary#Abomination of Desolation|Abomination of Desolation]] to the end of the [[Glossary#Antichrist|Antichrist]]'s reign.

One question was deliberately left open: what happens in heaven at the midpoint, and why does it happen then? Revelation 12 answers it. The chapter does not advance the trumpet sequence. It loops back — all the way to the birth of the Messiah — and then forward again to the single event that turns the Antichrist from a man of peace into a man demanding worship. That event is a war in heaven, and before reading it in Revelation, the timestamp has to be set from Daniel.

## Finding the Timestamp: Michael in Daniel

[[Glossary#Michael|Michael]] is named in Scripture only five times: twice in Daniel 10, once in Daniel 12, once in Jude, and once in Revelation 12. The first Daniel reference is the one this study has already walked through — [[Glossary#Gabriel|Gabriel]] telling Daniel that the prince of Persia withstood him for twenty-one days until Michael came to help (Daniel 10:13). The reference that matters for Revelation 12 is the one in Daniel 12. When Revelation describes Michael rising up to fight, the way to read it is to go back to what Daniel already said about when Michael rises. Daniel supplies the when. Revelation supplies the what.

The chapter break between Daniel 11 and 12 was not in the original text. Chapter divisions were added to the Bible more than a thousand years after these books were written, and here the break obscures the connection. Daniel 12:1 begins with "at that time," and the time in question is described in the verses immediately before it.

From Daniel 11:36 to the end of the chapter, the subject is the Antichrist revealing himself on the earth, before the Abomination of Desolation. He goes to war. The [[Glossary#King of the North / King of the South|king of the South]] attacks him; he answers like a whirlwind with chariots, horsemen, and ships. He enters "the glorious land," and tens of thousands fall — but Edom, Moab, and the main part of the Ammonites escape his hand. That is the land directly east of Jerusalem across the Jordan, the same territory Israel passed through on the way to Jericho. It is the one region Daniel marks as outside his control. He takes Egypt and its treasures, with the Libyans and the Cushites following in his train. Then news from the east and the north alarms him — the lands of Persia and beyond — and he goes out in great fury to destroy. These are the consolidating wars of the first half of the seven years.

Verse 45 is the one to key on: *"And he shall pitch his palatial tents between the sea and the glorious holy mountain. Yet he shall come to his end, with none to help him."* The sea is the Mediterranean. The glorious holy mountain is Mount Zion. He has parked his army outside Jerusalem.

This is not Armageddon. That comes much later. This is the moment before he first enters the city — the moment before he goes into the temple. Luke's parallel to Matthew 24 describes exactly this scene: *"When you see Jerusalem surrounded by armies, then know that its desolation has come near. Then let those who are in Judea flee to the mountains"* (Luke 21:20–21). The mountains in view lie to the east, in the same Edom-Moab-Ammon region Daniel said the Antichrist would not control. Up to this point he has presented himself as a man of peace — the one who brought peace to the Middle East. He has not yet shown his hand. The army camped outside Jerusalem is the precursor to the assault on the temple.

Then Daniel 12:1: *"At that time shall arise Michael, the great prince who has charge of your people. And there shall be a time of trouble, such as never has been since there was a nation till that time."*

Michael's rising is bracketed by two events. Immediately before it, the Antichrist is camped outside Jerusalem and has not yet desecrated the temple. Immediately after it, a time of trouble begins unlike any in history — the [[Glossary#Great Tribulation|Great Tribulation]], which begins the moment the Antichrist stands in the temple and declares himself God. Michael rises right at the three-and-a-half-year mark, in the gap between those two.

Daniel does not say what Michael does. "The great prince who has charge of your people" is a description of who Michael is — Israel's appointed guardian — not an account of his action. The only action recorded is that he arises. The text gives a precise timestamp and withholds the content, which is Daniel's pattern throughout: clues given, details sealed, and the details unpacked later in Revelation.

> **Key Principle:** Daniel 12:1 tells us *when* Michael rises — between the Antichrist's army surrounding Jerusalem and the beginning of the Great Tribulation. Revelation 12 tells us *what* he rises to do.

## The Woman Clothed With the Sun

*"And a great sign appeared in heaven: a woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars. She was pregnant and was crying out in birth pains and the agony of giving birth."* (Revelation 12:1–2)

Revelation 12 is built out of signs, and each one has to be unpacked on its own. The first question is who the [[Glossary#Woman Clothed with the Sun|woman]] is. The two common answers are Mary and Israel. The way to decide between them is not preference but fit: which reading holds up through the rest of the chapter?

The imagery traces directly to Joseph's second dream in Genesis 37. Joseph had already angered his brothers once — his father loved him best and had given him a robe of many colors, and his first dream had their sheaves of grain bowing down to his. Then he dreamed again: *"Behold, the sun, the moon, and eleven stars were bowing down to me."* His father rebuked him: *"Shall I and your mother and your brothers indeed come to bow ourselves to the ground before you?"* (Genesis 37:9–10). Jacob interpreted the dream on the spot. The sun was Jacob himself, the moon was Joseph's mother, and the eleven stars were Joseph's brothers. Add Joseph, and the stars number twelve.

What does that group represent? Jacob's name had been changed to Israel. The sun, moon, and twelve stars are the family of Israel — the nation in seed form. Revelation's imagery is not an exact copy of the dream. The dream had eleven stars bowing; the woman wears a crown of twelve. The picture has been completed: all twelve tribes, crowned together. The woman is Israel.

Mary fits part of the picture, because Mary belongs to Israel and she is the one through whom the seed was actually born. But the vision is not about one woman. It is about the nation that carried the promise of the Messiah from Abraham forward, and the Mary reading breaks down as the chapter goes on — this woman flees into the wilderness to be protected for 1,260 days at the end of the age, and the dragon goes on to make war against the rest of her offspring. That is Israel's story, not Mary's alone.

The language also differs from Joseph's dream in one other respect: this is a sign *in heaven*. Some interpreters connect it to an actual arrangement of the sky — the constellation Virgo, the virgin, with the sun and moon positioned around her and a crown of stars and planets above her head — and some tie the Star of Bethlehem to related astronomical events. Retrograde motion is part of that discussion: the way a planet can appear to stop and move backward against the stars, the same way a car you pass on the freeway seems to drift backward even though it is moving forward. Those proposals are intriguing, but the interpretation does not rest on them. *(Tracing the birth of Jesus through the stars was set aside for a later time.)*

## The Great Red Dragon

*"And another sign appeared in heaven: behold, a great red dragon, with seven heads and ten horns, and on his heads seven diadems. His tail swept down a third of the stars of heaven and cast them to the earth."* (Revelation 12:3–4)

Revelation's symbols are always borrowed from somewhere, and this one is borrowed from Daniel. The fourth beast of Daniel 7 — the one with iron teeth, unlike all the others — had ten horns, and Daniel was told those horns are ten kings who arise in the last kingdom before the little horn rises among them, subdues three, and takes over (Daniel 7:7–8, 24). The ten horns on the [[Glossary#Dragon|dragon]] are a direct correlation back to Daniel's ten kings.

The seven heads have no clear precedent before this point in Scripture. They will be unpacked again later in Revelation, when the study reaches Babylon and the kingdom of the Antichrist. For now, the key to them is a question: what has the dragon been trying to do from the beginning?

The dragon's single aim since Eden has been to kill the Messiah — and, failing that, to destroy the people through whom the Messiah would come, so that the seed promised in Genesis 3:15 would never be born. The third of the stars swept down by his tail are angels: the host that fell with him and was cast to the earth. *(The timing of that casting-down comes into focus later in the chapter.)*

## Seven Heads: The Empires That Came for the Seed

The [[Glossary#Seven Heads and Ten Horns|seven heads]] are kingdoms — and kings, in the sense that kingdoms are embodied in the men who rule them. Their common denominator is the dragon's own goal. Walk through biblical history and ask which empires, one after another, set out to destroy Israel:

1. **Egypt** — the first Gentile nation in Scripture that tried to destroy Israel, before Israel was even a nation, when Pharaoh ordered the Hebrew boys killed at birth.
2. **Assyria** — which conquered the northern kingdom and carried the ten tribes away.
3. **Babylon** — which destroyed Jerusalem and took Judah into exile.
4. **Medo-Persia** — the empire under which Haman plotted the annihilation of the Jews.
5. **Greece** — through the Seleucid king Antiochus IV Epiphanes, who tried to erase Jewish worship altogether.
6. **Rome** — which destroyed Jerusalem and the temple in 70 AD.
7. **The Antichrist's kingdom** — which the study identifies as a revived [[Glossary#Islamic Caliphate|Islamic caliphate]].

Every one of the first six destroyed Jerusalem, exiled its people, or tried to. The first three of Daniel's empires are already familiar from the [[Glossary#Four Kingdoms of Daniel|four kingdoms]]; the heads extend the list backward to Egypt and Assyria and forward to the final kingdom.

A caliphate is a political and religious union of peoples under a single ruler, the caliph, who holds both political and religious authority. It is a conglomeration without internal borders — many nations that have agreed to come under one man. In Islamic eschatology the end-times ruler anticipated in this role is the [[Glossary#Mahdi|Mahdi]]. *(More clues to the seventh head appear as Revelation continues, and they will be developed there.)*

The pattern underneath the list is the point. Satan uses Gentile empires to try to destroy Israel and prevent the seed from being born. The heads on the dragon are the history of that effort.

## The Child Caught Up

*"And the dragon stood before the woman who was about to give birth, so that when she bore her child he might devour it. She gave birth to a male child, one who is to rule all the nations with a rod of iron, but her child was caught up to God and to his throne, and the woman fled into the wilderness, where she has a place prepared by God, in which she is to be nourished for 1,260 days."* (Revelation 12:4–6)

"Rule all the nations with a rod of iron" is the language of Psalm 2:9. The male child is Jesus. In a single verse the vision compresses his entire first coming — born, crucified, resurrected, ascended — into one movement: born, then caught up to God's throne. He is in heaven now, waiting to return.

Then the vision jumps forward. The woman flees into the wilderness to be nourished for 1,260 days. The Lord tells Israel exactly when to flee: when they see Jerusalem surrounded by armies and the Antichrist standing in the temple. The 1,260 days are three and a half years — the second half of the seven, the Great Tribulation — and the event that starts the clock is the Abomination of Desolation.

So by verse 6, the vision is standing right at the threshold of the Abomination. That is exactly where Daniel 12:1 placed Michael's rising. The next verse is where he rises.

## War in Heaven

*"Now war arose in heaven, Michael and his angels fighting against the dragon. And the dragon and his angels fought back, but he was defeated, and there was no longer any place for them in heaven."* (Revelation 12:7–8)

Daniel said Michael would arise at the midpoint and did not say why. Here is why. He is not merely preparing. He is fighting — Michael and his angels against the dragon and his angels — and the dragon loses.

In case there were any confusion about who the dragon is, verse 9 ends it: *"the great dragon was thrown down, that ancient serpent, who is called the devil and Satan, the deceiver of the whole world."* "Ancient serpent" reaches back to Eden. This is one of the clearest identifications in all of Scripture. The dragon is Satan. The Antichrist is a human being — he did not exist through the ages of Egypt, Assyria, Babylon, Medo-Persia, and Greece. Satan did. The dragon spans all seven heads because the dragon is not bound to a single lifetime.

This raises an obvious question. If Satan is thrown down to the earth at a specific moment in the future, where is he now?

He has access to heaven. He does not live there, but he has standing to appear there. He is part of the heavenly court as Job depicts it, where the sons of God present themselves before the Lord and Satan comes among them to accuse (Job 1:6; 2:1). He has held that access since Eden, because he holds legitimate authority on the earth — authority Adam handed to him. That is the standing that makes him the [[Glossary#Prince of This World|prince of this world]], and it is why he can enter the [[Glossary#Divine Council|divine council]] as the [[Glossary#Accuser of the Brethren|accuser]] of God's people. Revelation 12 records the moment that access ends.

## A Rebellion With No Way Back

Satan knows exactly who the Son of God is. He watched Christ overcome death and ascend to the throne. Knowing all of that, he keeps going — because there is no recovery from what he has done. His rebellion is final, and with nothing left to gain, his only remaining goal is to take as many with him as he can.

The same finality holds for the angels who rebelled with him, and for the [[Glossary#Watchers|Watchers]]. They made their choice, and there is no forgiveness offered to them. The reason is the point Enoch makes, and the reason this study walked through 1 Enoch in depth: the angels saw God in the fullness of his glory. They were exposed to all of it, and they still chose to rebel. Humanity is given grace in part because we have never seen what they saw. We see, as Paul says, through a glass darkly. Rebellion committed in full sight of God's glory leaves nothing further to be shown.

## "I Saw Satan Fall": Luke 10:18

When the seventy-two returned from their mission rejoicing that even the demons submitted to them, Jesus said, *"I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven"* (Luke 10:18). Does that mean Satan was cast out of heaven during Jesus's ministry?

When a prophet says "I saw," the question is what kind of seeing it is and what timeline the vision refers to. Daniel said, "I saw the little horn come up." It had not happened yet; he was seeing what was coming. Jesus's statement carries no timestamp, and the study reads it the same way: Jesus is seeing what will certainly happen, not reporting that it has happened. The same holds for his declaration that he holds the keys of death and Hades (Revelation 1:18). He possesses them now, and yet death and Hades still operate. He is waiting for the proper time for his kingdom to be revealed on the earth.

The verses that follow in Luke are in the present tense for a reason. Because of who Jesus is and what he was about to accomplish, the disciples had real authority over spirits — they cast out demons and healed the sick, and that happened immediately. But casting a demon out of a person in a village is a very different thing from Satan being expelled from heaven. The disciples did not cast down the principality that governed Rome or free Jerusalem from Roman rule. There is still authority in place in the heavenly places that was not dislodged at that time. Local authority over spirits was granted then. The expulsion of the accuser is still future.

## The Accuser Thrown Down

The expulsion is total. Satan's angels went with him — the third of the stars swept down by the dragon's tail. It was a package deal: when he went, every member of the council aligned with him went too.

Then a loud voice in heaven:

*"Now the salvation and the power and the kingdom of our God and the authority of his Christ have come, for the accuser of our brothers has been thrown down, who accuses them day and night before our God. And they have conquered him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony, for they loved not their lives even unto death. Therefore, rejoice, O heavens, and you who dwell in them! But woe to you, O earth and sea, for the devil has come down to you in great wrath, because he knows that his time is short!"* (Revelation 12:10–12)

After this moment, the fight in heaven is over. There is no longer an accuser with legal recourse to bring charges before the Lord. Michael and his angels did the fighting, but the victory is credited to two things: the blood of the Lamb, and the testimony of the saints on earth — people who did not love their lives even unto death. The faithfulness of the martyrs has a part in Satan's expulsion.

The heavens rejoice because the accuser and his angels are gone. The earth and the sea receive a woe, because now it is their problem. Satan no longer has any option to influence things in heaven. His only remaining avenue is the earth, through human beings — and he arrives in great wrath because he knows his time is short.

He knows the timeline. He knows prophecy, he knows Christ, and he knows the [[Glossary#Appointed Times|appointed time]]. Demons know it too. The demons at Gadara confronted Jesus with exactly that: *"Have you come here to torment us before the time?"* (Matthew 8:29). Anyone who has dealt with the demonic will recognize the pattern — sooner or later, the appointed time comes up.

## Why the Abomination Happens When It Does

With heaven closed to him, who is Satan's best path to accomplish his will on the earth? The Antichrist — the man who has already dedicated his soul to Satan.

Until this point, the Antichrist has been riding out on the white horse. He conquers, but he does it as a bringer of security: *these people are rebelling, and I will crush the rebellion so there can be peace.* One man's freedom fighter is another man's rebel, and he presents himself as the man of power who brings peace — masquerading as the Mahdi, the leader who brings unity to the world.

When Satan is thrown down, that changes. Satan enters and possesses the Antichrist. From that point it is no longer only the Antichrist acting; it is Satan acting through him. And the first thing he does is go into the temple and declare himself God, demanding worship.

He had three and a half years to do that before. Why does he do it at exactly this moment? Because Satan has just been kicked out of heaven, and this is the only play he has left. The Abomination of Desolation happens *because* of the war in heaven.

> **Key Principle:** The Abomination of Desolation is the direct consequence of Satan's expulsion from heaven. Stripped of his access to the divine court, he has one avenue left — the earth, through the human agent he has already prepared — and he uses it immediately.

## The Rules of the Board

If Satan wants to be worshiped, why does he need the Antichrist at all? Why not go to the temple and manifest himself?

Spiritual beings can make themselves visible; Scripture records that plainly. The issue is not visibility. It is the rules. The Watchers were thrown directly into the abyss because they broke those rules. They manifested physically on the earth and took whomever they wanted, giving mankind no choice. They usurped human will by exerting angelic power over the human world — something far beyond one human dominating another, because an angelic being is inherently stronger than any man. And they knew beforehand they would be punished for it; that is why they bound themselves by an oath on Mount Hermon, their leader saying he feared the others would back out and leave him alone to pay the penalty.

Satan was not thrown into the abyss because he did not do that. In the garden he gave Eve and Adam a choice. He manipulated and deceived, but he did not force. The spiritual powers are permitted to watch and to influence humans. They are not permitted to take over the board. Any being that starts overriding human will on the earth ends up where the Watchers did — taken off the board.

So Satan still has to preserve human choice, even at the end. That is why he needs a human player. The Antichrist is already in position, already holds the power, and has already given his loyalty — his soul — to Satan in exchange for power and authority. Possession does not violate that rule, because possession requires an invitation. An entity cannot take a person over against his will. A person has to ask for something, or surrender his rights in exchange for what he wants — and the entity will bargain without revealing what it is really bargaining for. The Antichrist made that choice with open hands.

The same logic runs through everything Satan does at the end. He does not say, "Everyone must worship me." He says, in effect, "Worship me and you may participate in my covenant." Refuse, and you cannot buy food; refuse further, and you die. That is brutal coercion — but it is still a choice. It is the same choice Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego faced before Nebuchadnezzar's statue, and the same choice Revelation 12:11 describes: the saints *loved not their lives even unto death.* The choice is to serve the Antichrist or to die, and it remains a choice.

## The Woman in the Wilderness

*"And when the dragon saw that he had been thrown down to the earth, he pursued the woman who had given birth to the male child. But the woman was given the two wings of the great eagle so that she might fly from the serpent into the wilderness, to the place where she is to be nourished for a time, and times, and half a time."* (Revelation 12:13–14)

Having lost heaven, the dragon's first move on earth is to go after Israel. "A time, times, and half a time" is three and a half years — the same period as the 1,260 days of verse 6, restated. It is the same picture: the Jews fleeing to the east, to the mountains, to a place prepared for them and protected from the Antichrist.

The eagle's wings echo what God said to Israel at Sinai: *"You yourselves have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself"* (Exodus 19:4). It describes divine providence carrying them through the wilderness. Whether the fulfillment involves an airlift, some other means, or something no one has thought of, the point is that God himself carries them to the place he prepared and shields them there.

*"The serpent poured water like a river out of his mouth after the woman, to sweep her away with a flood. But the earth came to the help of the woman, and the earth opened its mouth and swallowed the river that the dragon had poured from his mouth."* (Revelation 12:15–16)

Throughout the Old Testament, being swept away by a flood is the language of military invasion. This is some kind of physical attempt to capture and kill those who are fleeing — and the earth itself swallows it. Is it literal water, and a literal opening of the ground? The text does not settle the mechanism. It settles the outcome: the dragon cannot reach them.

*"Then the dragon became furious with the woman and went off to make war on the rest of her offspring, on those who keep the commandments of God and hold to the testimony of Jesus."* (Revelation 12:17)

Unable to reach the ones who fled, he turns on the rest. The study reads this verse as naming two groups within the woman's offspring: those who keep the commandments of God — observant Jews who follow the Law — and those who hold to the testimony of Jesus, the church. From Satan's perspective the distinction is irrelevant; he goes after both. God does distinguish between them — they fall into slightly different covenant categories — but both belong to the covenants of God. *(How much that distinction matters to God, as opposed to us, will come into sharper focus later in Revelation.)*

This scene is not the sealing of the [[Glossary#144,000|144,000]]. That happens later in the sequence, after the gathering of the church. Revelation 12:17 is set at the Abomination of Desolation, before the Great Tribulation has run its course.

## The Dragon on the Shore

*"And he stood on the sand of the sea. And I saw a beast rising out of the sea."* (Revelation 12:17b–13:1)

Where Satan stands is deliberate. The mighty angel of Revelation 10 — Christ — planted one foot on the sea and one foot on the land, claiming both. Satan stands on the sand, at the edge of the sea, and something rises out of it.

There are two characters here. The dragon is Satan. The beast is the little horn of Daniel — the Antichrist. Satan standing on the shore while the beast rises is not a contradiction of his possessing the Antichrist, because this is a vision, not a monster literally surfacing from the Mediterranean. The image depicts a transition. Satan no longer has access to heaven; he cannot take his accusations there anymore. So he stands at the shoreline and says, in effect: *now I work through the beast. I am taking it to another level.*

## The Beast From the Sea

*"And I saw a beast rising out of the sea, with ten horns and seven heads, with ten diadems on its horns and blasphemous names on its heads."* (Revelation 13:1)

The [[Glossary#Beast from the Sea|beast]] rises from the [[Glossary#Sea (Biblical Symbol of Chaos)|sea]] — the realm of chaos — and carries the same ten horns and seven heads as the dragon. Some things match; one thing does not.

The seven heads are the same because the beast comes out of the same tradition as the dragon's heads: the line of kingdoms that have tried to destroy Israel. The ten horns are the same because they are the ten kings of the final kingdom. The difference is the crowns. The dragon wears seven [[Glossary#Diadem|diadems]], on its heads. The beast wears ten, on its horns.

A diadem is a royal crown, and it signifies kings. The dragon has seven crowns because Satan has used the kings of all seven kingdoms across history — Pharaoh, Nebuchadnezzar, Alexander, all of them — and he is not constrained by time. The beast has ten crowns because the Antichrist is a man, confined to his own time and space. His authority is the authority of the ten kings who rise within his one kingdom. The dragon's crowns span history. The beast's crowns span one final empire.

Then the description: *"And the beast that I saw was like a leopard; its feet were like a bear's, and its mouth was like a lion's mouth. And to it the dragon gave his power and his throne and great authority."* (Revelation 13:2)

Whenever Revelation uses a symbol, it points back to the Old Testament, and this one points straight to Daniel 7. There, the lion was Babylon, the bear was Medo-Persia, and the leopard was Greece. John sees a single beast built from all three. Daniel described the last kingdom as a separate beast with iron teeth, different from the others. John's vision reveals what that last kingdom is made of: it takes up parts of every kingdom that came before it.

That is a geographic statement as much as a historical one. Babylon lay to the east and north of Israel. Medo-Persia lay further east and south of it. Greece reached through Asia Minor to the edge of Europe. Stand in Israel facing the Mediterranean and those three empires form a crescent around the land — from Greece through Turkey and Lebanon in the north, across the lands to the east, and down around to North Africa. Nearly every nation within that crescent today is Islamic. The composite beast describes the region the Antichrist's kingdom will come from.

## The Mortal Wound

*"One of its heads seemed to have a mortal wound, but its mortal wound was healed, and the whole earth marveled as they followed the beast. And they worshiped the dragon, for he had given his authority to the beast, and they worshiped the beast, saying, 'Who is like the beast, and who can fight against it?'"* (Revelation 13:3–4)

There has been endless speculation about this verse. The key is to hold on to what the heads represent — kingdoms. For a kingdom to suffer a mortal wound, it has to cease to exist as a kingdom. For the wound to be healed, it has to come back.

The Islamic caliphate held power, in one form or another, for well over a thousand years. After World War I it was dismantled. The Ottoman Empire collapsed, Turkey was reconstituted as a secular state under Atatürk, the caliph was exiled, and in 1924 the caliphate was formally abolished. The peoples it had united devolved into separate states, with borders drawn not by the people of the region but by the victorious Western powers, on maps in Europe. The kingdom died. It has now been dead for over a hundred years. If it returns as a unified caliphate under one ruler, it will look like a kingdom risen from the dead — and the whole world will marvel.

Muslims never disappeared, of course. What died was the kingdom — the unified political-religious entity — not the religion or its people. And this reading fits the way the heads are presented throughout: they are kingdoms, not individual men. That does not rule out a personal element. The Antichrist could suffer some wound and be healed of it as a display of power, and a double fulfillment is possible. But the symbol, read consistently, points to a kingdom.

There is a precedent for a nation dying and rising. Israel ceased to exist as a nation and came back in 1948. The Antichrist's kingdom rising from the dead is one more way the counterfeit imitates God — a resurrection of its own to rival the real one.

## Forty-Two Months of Blasphemy

*"And the beast was given a mouth uttering haughty and blasphemous words, and it was allowed to exercise authority for forty-two months. It opened its mouth to utter blasphemies against God, blaspheming his name and his dwelling, that is, those who dwell in heaven. Also it was allowed to make war on the saints and to conquer them. And authority was given it over every tribe and people and language and nation."* (Revelation 13:5–7)

This is Daniel's little horn again — the mouth speaking great things, the war against the saints, the authority to prevail over them (Daniel 7:8, 21, 25). Forty-two months is three and a half years: the same span from the Abomination of Desolation to his death at the end of the seven.

Some conclude from verse 7 that the Antichrist will control the entire earth and every human in it. That is not what it says. Being given authority over people who have free will does not mean controlling them. People will rebel against the Antichrist and fight against him. Not everyone will take the mark of the beast, because everyone will have a choice.

## Written Before the Foundation of the World

*"And all who dwell on earth will worship it, everyone whose name has not been written before the foundation of the world in the book of life of the Lamb who was slain."* (Revelation 13:8)

Everyone will worship the beast except those written in the [[Glossary#Book of Life|book of life]]. That includes the saints living at that moment and everyone who will *become* a saint. The statement is not constrained to a single point in time. A name written in the book of life means that at some point in that person's lifetime — any time before the Great White Throne judgment — he or she will repent and believe on the name of Jesus. It could happen during the tribulation. It could happen during the millennial kingdom. The last opportunity is before that final judgment.

How can a name be written "before the foundation of the world" if a person becomes a believer partway through life? Because the question is being asked from a human standpoint, and the book is written from God's. God is not constrained by time. Romans 8:29 says, *"For those whom he foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son."* [[Glossary#Foreknowledge|Foreknowledge]] comes first in that sentence. God knew beforehand who would choose him. He lets them choose — the choice is genuinely theirs — but he knows before creation who will make it, and the book records it.

This is where the study parts ways with both classic systems. [[Glossary#Calvinism and Arminianism|Calvinism]] holds to strict predestination: before creation God ordained certain people to be saved, and their choosing follows from his ordaining. The study reads Romans 8 in the opposite order. There is not a cause-and-effect chain in which predestination causes the choice; foreknowledge is the reason. God knew who would choose, and for those who chose, he made a way — through Christ. Without Christ dying for our sins, there is no choice at all; we are trapped. What God predestined is that those who would choose him could actually be saved. Arminianism, on this view, errs in the opposite direction. Both are human systems pressing what God has ordained into the shape of human logic and following it to extremes that do not match the text.

> **Key Principle:** Doctrine is man's explanation of what God has ordained. It has to be held more loosely than the text it is trying to explain.

This has a direct practical consequence. If anyone may still come to Christ at any point before the final judgment, Christians have no business condemning people simply because they are not Christians yet. The calling is to love them and help them see the truth — to love them into the kingdom.

## To Captivity He Goes

*"If anyone has an ear, let him hear: If anyone is to be taken captive, to captivity he goes; if anyone is to be slain with the sword, with the sword must he be slain. Here is a call for the endurance and faith of the saints."* (Revelation 13:9–10)

This is the same instruction that runs through Daniel and the prophets, and it follows directly from verse 7: the beast has been given authority to make war on the saints and to conquer them. If you are going to be taken, you will be taken. If you are going to die, you will die.

That does not mean God has abandoned his people. The question is which part of us he is allowing to be subject to the Antichrist. It is the physical body — a temporary shell that will not last and will be discarded. Our real identity, the soul and spirit, he will never hand over to Satan. That is kept, and it will be resurrected. He may not protect our bodies; he will protect our souls.

That is why this calls for [[Glossary#Patient Endurance|endurance]] and faith. If a person does not believe that God has a plan and that his inheritance covers them for eternity, then this life is all there is — and anyone who believes this life is all there is will take the mark. Faith that something better follows is what makes refusing possible.

The Lord says it in advance, over and over, for a reason. When human beings do not understand the plan, or do not trust the person in charge, they take matters into their own hands and fight back. We are a rebellious lot. The warning is given beforehand so that when captivity or the sword comes, it is not received as betrayal but recognized as part of the plan — and endured.

## The Second Beast

*"Then I saw another beast rising out of the earth. It had two horns like a lamb and it spoke like a dragon."* (Revelation 13:11)

Little is written about the second beast, and many people do not realize there is one. One beast rises from the sea; this one rises from the earth. Its two horns like a lamb make it look harmless — even Christ-like. Its voice gives it away.

It functions as the religious authority of the Antichrist's regime — the prophet who speaks on his behalf. Later chapters call it by that name: the [[Glossary#False Prophet|false prophet]] (Revelation 16:13; 19:20). It is the Antichrist's hype man. It exercises the full authority of the first beast in his presence and makes the whole earth worship him. It performs great signs, even calling fire down from heaven in front of people, and by those signs it deceives the inhabitants of the earth.

It tells them to make an image of the beast that was wounded by the sword and yet lived, and it is permitted to give breath to that image, so that the image can speak and have killed anyone who will not worship it. This is Nebuchadnezzar's golden statue all over again. When the music played, everyone was to bow; whoever refused went into the furnace. Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego answered that their God was able to deliver them — *"But if not, be it known to you, O king, that we will not serve your gods or worship the golden image"* (Daniel 3:18). If I die, I die; I will not bow. That is the posture the saints will need.

## The Mark of the Beast

*"Also it causes all, both small and great, both rich and poor, both free and slave, to be marked on the right hand or the forehead, so that no one can buy or sell unless he has the mark, that is, the name of the beast or the number of its name. This calls for wisdom: let the one who has understanding calculate the number of the beast, for it is the number of a man, and his number is 666."* (Revelation 13:16–18)

What does a person have to do to receive the [[Glossary#Mark of the Beast|mark of the beast]]? The text ties it to worship of the image — worship of the Antichrist, and through him, of Satan. To get the mark, a person must bow down and worship.

That settles several things. The mark cannot be received involuntarily. It cannot be slipped into someone unawares. The number one rule of the universe is [[Glossary#Free Will|free will]], and the only way to receive the mark is to choose it. The internet is full of claims that the mark is a vaccine, or a chip that will be implanted when AI takes over, or some other Trojan horse. The mark will not be snuck in. It requires a conscious decision.

Two identifying features follow from the text: there has to be an Antichrist whom people worship, and the mark itself is visible — on the right hand or the forehead. It mirrors the seal God places on the foreheads of his own servants (Revelation 7:3; 14:1).

Much of the fixation on the mark centers on the [[Glossary#Number of the Beast|number]], but the text says the mark is *the name* of the beast, or the number of its name. Whoever receives it will carry his name.

## No More Shadows

The objection is natural: surely no rational person would knowingly choose to worship Satan — people will simply be deceived into it. But this period will not look like the present one. Today Satan operates in the shadows. At this point the Lord is doing the opposite of hiding him: he is revealing the one who has stayed in the shadows and exposing the one who has always demanded our worship. There is no subtlety left, no secrecy. Revelation 13:4 says it outright — the whole world worships the dragon.

Deception still plays its part, but Romans 1 describes deception that is chosen. People are given over to darkness because they deliberately refused to acknowledge the Creator. Whatever lie they have been told — that everything is meaningless, that humanity is an accident of evolution — each person still makes a conscious choice about whether to believe it once they have the information.

What decides that choice is the mindset a person already has. Someone who worships power, money, or prosperity — who will do whatever it takes to get what they think they are owed in this life — will love the beast. He embodies power, charisma, and success. And yes, taking the mark is the only way to eat. Food matters a great deal if making it in this life is what matters most. But from a biblical worldview, how important is the body in the scheme of eternity?

The goal in the Great Tribulation is not to survive. It is not to stockpile or to outmaneuver the system. If the Lord wants a person to survive, that person will survive; he will provide a way, as he did for Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. A believer consumed with prepping and preserving his own life is not witnessing and is not loving. He is trying to preserve something that is worth nothing in the scheme of eternity. Nobody wants to die. But the whole point is a paradigm shift: the body is a temporary tent. Believers are deployed to a battlefield, and they do not go home until the Lord comes back.

People will not choose in ignorance. Believers will be telling them plainly that worshiping the beast is worshiping Satan and that Jesus is the alternative. And the Lord does not rely on human messengers alone. The two witnesses are already on the scene, and angels will carry the message to all of humanity. *(The angels delivering messages to the earth, along with a return to the 144,000, are the subject of the next session.)* The church remains the primary conduit of his love.

## The Number of a Man

What does it mean to "calculate the number of the beast"? There is a whole discipline — [[Glossary#Gematria|gematria]] — that assigns numerical values to the letters of an alphabet and adds them up to derive a number from a name. In Hebrew, David is spelled with three consonants, dalet-vav-dalet, and their values sum to fourteen. The same can be done in Greek, in Arabic, and in other languages, with different methods and variations, and the result is hundreds of candidate names for the Antichrist.

The study does not calculate it. The confusion and contradiction among the theories is too great to teach any of them in good conscience. Debunking every theory one at a time would take years. The better approach is the one used by those trained to detect counterfeit currency: know the real thing so thoroughly that the counterfeit is obvious on sight.

Nobody needs to know the Antichrist's name in advance. What he will do, and when, is already written. When he does it, it will be clear who he is. His fruit will reveal his character far more reliably than any numerological guess — and a guess is all a calculation can be.

The sequence is what matters. When he confirms the [[Glossary#Covenant with Many|covenant with many]], the exact day may not be visible. But what the covenant produces will be: peace, and with it the resumption of sacrifice in Israel. When the sacrifices start, the seven years are underway. Once the first event of the sequence happens, cause and effect lay out the rest of the timeline. The exact day cannot be fixed, but it can be estimated backward — and because the Lord has consistently acted on his appointed feasts, certain dates are more likely than others. Passover and Rosh Hashanah are strong candidates. The first four of the seven feasts were fulfilled at Christ's first coming on the very days of those feasts; the remaining three are likely to be fulfilled the same way. *(The feasts and the overall timeline of Revelation will be revisited when the study reaches the end of the book.)*

## The Church at the Midpoint

Anyone alive at this three-and-a-half-year point will see these events. The Lord does not return until partway into the tribulation, so the entire church is still on the earth at the Abomination of Desolation. Some believers will have died before it, but the church will not be taken home for a year and a half to two years after it.

The latest possible point can be estimated backward. The Lord's return has to leave room for the events that follow the gathering: the fifth trumpet alone runs five months, and the bowls of wrath come after the trumpets. That places his return no later than roughly the six-and-a-half-year mark of the seven. When the trumpets begin is not stated, and any more precise date is a guess — informed by the pattern of the feasts, but a guess.

## Corrupt and Counterfeit: The Unholy Trinity

Who is the second beast? Scripture does not say. For that matter, the name of the first beast is not known either — what is known is what each of them does. The Antichrist's region of origin is identifiable only because there is so much prophetic evidence pointing to it. There is no comparable evidence about where the false prophet comes from; this is essentially the only passage that describes him.

What can be said is how Satan operates. He cannot create. He is not omnipotent and does not have God's power. So he does two things: he corrupts what God has created, and he counterfeits what God does. The dragon, the beast, and the false prophet form an [[Glossary#Unholy Trinity|unholy trinity]] — a counterfeit of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit.

Islamic eschatology presents a mirror image of the biblical end times. It awaits the Mahdi, who on this study's reading is the figure the Bible calls the Antichrist; it worships Allah, whom the study identifies not with the God of Israel but with Satan; and it has a prophetic figure who accompanies the Mahdi. Read side by side, the Qur'anic end-times scenario inverts the biblical one point for point. That is Satan's strategy: *this is going to happen, and I will explain it this way. The Bible is the smokescreen; this is what is really going on.* When the figures begin rising to power, the counterfeit explanation will be ready and waiting.

That is the chapter's whole movement. Up to the war in heaven, Satan and the Antichrist work through subterfuge and deception. After it, at this point in the Lord's plan, they are stripped bare and revealed to the world, for those who have eyes to see. The gloves are off. He declares openly what he has always wanted: to be worshiped as God.
