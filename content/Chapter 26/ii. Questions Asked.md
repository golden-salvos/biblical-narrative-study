*A record of genuine questions raised during this session, with the answers given in the room. This is a companion to the main chapter note, not a replacement for it.*

### Q: Is the Antichrist pitching his tents between the sea and the holy mountain in Daniel 11:45 the Battle of Armageddon?

No — that is far too early. At Daniel 11:45 the Antichrist is still consolidating his power. He has fought his wars with Egypt and its allies and answered threats from the east and north, and now he has camped his army outside Jerusalem, between the Mediterranean and Mount Zion. He has not yet entered the city or the temple. This is the scene Luke describes — Jerusalem surrounded by armies — and it is the immediate precursor to the Abomination of Desolation. Armageddon comes at the end of the seven years, not the middle.

### Q: Doesn't Daniel 12:1 say that Michael protects Israel? Isn't that what he's doing when he arises?

"The great prince who has charge of your people" — or "who protects your people" — is a description of who Michael is: Israel's appointed angelic guardian. It is not a description of what he does at this moment. The only action the verse records is that he *arises*. Daniel gives the precise timestamp and deliberately withholds the content. Revelation 12:7 supplies it: he arises to make war on the dragon and expel him from heaven.

### Q: So is Revelation 12 describing what happens before the Antichrist appears on the earth?

The Antichrist is already on the earth and already powerful by this point — he has spent the first half of the seven years consolidating his kingdom. Revelation 12:7 is set right before the Abomination of Desolation: his army is outside Jerusalem, Michael arises, and the Great Tribulation follows. The war in heaven is the hinge between the Antichrist's masquerade as a man of peace and his open demand for worship.

### Q: Could the woman be Israel, since the sun, moon, and stars echo the dream in Genesis?

Yes, and that is the correct reading. The imagery comes from Joseph's second dream in Genesis 37:9–11. Jacob himself interpreted it: the sun was Jacob, the moon was Joseph's mother, and the eleven stars were Joseph's brothers. With Joseph added, the stars number twelve — and Jacob's name had been changed to Israel. The woman crowned with twelve stars is the nation of Israel. The picture is not an exact copy of the dream; the eleven stars have become a full crown of twelve.

### Q: Mary is part of Israel, too. Doesn't that make her the woman?

Mary belongs to Israel, and she is the woman through whom the seed was actually born, so the picture touches her. But the vision is about the whole nation. The Mary reading breaks down as the chapter continues: the woman flees into the wilderness for 1,260 days at the end of the age, the dragon pursues her, and then he goes to war against "the rest of her offspring." That is the history and future of Israel, not the life of one woman.

### Q: How do we count twelve stars if Joseph's two sons each became a tribe? Wasn't Levi still a tribe? And didn't Reuben lose his inheritance?

Jacob had twelve sons, and they are the twelve stars. The tribal count gets more complicated afterward, and all three of these observations are part of why.

Reuben, the firstborn, lost the birthright after sleeping with his father's concubine Bilhah (Genesis 35:22; 49:3–4). The firstborn's double portion passed to Joseph — not because he was next in line, but by his father's blessing — and it was expressed by adopting Joseph's two sons, Ephraim and Manasseh, as full tribes (Genesis 48:5; 1 Chronicles 5:1–2). That produces thirteen tribal lines.

Scripture keeps the count at twelve by context. When the land is divided, Levi receives no territorial inheritance — the Levites were the priestly tribe, supported by the others and given cities and the service of the sanctuary instead — so Ephraim and Manasseh fill out the twelve land allotments (Joshua 14:3–4). Levi remains a tribe of Israel throughout; it simply drops out of the lists concerned with land. Other lists handle the count differently. Revelation 7's list of the 144,000, for example, includes both Levi and Joseph alongside Manasseh, and omits Dan and Ephraim. The number twelve is fixed; which names fill it depends on what the list is for.

### Q: Are the seven heads the seven kingdoms left over after the Antichrist conquers three of the ten?

No. The *horns* are the ten kings of the final kingdom, three of whom the Antichrist subdues. The *heads* are something else: the succession of empires across history that tried to destroy Israel and the messianic seed — Egypt, Assyria, Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome, and finally the Antichrist's own kingdom.

### Q: Were all of those empires ones that tried to destroy Israel, before 70 AD?

Yes. Egypt tried to kill Israel in its infancy. Assyria exiled the ten northern tribes. Babylon destroyed Jerusalem and exiled Judah. Under Medo-Persia, Haman plotted the annihilation of the Jews. Greece, through Antiochus IV Epiphanes, tried to erase Jewish worship. Rome destroyed Jerusalem and the temple in 70 AD. Each one destroyed, exiled, or attempted to eliminate the people through whom the Messiah would come — or, in Rome's case, the people who had already produced him.

### Q: What does the word "caliphate" mean?

A caliphate is an Islamic political-religious union: many peoples united under a single ruler, the caliph, who holds both political and religious authority. It functions without internal borders — nations that have agreed to come under one man. In Islamic eschatology, the end-times leader expected to unite the Muslim world in this way is called the Mahdi.

### Q: Is the dragon the Antichrist?

No. The dragon is Satan — Revelation 12:9 identifies him in so many words as "that ancient serpent, who is called the devil and Satan." The Antichrist is a human being. He did not exist during the ages of Egypt, Assyria, Babylon, Medo-Persia, and Greece. Satan did, which is why all seven heads belong to the dragon. The Antichrist appears in the next scene as the beast rising from the sea.

### Q: Satan was behind the crucifixion, then watched Christ rise and ascend into heaven. How could he see that and not realize he had lost?

He knows exactly who the Son of God is, and he knows he has lost. But there is no recovery from what he has done. His rebellion is final. With nothing left to gain for himself, his remaining goal is to take as many with him as he can.

### Q: Do demons and fallen angels ever get the chance to change their minds, the way humans can?

No. Angels made their choice, and the Watchers made theirs, and no forgiveness is offered to them. The reason is the one Enoch makes: the angels saw God in the fullness of his glory, were exposed to all of it, and still chose to rebel. There is nothing further that could be shown to them. Humanity has never seen God's glory directly — we see through a glass darkly — and that is part of why grace is extended to us and a real choice remains open throughout a human lifetime.

### Q: What does Jesus mean in Luke 10:18 when he says, "I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven"?

The study reads it as prophetic sight. When a prophet says "I saw," the question is what timeline the vision refers to. Daniel said, "I saw the little horn come up," and it had not happened yet. Jesus's statement carries no timestamp, and it describes something that will certainly happen — the event Revelation 12 records — not something that had just occurred. The same is true when he says he holds the keys of death and Hades: he possesses them now, yet death and Hades still operate, because he is waiting for the appointed time for his kingdom to be revealed on the earth.

It is worth knowing that interpreters divide on this verse. Some read it as Jesus's commentary on the disciples' mission — Satan's power visibly broken as the seventy-two cast out demons — and some as a reference to Satan's original fall. The Greek verb is in the imperfect tense ("I was watching"), which does not by itself fix the timing either way.

### Q: But the verses right after that are in the present tense — "I have given you authority to tread on serpents and scorpions... rejoice that your names are written in heaven." Isn't Jesus talking about something that just happened?

He is — but not about Satan's expulsion from heaven. Because of who Jesus is and what his death and resurrection would accomplish, the disciples had real authority over spirits. They cast out demons and healed the sick, and that happened immediately, on the earth. That is a very different thing from Satan being thrown out of heaven. Satan did not need to be expelled from heaven for the disciples to cast demons out of people in the villages they visited. They did not cast down the principality over Rome or free Jerusalem from Roman rule. Local authority over spirits was granted then; the heavenly authority structure was not yet shaken, and the accuser's expulsion is still future.

### Q: Why does Satan need to use the Antichrist's body? Why can't he go straight to the temple himself and declare himself God?

He could manifest himself — spiritual beings can make themselves visible, and Scripture records it. The obstacle is not visibility; it is the rules. The Watchers were thrown into the abyss because they manifested physically on the earth, took whomever they wanted, and usurped human free will by exerting angelic power. An angelic being is inherently stronger than any man, and overriding human choice is the line that gets a spiritual being removed from the board entirely. Satan was not sent to the abyss because he did not do that. In Eden he gave Adam and Eve a choice; he deceived, but he did not compel.

So Satan still has to work through a human agent who has chosen him. The Antichrist is already in position, already holds the power, and has already given his loyalty to Satan in exchange for power and authority. The same pattern appears in the crucifixion: Satan did not kill Jesus directly. He worked through human beings — the leaders who handed him over and the Romans who executed him.

### Q: Isn't possession itself taking away a person's choice?

No. Possession requires an invitation. An entity cannot take a person over against his will, whether or not that person is a Christian. The person has to ask for something, or give up his rights in exchange for what he wants — and the entity will bargain without revealing what it is really bargaining for. It is a decision, even if it is made in ignorance of the full price. The Antichrist has made that decision with complete loyalty.

### Q: How is "worship me or you can't eat" a real choice? That sounds like being forced.

It is brutal coercion, but it is still a choice, because the alternative remains open: refusing and dying. Revelation 12:11 describes the saints who choose it — they "loved not their lives even unto death." It is the same choice Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego faced before Nebuchadnezzar's statue: bow or burn. Satan does not say, "Everyone must worship me." He says, "Worship me, and you may participate in my covenant." The price for refusing is severe, but the decision is left to the person.

### Q: Is Revelation 12:15 — the serpent pouring out water like a river — describing a military invasion?

Most likely. In the Old Testament, being swept away by a flood is standard language for military invasion (Daniel 9:26; 11:10, 22, 40; Isaiah 8:7–8). The verse describes some physical attempt to capture and kill those fleeing — and the earth swallows it. The text settles the outcome rather than the mechanism. Whether literal water and a literal opening of the ground are involved is not something the passage requires us to decide, any more than the "two wings of the great eagle" require a literal eagle. The eagle's wings recall God's words at Sinai — "I bore you on eagles' wings" (Exodus 19:4) — and describe his providence carrying his people to safety.

### Q: Who are "those who keep the commandments of God and hold to the testimony of Jesus"? Wouldn't that just be Jews?

The study reads the verse as naming two groups within the woman's offspring: observant Jews who keep the commandments of God, and the church, which holds to the testimony of Jesus. From Satan's perspective the distinction does not matter — he goes after both. From God's perspective, both belong to his covenants, though they fall into slightly different categories.

### Q: Is this group the 144,000?

No. The sealing of the 144,000 comes later in the sequence, after the Lord has come for the church. Revelation 12:17 is set at the Abomination of Desolation, at the start of the Great Tribulation.

### Q: If the beast is the Antichrist possessed by Satan, how can Satan be standing on the seashore while the beast rises from the sea? Can he be in both places?

This is a vision, not a literal monster surfacing out of the Mediterranean, and the image is symbolic. It depicts a transition. Satan stands on the shore because he no longer has access to heaven and can no longer bring his accusations there. The beast rising from the sea is Satan's new method: he now works directly through the Antichrist. The two figures on the page represent a change in how Satan operates, not two locations he occupies at once.

### Q: Why does the dragon have seven crowns and the beast ten?

A diadem is a royal crown, and it signifies kings. The dragon's seven crowns sit on its seven heads: Satan has used the kings of every one of the seven kingdoms — Pharaoh, Nebuchadnezzar, Alexander, and the rest — because he is not bound by time. The beast's ten crowns sit on its ten horns: the Antichrist is a human being confined to his own time and place, and his authority is exercised through the ten kings who rise within his single kingdom. The dragon's crowns span history. The beast's crowns span one final empire.

### Q: People say the Antichrist will have a head wound. Is the "mortal wound" literal, or is it about something else?

The heads throughout this vision represent kingdoms, not individual men, and a consistent reading points to a kingdom. For a kingdom to suffer a mortal wound, it has to cease to exist; for the wound to heal, it has to come back. The Islamic caliphate was abolished in 1924 and has been dead for over a century. If it is restored as a unified caliphate, it will look like a kingdom risen from the dead. That does not rule out a personal element — the Antichrist could suffer an actual wound and be healed as a display of power, and a double fulfillment is possible — but the symbol fits the kingdom reading.

An audience member noted that Israel itself ceased to exist as a nation and came back in 1948. The parallel is apt: the Antichrist's kingdom rising from the dead is one more way the counterfeit imitates what God has done.

### Q: I thought our names are written in the book of life when we become Christians. How can a name be written "before the foundation of the world"? And can a name be written in after someone dies?

No one comes to Christ after death; the opportunity runs through a person's lifetime, and the last point it can be exercised is before the Great White Throne judgment. The question of "before the foundation of the world" is being asked from a human standpoint. From God's standpoint, he knows from the beginning who will accept Christ. Romans 8:29 says, "For those whom he foreknew he also predestined." God lets each person choose, and the choice is theirs — but he knows before creation who will make it, and the book records it. For people living through the tribulation or the millennial kingdom, that choice can come at any point before the final judgment, and if they will make it, their names are already in the book.

### Q: Is this where Calvinism gets its doctrine of predestination?

Partly. Calvinism's primary text is Romans 8–9, with passages like Revelation 13:8 read in support. Calvinism teaches strict predestination: before creation, God ordained certain people to be saved. The study reads Romans 8:29 differently: foreknowledge is the reason, not predestination. God knew who would choose him, and for those who chose, he made a way through Christ — without which there would be no choice at all. The study regards both Calvinism and Arminianism as human systems that press what God has ordained into human logic and follow it to extremes that do not match the text. Doctrine is man's explanation of what God has ordained, and it has to be held accordingly.

### Q: So the two things that distinguish the real mark of the beast from all the false claims are that there has to be an Antichrist you worship, and the mark has to be visible?

Yes. The mark requires worship of the beast and his image — a conscious choice — and it is identifiable, placed on the right hand or the forehead, the counterpart of the seal of God placed on the foreheads of his servants. A vaccine, a chip, or anything that can be received without knowingly worshiping the Antichrist is not the mark.

### Q: Couldn't people be deceived into it — trusting AI or government or anything that offers them an easy solution — without really knowing they are choosing Satan?

The deception is real, but this period will not look like the present one. Today Satan works in the shadows. At this point the Lord is exposing him — the whole world is openly worshiping the dragon (Revelation 13:4), and there is no subtlety left. Believers will also be telling people directly that worshiping the beast is worshiping Satan, and God will send his own messengers besides. Romans 1 describes deception that people choose: they are given over to it because they deliberately refused to acknowledge the Creator. Whatever lie a person has been told, they still make a conscious decision about whether to believe it once the truth is in front of them. What decides it is the mindset they already hold — whether their life is built around what this world can give them.

### Q: What does it mean to "calculate the number of the beast, for it is the number of a man"?

It refers to the practice of assigning numerical values to letters and adding up a name — known in Hebrew as gematria. An audience member gave the example of David: in Hebrew, written with three consonants (dalet, vav, dalet), with values of 4, 6, and 4, totaling fourteen. The same can be done in Greek, Arabic, and other languages, with many variations, and the internet holds hundreds of candidate names as a result. The study does not calculate the number. The contradictions among the theories are too great to teach any of them in good conscience, and a calculation can only ever be a guess. Knowing what the Antichrist will do, and when, is far more reliable. His fruit will reveal who he is.

### Q: So the mark is a name, not the number 666?

The text says the mark is "the name of the beast or the number of its name." The number is the numerical value of the name. The emphasis usually falls on 666, but what a person receives is the beast's name — written on the right hand or the forehead.

### Q: Aren't we in one of the feasts right now, in September?

Yes — Rosh Hashanah falls in the early fall, in a cluster of feasts that follows the spring feasts. The Lord fulfilled the first four of the seven feasts on the very days those feasts were being observed, and the remaining three — which include the fall feasts — are likely to be fulfilled the same way. That is why feast days like Passover and Rosh Hashanah are the more likely dates for key end-times events. The feasts will be revisited when the study reaches the end of Revelation.

### Q: When exactly is the church gathered? Are you saying it happens at the six-and-a-half-year mark?

Not at it — no later than it. The entire church is on the earth at the three-and-a-half-year midpoint and sees the Abomination of Desolation. The gathering comes sometime in the second half, likely a year and a half to two years after the midpoint. The latest possible point is estimated backward: the fifth trumpet alone lasts five months, and the bowls of wrath follow the trumpets, so the Lord's return has to leave room for all of that before the end of the seventh year. That puts it no later than about the six-and-a-half-year mark. The year the trumpets begin is not given, so anything more precise is an informed guess.

### Q: Do we know who the second beast is?

No. For that matter, the first beast's name is not known either — what is known is what he does. The first beast is the Antichrist; the second is his false prophet, the religious voice that promotes him and compels worship of him. The Antichrist's region of origin is identifiable only because a great deal of prophecy points to it. There is no comparable evidence for where the false prophet comes from; this is essentially the only passage that describes him.
