## Recap

The previous session covered Revelation 17 and 18, where the angel explains the mystery of [[Glossary#Babylon the Great|Babylon the Great]]. The [[Glossary#Great Prostitute|great prostitute]] rides the beast with seven heads and ten horns, and the angel says plainly what she is: the great city that has dominion over the kings of the earth. Her dominion is influence, bought with wealth and trade. The seven heads are the empires that came against Israel and the promised seed, and the beast that was, and is not, and is to come is the revived caliphate. The [[Glossary#Ten Kings|ten kings]] hand their power to the [[Glossary#Antichrist|Antichrist]], and then they and the beast turn on the woman and burn her with fire, because God has put it into their hearts to carry out his purpose.

Revelation 18 shows that the great city is a seaport, mourned by kings, merchants, and sailors, and its cargo list looks back to the Red Sea trade of Solomon's day. In a single hour she is laid waste. She becomes a haunt for demons and every unclean creature, and she is never rebuilt. The session weighed candidates on Saudi Arabia's Red Sea coast, with the reminder that what Scripture says is certain and every identification of a modern city is held loosely.

The rule for reading these chapters still holds. Each one is a vision, and each vision has to be placed on the timeline by its own clues. Revelation 19 moves from Babylon's ruin on the earth to a scene in heaven.

## The Hallelujah Chorus in Heaven

*"After this I heard what seemed to be the loud voice of a great multitude in heaven, crying out, 'Hallelujah! Salvation and glory and power belong to our God, for his judgments are true and just; for he has judged the great prostitute who corrupted the earth with her immorality, and has avenged on her the blood of his servants.' Once more they cried out, 'Hallelujah! The smoke from her goes up forever and ever.'"* (Revelation 19:1–3)

### Placing the Vision

The scene has no exact timestamp, but it can be bracketed. The [[Glossary#Great Multitude|great multitude]] is the church, already in heaven, so the scene cannot come before the gathering of the church between the sixth and seventh seals. It also comes before the Battle of [[Glossary#Armageddon|Armageddon]], which follows in the same chapter. It falls somewhere between the seventh seal and the end of the seventh bowl, and most likely close to the end.

### Witnesses to Justice

The great multitude is looking down on the earth and watching the [[Glossary#Wrath of God|wrath of God]] poured out on it. The church is a spectator to God's judgment.

That matters because of what believers have longed for from the moment they understood what is wrong with this world: justice. They want wrong punished and right vindicated. The martyrs under the altar cried out for exactly that. Here God brings his people into heaven and lets them watch him carry it out.

### Training the Rulers of the Kingdom

There is a second reason. If eternity were nothing more than playing harps and drifting among the clouds, there would be little point in showing the church the execution of God's judgment. But that is not the church's future. Daniel says the saints will be given over to the Antichrist for a time, and then the kingdom will be handed over to them (Daniel 7:25–27). Elsewhere Scripture says they will reign with Christ (2 Timothy 2:12; Revelation 20:4–6). Believers are going to be rulers in his kingdom.

How do you prepare leaders? You give them responsibility, a little at a time, to see what they do with it. You tell them how you want that responsibility carried out. And you let them watch you do it. That is what is happening here. God is letting his people watch him govern the earth, because they are going to help him govern it.

The question is whose place they are taking. Adam and Eve were the first to fail in that role, and they were not the last. The nations are currently governed in the spiritual realm by members of the [[Glossary#Divine Council|divine council]] who rebelled with Satan, and they have not ruled according to God's will (Psalm 82). They are being judged and sent away for eternity. Believers are taking their place.

> **Key Principle:** The church witnesses God's judgment from heaven because it is being prepared to rule. A whole class of beings, spiritual and earthly, is judged for its choices, and those who gave their allegiance to Christ take the place of the rebellious powers who governed the nations.

### Why There Has to Be a Test

This is the answer to the question people ask most often: why is all of this happening, and why does God allow the bad things?

Go back to [[Glossary#Free Will|free will]]. How do you get faithful followers, people who love you freely and for your own sake? They have to have a choice. God's angels had a choice. Some made a bad one, and they are being punished for it. They will not be ruling in the kingdom. They are being locked away, and believers are taking their place.

Love cannot be commanded into existence. If you begin with a kingdom in which everyone is required to pay attention and obey, there is no choice, and that is not love. It is slavery. It is a world of robots, and God does not want that. However many permutations you run, there is only one way to arrive at a kingdom of free choosers who are loyal and obedient and love their King with all their heart because they want to: there must first be a period of choice, in which each person decides one way or the other.

However you look at this life on the earth, then, it is a test, a trial, and it is pass or fail. Choose poorly, and the consequence is eternal. Choose wisely, and the reward is eternal. Those who do not pass do not enter the kingdom. They get exactly what they wanted: separation from it.

Suffering belongs in that picture. A real choice requires a real alternative, something that can actually be pursued instead of God, and that is how the people who will make that choice are separated out. Inside a worldview built only on the experiences of this world, none of it makes sense. It only makes sense in light of eternity. That is the difference between a worldly worldview and a [[Glossary#Biblical Worldview|biblical worldview]], which sees from God's perspective. And no one can have God's perspective without his word, because there is no other way to figure it out.

## The Marriage of the Lamb

*"And the twenty-four elders and the four living creatures fell down and worshiped God who was seated on the throne, saying, 'Amen. Hallelujah!' And from the throne came a voice saying, 'Praise our God, all you his servants, you who fear him, small and great.' Then I heard what seemed to be the voice of a great multitude, like the roar of many waters and like the sound of mighty peals of thunder, crying out, 'Hallelujah! For the Lord our God the Almighty reigns. Let us rejoice and exult and give him the glory, for the marriage of the Lamb has come, and his Bride has made herself ready; it was granted her to clothe herself with fine linen, bright and pure' — for the fine linen is the righteous deeds of the saints."* (Revelation 19:4–8)

The [[Glossary#Twenty-Four Elders|twenty-four elders]] and the [[Glossary#Four Living Creatures|four living creatures]] join the multitude in worship, and the reason for the celebration is announced: the marriage of the Lamb has come, and the [[Glossary#Bride of Christ|Bride]] has made herself ready.

### The White Robes

The clothing of the saints has been carried through Revelation. Think back to the fifth seal. The souls under the altar cried out, *how long, O Lord, before you avenge our blood?* — and God gave each of them a white robe and told them to wait a little longer (Revelation 6:9–11). In the next scene, the great multitude stands before the throne, and they are wearing white robes (Revelation 7:9, 14).

Now Revelation says what the [[Glossary#White Robes and Fine Linen|fine linen]] is: the righteous deeds of the saints. Part of the clothing believers wear in heaven is earned on the earth — in the life lived after accepting the Lord, as his hands and feet, serving, loving, and letting the Holy Spirit work through them.

### The Guest Without Wedding Clothes

That explains one of Jesus's most unsettling parables. In the [[Glossary#Parable of the Wedding Banquet|parable of the wedding banquet]], a king invites guests to his son's wedding, and the invited guests do not come; they are too busy. So he sends his servants out to the roads to call in everyone they find, and the hall fills. Then the king finds a man without wedding clothes. *"Friend, how did you get in here without a wedding garment?"* The man is speechless, and he is bound and cast into the outer darkness, where there is weeping and gnashing of teeth (Matthew 22:1–14).

Read on its own, the parable leaves you asking what you just read. Where did everyone else get their wedding clothes? The parable gives no explanation. It cannot be understood until Revelation 19 supplies it: the wedding clothes are the righteous deeds of the saints.

### Righteous Deeds Are Not a Scorecard

Does that mean the righteous are the ones whose good deeds outweighed their bad — fifty-one percent good, forty-nine percent bad? It cannot mean that. The thief on the cross had no lifetime of righteous deeds to show, and Jesus told him, *"Today you will be with me in paradise"* (Luke 23:43). He got in by believing. That is all.

The old picture of Saint Peter at the gate makes the point, even if the picture itself is silly. If he asks why he should let you in, there is only one answer: because I love Jesus, and Jesus said I could come.

So where do the righteous deeds come from? Once a person has accepted Christ, Christ lives in them by the Holy Spirit. And what the Holy Spirit works in a person is love and good deeds — everything 1 Corinthians 13 describes. The righteous deeds are what believers do in the power of the Holy Spirit, poured into them and poured out through them. Notice the wording of the verse itself: it was *granted* to her to clothe herself. It is not by works. Anyone who doubts that should read Galatians, because Galatians is clear.

> **Key Principle:** The fine linen of the Bride is the righteous deeds of the saints, but those deeds are not a balance sheet that earns entry. They are what the Holy Spirit produces through those who belong to Christ. The only way to be clothed in wedding clothes is through Jesus, believing in him with all your heart, mind, soul, and strength.

## Blessed Are Those Who Are Invited

*"And the angel said to me, 'Write this: Blessed are those who are invited to the marriage supper of the Lamb.' And he said to me, 'These are the true words of God.' Then I fell down at his feet to worship him, but he said to me, 'You must not do that! I am a fellow servant with you and your brothers who hold to the testimony of Jesus. Worship God.' For the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy."* (Revelation 19:9–10)

That is all Revelation says about the [[Glossary#Marriage Supper of the Lamb|marriage supper of the Lamb]]. But it raises a real question: why are those who are *invited* called blessed, and how does a person get invited?

### Children, Not Grandchildren

Who is invited to a wedding supper? The bride and the groom, and their family and friends. Ask it this way: does the Lord have any grandchildren? No. He only has children. No one gets into his family through someone else's faith — through a parent, a spouse, or a church. Each person has to believe in Jesus himself. The invitation to the marriage supper comes only through a personal relationship with him.

### The Parable of the Ten Virgins

It is remarkable how much of Revelation the Lord set up with parables, and this one has its own: the [[Glossary#Parable of the Ten Virgins|parable of the ten virgins]] (Matthew 25:1–13). Ten virgins gather, dressed for the wedding, holding their lamps, waiting for the bridegroom. Five have brought extra oil, and five have not. In the middle of the night the cry goes up that the bridegroom is coming, and the five without oil find their lamps going out. They ask the others to share, and the answer is no — there is not enough for both. Go and buy your own.

Look at the differences between the two groups. They are all virgins. They are all dressed the same. They all have lamps. The only difference is the oil.

The oil represents the Holy Spirit. But more importantly, it represents relationship. The wise virgins had a personal relationship with the source of the oil. They had all they needed and a daily supply of it. The foolish five were counting on others to give them what they needed.

Apply that to the church. There are people who want to check the box — who go to church because it is socially acceptable and because they want to be associated with it. But do they have a relationship with Jesus? They do not have the extra oil. They are trying to get it from someone else.

### "I Never Knew You"

The same warning appears in the Sermon on the Mount. On that day many will say, *"Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in your name, and cast out demons in your name, and do many mighty works in your name?"* And Jesus will declare, *"I never knew you; depart from me"* (Matthew 7:21–23).

Why would he say that to people who did so much in his name? Because of what *knowing* means in Scripture. In Hebrew, to know someone is the language of deep intimacy; it is the word used when a husband and wife come together to conceive a child (Genesis 4:1). You may think you know him. But if he never knew you, you were playing church, not living in a relationship with God. He is looking for a human being in relationship with him, not a human doing. No one can work their way into that.

Think about name-dropping. Why do people throw names around? Association. They are trying to raise their status, their access, or their influence by claiming to be closer to someone important than they are. Jesus is saying it does not matter whether you think you belong to him. If he does not know you, he does not know you.

And when someone invites you to their wedding, who is the controlling party? They are. You do not get in without an invitation, and you cannot force your way through the door. The relationship runs both ways.

### A Specific Event at a Specific Time

The marriage supper of the Lamb is not a picture of salvation in general. It is a specific event at a specific time in history. Everyone is invited to love and serve God. But the invitation to the marriage supper goes to those who have already chosen him.

To be at the marriage supper in heaven, a person has to have been gathered at the [[Glossary#Rapture|rapture]]. And to be gathered, a person has to have loved and accepted Jesus as Lord and Savior *before he is revealed in glory*. Up until Jesus comes back, the church is the Bride of Christ. When he comes back, the Bride and the Groom are married, and the feast is held. When the feast is over, there is not another one.

That does not mean everyone who believes after the Lord returns is lost. People will come to him after his return. Some who have not yet accepted Christ will refuse the [[Glossary#Mark of the Beast|mark of the beast]] because their names are written in the [[Glossary#Book of Life|book of life]], and they will come in later. They will be in his kingdom. But they will not have been at this wedding supper. What that difference means in eternity, Scripture does not say. It says only that those who are invited are blessed.

Why the distinction? Because before he is revealed, belief requires faith. After he appears in glory, there is no faith left in believing him. Every knee will bow, and there will be no denying it. He honors differently those who trusted him before they could see him.

That is not a second chance for those who took the mark. Someone who took it and then sees the Lord return and realizes they chose the wrong side has no way back. Many will not repent at all; they will curse God instead.

> **Key Principle:** Everyone is invited to love and serve God, but the marriage supper of the Lamb is a specific event for a specific people: those who belonged to Christ by faith before he was revealed in glory. Its blessing comes through a personal relationship with him, not through association with his people.

## Faithful and True

*"Then I saw heaven opened, and behold, a white horse! The one sitting on it is called Faithful and True, and in righteousness he judges and makes war. His eyes are like a flame of fire, and on his head are many diadems, and he has a name written that no one knows but himself."* (Revelation 19:11–12)

Where has a white horse appeared in Revelation before? At the first seal. A rider came out on a white horse with a bow in his hand, and he was given a [[Glossary#Victor's Crown (Stephanos)|victor's crown]] and rode out conquering and to conquer (Revelation 6:2). That was the Antichrist arriving on the scene — a poser, the first counterfeit.

This is the real one, coming straight out of heaven. The description deliberately uses the same picture with a different emphasis. The rider on the first white horse wore a victor's wreath, a crown that says *I won*. This rider wears many [[Glossary#Diadem|diadems]], royal crowns, because he is the legitimate King with real royal authority. He is not posing. He is called Faithful and True, where the counterfeit was faithless and a liar. In righteousness he judges and makes war. His eyes are like a flame of fire, and he has a name written that no one knows but himself.

### The Robe Dipped in Blood

*"He is clothed in a robe dipped in blood, and the name by which he is called is The Word of God. And the armies of heaven, arrayed in fine linen, white and pure, were following him on white horses. From his mouth comes a sharp sword with which to strike down the nations, and he will rule them with a rod of iron. He will tread the winepress of the fury of the wrath of God the Almighty. On his robe and on his thigh he has a name written, King of kings and Lord of lords."* (Revelation 19:13–16)

This is the follow-on to the [[Glossary#Winepress of the Wrath of God|winepress of the wrath of God]] from the two harvests of Revelation 14. There, the grape harvest of the earth was thrown into the winepress, the winepress was trodden outside the city, and blood flowed as high as a horse's bridle for 1,600 stadia, about 180 miles (Revelation 14:17–20). Here is the rider whose robe is dipped in that blood. He is the one who treads the winepress.

His name is the Word of God — the [[Glossary#Logos|Word]] who was in the beginning with God and was God (John 1:1). The armies of heaven follow him, dressed in the same fine linen, white and pure, that was given to the Bride. From his mouth comes a sharp sword, and he will rule the nations with a rod of iron (Psalm 2:9). On his robe and on his thigh is written his name: [[Glossary#King of Kings and Lord of Lords|King of kings and Lord of lords]].

Every parable and every prophecy that has been pointing to this moment is fulfilled here.

## The Great Supper of God

*"Then I saw an angel standing in the sun, and with a loud voice he called to all the birds that fly directly overhead, 'Come, gather for the great supper of God, to eat the flesh of kings, the flesh of captains, the flesh of mighty men, the flesh of horses and their riders, and the flesh of all men, both free and slave, both small and great.'"* (Revelation 19:17–18)

This is the summons Ezekiel recorded after the defeat of Gog, when God calls every bird and every beast to a sacrificial feast on the mighty men and princes of the earth (Ezekiel 39:17–20). Revelation 19 is its fulfillment: the [[Glossary#Great Supper of God|great supper of God]].

*"And I saw the beast and the kings of the earth with their armies gathered to make war against him who was sitting on the horse and against his army. And the beast was captured, and with it the false prophet who in its presence had done the signs by which he deceived those who had received the mark of the beast and those who worshiped its image. These two were thrown alive into the lake of fire that burns with sulfur. And the rest were slain by the sword that came from the mouth of him who was sitting on the horse, and all the birds were gorged with their flesh."* (Revelation 19:19–21)

The beast and the [[Glossary#False Prophet|false prophet]] are captured and thrown alive into the [[Glossary#Lake of Fire|lake of fire]]. Everyone else gathered against him is killed by the sword from his mouth.

### Justice Demonstrated

It is a dark scene. Why does the Lord bother to record it? Because justice can be talked about, and justice can be carried out, and this is the second. It is like watching an execution. This is God's wrath poured out on the earth, and justice demonstrated rather than described.

## Patient Endurance and Knowing the End

That brings back the attitude Revelation has asked of believers from the beginning. In chapter 1, John calls himself a partner with the churches in the tribulation, the kingdom, and the [[Glossary#Patient Endurance|patient endurance]] that are in Jesus (Revelation 1:9). The call comes up again and again through the book (Revelation 13:10; 14:12), and Paul says the same to the Thessalonians (2 Thessalonians 1:4).

The church is in heaven when the battle happens. But it has been told about it beforehand, and that is the point. If all believers knew was the cry of the saints under the altar — *how long, Lord, before you avenge us?* — patient endurance would be far harder. A wait for justice with no end in sight wears down faith. It is the confusion Paul had to address when the Thessalonians were told that the day of the Lord had already come (2 Thessalonians 2:2), and the taunt Peter answered from those who said it was never coming (2 Peter 3:3–4).

So why would the Lord tell his people how the story ends, and exactly how he will carry out his justice and his wrath? So that they can endure patiently. Without knowing the end, people are tempted to give up. More often, they question God, and then they fight. They try to take vengeance themselves. And when people take vengeance for themselves, who do they take it on? Each other. That runs directly against what Paul says: the struggle is not against flesh and blood but against the rulers, authorities, and spiritual forces of this dark world (Ephesians 6:12).

The Lord wants his people to endure patiently, with an attitude of love all the way to the end. If believers do not love their enemies on this earth, how will those enemies ever know there is a God in heaven who loves them and forgives them? That is kingdom living, and the only way to do it is through him, knowing how it ends.

> **Key Principle:** Revelation was given so the church would know the end. Two-thirds of the book describes events the church will witness from heaven rather than live through on the earth, and it is recorded so that believers can endure patiently and keep loving their enemies instead of giving up, questioning God, or taking vengeance into their own hands.

## The End of the Seven Years

With the Battle of Armageddon, the study has reached the last event of the seven years. The seventieth week begins with the [[Glossary#Covenant with Many|covenant with many]] and ends with the death of the Antichrist, and the Antichrist meets his end at Armageddon. The Lord has already returned. He has already begun to reign. Now he sets up his thousand-year reign.

## Looking Ahead: Why a Thousand Years?

Revelation 20 opens with the thousand-year reign. Most people do not understand why it exists. Why doesn't the Lord go straight to the new heaven and the new earth?

That is why this study spent so much time on the covenants: the [[Glossary#Abrahamic Covenant|Abrahamic]], the Mosaic, the [[Glossary#Davidic Covenant|Davidic]], and the New. God's covenant promises to Israel have not all been fulfilled. Israel is still waiting for an eternal kingdom with the son of David, their Messiah, as king. They have never occupied the full land grant they were given. They are still waiting for the lasting peace the Messiah was promised to bring. If the Lord said it would happen and it has not happened yet, it will happen — and it will happen in the [[Glossary#Millennial Kingdom|Millennial Kingdom]], in Jerusalem, with the Lord reigning from the temple. The [[Glossary#144,000|144,000]] are its [[Glossary#First Fruits|firstfruits]].

It is not only for the Jews. The Lord promised Abraham that through him all nations would be blessed. Gentile nations will remain on the earth, stripped of their authority, and they will be required to come up to Jerusalem every year to worship the King at the Feast of Tabernacles (Zechariah 14:16). They will have to see that he is not only the King of Israel but ruler over the whole earth. The church will be there too — not ruling from heaven, but on the earth, in [[Glossary#Resurrection Body|resurrection bodies]] like the one Jesus had after he rose.

Ezekiel 40–48 describes the temple he will reign from, including the Lord entering through the eastern gate into the Holy of Holies to begin his reign. Ezekiel 37, the [[Glossary#Valley of Dry Bones|valley of dry bones]], will also need to be revisited. God tells Ezekiel the bones are the whole house of Israel. It may suggest that the Lord will raise up the entire house of Israel to live under their Messiah King. Why would he give that only to the last generation and not to all of them, including Abraham? It certainly reads that way, but it is not something to stand on firmly.

Satan will be taken out of the picture for the thousand years, and people will still have a choice. *(The Millennial Kingdom, the passages that describe it, and the Great White Throne Judgment are the subject of the next session. The new heaven and the new earth, and how they differ from the Millennial Kingdom, will follow in the session after that.)*

### What the Millennium Reveals About God

If the Millennial Kingdom is what it appears to be, it says something about God. He is patient. He brings about whatever he has promised. And he is merciful. Everyone on the earth has had time to make their choice, and he gives chance after chance after chance. How many times did Israel rebel, and how many times did he bring them back into the land?

Many people read parts of the Old Testament and parts of Revelation and conclude that God is capricious and wants to destroy everything. If he wanted to destroy everything, he would have done it long ago. There is a purpose for everything he is doing on the earth, and for everything he is not doing, with a time for each in accordance with his sovereign will.

No one will understand everything. God has not told us everything. But what he has given is what his people need to know, and that is enough to have faith in.
