> *Questions raised during the session, with answers drawn from the teaching.*

---

### Q: If the fine linen is the righteous deeds of the saints, what about someone like the thief on the cross, who had no righteous deeds?

The thief on the cross got into paradise by believing, and that is all (Luke 23:43). The righteous deeds of Revelation 19:8 are not a scorecard in which the good has to outweigh the bad. They are what the Holy Spirit does through a person once Christ lives in them. Entry into the kingdom comes through faith in Jesus, and the text itself says the Bride's clothing was *granted* to her. Galatians settles any question about whether it is by works.

### Q: Where is the parable of the ten virgins?

Matthew 25:1–13. It is part of the Olivet Discourse, immediately after Jesus' teaching on his return in Matthew 24.

### Q: Isn't the "I never knew you" passage connected to the sheep and the goats?

The passage in which people say *"Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in your name?"* and Jesus answers *"I never knew you; depart from me"* is in the Sermon on the Mount, Matthew 7:21–23. The parable of the sheep and the goats is in Matthew 25:31–46, later in the same chapter as the ten virgins. Both passages make the same point: what matters is not what a person did in the Lord's name but whether they belong to him. The foolish virgins hear almost the same words: *"I do not know you"* (Matthew 25:12).

### Q: Isn't it true that God has invited everybody, and it's our choice whether to accept the invitation?

Yes. Everyone is invited to love and serve him, and accepting is each person's choice. But in this study's reading, the marriage supper of the Lamb is a specific event at a specific time in history, and the invitation to it goes to those who have already accepted him: the church, gathered at his return. The verse is not describing salvation in general.

### Q: Does this mean that people who accept Christ after he returns are thrown into hell?

No. People will come to the Lord after his return, including those who refused the mark of the beast because their names are in the book of life. They will be in his kingdom. They will not have been at the marriage supper, which takes place once, when the Bride and the Groom are married. The opportunity to repent runs throughout a person's life, including for those living in the Millennial Kingdom; judgment follows death (Hebrews 9:27).

### Q: Why does it matter whether someone was at the marriage supper?

Scripture does not say what the difference means in eternity. It says that those who are invited are blessed. There is an extra blessing attached to having trusted him before he was revealed in glory, when belief still required faith. After he appears, every knee bows and there is no denying him, so there is no faith involved in believing him.

### Q: Didn't we say a few weeks ago that not everyone will repent when he comes back?

Correct. Those who took the mark of the beast do not repent. Under the bowl judgments they curse God instead (Revelation 16:9, 11, 21). Someone who took the mark, saw the Lord return, and realized they had chosen the wrong side has no way back.

### Q: Wouldn't Calvinists use verse 9, "blessed are those who are invited," to support their view?

The invitation in verse 9 is not an invitation to salvation that some receive and others do not. Everyone is invited to love and serve God. The invitation to the marriage supper goes to those who have already chosen him and are part of his family. Under the New Covenant, a person who accepts the Lord becomes a child of God, and it is as a member of the family that they are invited to the wedding.

### Q: Are we still in heaven while this battle happens, or did we come down to the earth early?

The church comes with the King: the armies of heaven that follow him are clothed in the same fine linen as the Bride (Revelation 19:8, 14; compare 17:14). But the church does not fight; the victory is won by the word of his mouth. And it is told about the battle beforehand, in Revelation, which is why believers can endure patiently: they know how it ends.

### Q: Will the 144,000 never die?

They will not die during the seven years. They are sealed and preserved through all of God's wrath, and they enter the Millennial Kingdom alive as its firstfruits. What happens to them over the course of the thousand years is not described here. *(The Millennial Kingdom is the subject of [[i. The Thousand Years|Chapter 30]].)*

### Q: Will we rule in the Millennial Kingdom from heaven?

No. The church will rule on the earth. Believers will be in their resurrection bodies, like the body Jesus had after he rose, able to appear and disappear, to enter a locked room, and to move from place to place in an instant, but the kingdom is on this earth, centered in Jerusalem.

### Q: Is the eastern gate of Jerusalem still sealed?

Yes. The eastern gate of the Temple Mount, often called the Golden Gate, has been walled shut for centuries; its present sealing is usually credited to the Ottoman sultan Suleiman the Magnificent in the 1540s. Ezekiel 43:1–5 and 44:1–2 describe the glory of the Lord entering the temple by the east gate and the gate then being shut because the Lord has entered by it.

### Q: Who else will be on the earth during the Millennial Kingdom? Will some people be surrendered to God and others not?

Israel will be in the land, but not only Israel. Daniel 7:12 says the other beasts had their dominion taken away but their lives prolonged for a season. The Gentile nations will remain on the earth, stripped of their authority, and they will have a choice. Some will be people who never made a choice for either side before the return. Some will have chosen God after he returned, too late to be gathered at the rapture. Everyone who took the mark of the beast will be dead.

### Q: Doesn't Scripture say those who take the mark will be tormented forever?

Yes (Revelation 14:10–11). Whether they are dead or in the lake of fire, the result is the same: they are not in the Millennial Kingdom. No one who is thrown into the lake of fire comes out of it. Satan is released at the end of the thousand years because he is chained in the abyss, not in the lake of fire (Revelation 20:1–3, 7). When he is finally thrown into the lake of fire, the beast and the false prophet are still there (Revelation 20:10).

### Q: If it's a thousand years, will people be born during that time?

Scripture says little directly about life during the Millennial Kingdom. The passages that describe it are brought together in [[i. The Thousand Years|Chapter 30]].
