> *"The rest of mankind who were not killed by these plagues still did not repent of the work of their hands."*
> — Revelation 9:20

---

## Overview

The seventh seal is opened, heaven goes silent, and seven angels are handed seven trumpets. What follows in Revelation 8 and 9 is the first movement of God's [[Glossary#Wrath of God|wrath]] on the earth: a burning mountain, a star named Wormwood, an army of locusts with the tails of scorpions, two hundred million riders. The pull is to go straight to the details and try to decode each one. The details matter, but they serve a design, and the design has to come first. The [[Glossary#Seven Trumpets|trumpet judgments]] have a specific target, a specific audience, and a specific order, and each of those says something about who God is. This session works through the first six trumpets with that design in view: wrath aimed at those whose choice is already made, displayed for those whose choice is not, and sequenced as an answer to what humanity has done with its Creator.

---

## 1. Recap: Sealed to Witness

Last session closed with the great multitude in white robes standing before the throne in heaven. Just before that, the 144,000 were sealed, and the timing of that sealing is the key to what follows. They were sealed so that they would survive. God's wrath is about to fall on the earth, and he wants the 144,000 alive on the far side of it as witnesses inside the [[Glossary#Millennial Kingdom|Millennial Kingdom]]. They have to come through this, and they have to see it.

The model for this is the Exodus. When the plagues fell on Egypt, God protected Israel from what was happening to the Egyptians, and Israel watched. The same pattern is repeating on a larger scale. What is about to happen is a testimony to the Jews — the 144,000 in particular, and the rest of the Jewish people alongside them — and a testimony to the Gentiles of the power of God, exactly as the plagues were in Egypt.

The scene, then, is this. The church has been [[Glossary#Rapture|raptured]] and taken to heaven. The sealed remnant remains on the earth. And God's wrath begins to fall in a series of seven trumpets.

---

## 2. A Target and an Audience

If this is God's wrath, the first question is who it is aimed at. The text supplies the answer through a repeated detail: a third. A third of the earth burns. A third of the sea turns to blood. A third of the rivers turn bitter. A third affected means two-thirds are not, and that proportion indicates a specific target.

The target is the center of the [[Glossary#Antichrist|Antichrist's]] kingdom, and that center is not Jerusalem. It is the Middle East. Take Israel and draw a half-moon around it, running from Turkey across to Iraq and Iran and back through Egypt and North Africa. These are the nations of Ezekiel and Daniel, the nations that surround Israel, and the region from which the ten kings come. The Antichrist is consolidating power there, and opposition is rising against him there. That is where the judgment concentrates.

Outside that region the picture is less clear. Scripture says little about what is happening in North America or elsewhere at this stage. What it does show is that those places are not co-located with the Antichrist's kingdom, at least in its initial phase, and that the judgments do not fall on them equally. That is deliberate. The rest of the earth is the audience.

The Exodus explains why. When God struck Egypt, Israel stood outside the blow and watched what happened to the Egyptians, and the watching built their faith, because they did not yet know who God was. The same is true of the Gentiles who remain after the church has been taken. They do not know who God is. Many may never have read the Bible. They are about to see his power the way Israel and Egypt saw it in the Exodus.

God is already working in this region in the present. Dreams are among the primary ways he is evangelizing the Middle East today, and the most common element across the accounts is a man in white who appears to the dreamer — a figure those who see him come to recognize as Jesus.

What does the Lord want from those outside the target? That they witness his power, and then repent and turn to him. That is the purpose behind everything that follows.

> **Key Principle:** God's wrath is aimed at those for whom it is too late and displayed for those for whom it is not. Pouring out wrath on the center of the Antichrist's kingdom is an act of mercy toward everyone watching.

## 3. The Seventh Seal and the Golden Censer — Revelation 8:1–5

*"When he opened the seventh seal, there was silence in heaven for about half an hour"* (Revelation 8:1). The silence is solemn, a held breath before what comes next. John sees the seven angels who stand before God, and to them are given seven trumpets.

Another angel, carrying a golden censer — a bowl for holding hot coals — comes and stands at the altar. He is given much incense to offer with the prayers of all the saints on the golden altar before the throne, and the smoke of the incense rises with those prayers before God. Then the angel fills the censer with fire from the altar and hurls it to the earth. Thunder, rumblings, flashes of lightning, and an earthquake follow. All of this sets the scene. The seven angels then prepare to sound their trumpets.

---

## 4. The First Trumpet — Fire on the Land — Revelation 8:7

The first angel sounds, and hail and fire mixed with blood are hurled down on the earth. A third of the earth is burned up, a third of the trees, and all the green grass.

The scale is enormous. A third of the earth is a very large region. But it is not the whole earth, and that distinction is the point. It is wide-scale destruction, not universal destruction: there is a target that receives the blow, and there is an audience God intends to see it.

---

## 5. The Second Trumpet — The Burning Mountain and the Sea — Revelation 8:8–9

The second angel sounds, and something like a huge mountain, all ablaze, is thrown into the sea. A third of the sea turns to blood, a third of the living creatures in the sea die, and a third of the ships are destroyed.

What are ships doing on the sea? Some could be naval vessels, but most ships are engaged in trade. The strike falls on the economy. The Mediterranean and the Red Sea, in the period of the Antichrist's kingdom, are pictured as heavily trafficked corridors — lots of goods moving back and forth, an economy that is booming. That commerce is what this trumpet breaks. As for the mountain itself, the text says only that it is something like a mountain. It could be an eruption. What it is cannot be known from the passage.

---

## 6. The Third Trumpet — Wormwood — Revelation 8:10–11

The third angel sounds, and a great star, blazing like a torch, falls from the sky on a third of the rivers and springs of water. The name of the star is [[Glossary#Wormwood|Wormwood]]. A third of the waters turn bitter, and many people die from the waters that have become bitter. A blazing object falling from the sky sounds like a meteorite or a missile, though the text does not say.

The Old Testament background is the Exodus again. Millions of people are crossing the wilderness, and in a dry land the most pressing need is water. Israel comes to water and tastes it, and it is bitter. God shows Moses a piece of wood; Moses throws it in, and the water is made sweet (Exodus 15:22–25). The third trumpet is that scene in reverse. Water that was sweetened is now embittered, and the outcome is worse than the original problem. It does not merely taste bad; it kills. Whatever the physical mechanism, nothing known on earth turns fresh water bitter and deadly in this way. It is a miracle.

---

## 7. The Fourth Trumpet — The Lights Struck — Revelation 8:12

*"A third of the sun was struck, a third of the moon, and a third of the stars, so that a third of them turned dark. A third of the day was without light, and also a third of the night."*

This is the most confusing passage of the six, and reading it literally shows why. If a third of the sun were destroyed, the earth would cease to exist; the balance of distance and heat between the earth and the sun is too delicate. A third of the moon destroyed would knock it out of its orbit. Stars are suns at enormous distances, and "a third of the stars" does not describe anything that could be done to them.

The next line changes the picture. Something is affecting how much light reaches the earth: a third of the day and a third of the night are without light. If the sun were simply dimmer, the whole day would be dim, not a third of it dark. Something is interrupting the light in a structured way. Two possibilities present themselves. Something could be blocking the sky, such as an eruption whose debris covers a portion of it. Or the earth's rotation could have changed, perhaps from an impact like the one in the earlier trumpets, so that the familiar twenty-four-hour day no longer holds. The text does not say which. Those watching from heaven will see it play out.

What the text makes clear is the effect on those living through it. The most stable pattern in human experience, the rhythm of day and night unchanged for thousands of years, is suddenly disrupted in ways no one can explain. The result is chaos.

---

## 8. The Eagle's Warning — Revelation 8:13

*"Woe! Woe! Woe to the inhabitants of the earth, because of the trumpet blasts about to be sounded by the other three angels!"*

An eagle flying in midair cries out these words. Whether it is a literal eagle or a symbolic one, the text does not settle; this is a vision. What the warning says is unmistakable. The first four trumpets were bad, and the last three will be worse. Three angels are still waiting.

The judgment behind the warning is aimed with precision. The Lord is targeting those who have already taken the mark of the beast, who are already inside the Antichrist's system, who have already chosen and hardened their hearts against God.

---

## 9. The Fifth Trumpet — A Fallen Star and an Open Abyss — Revelation 9:1–2

*"I saw a star that had fallen from the sky to the earth. The star was given the key to the shaft of the Abyss."*

The first question is what a star is. It cannot be an actual star. A star is a sun, and if one struck the earth there would be no earth left. An audience member identified the star as an angel, and the text supports it: a star that receives a key and opens a shaft is acting as a person.

The grammar carries the next clue. John does not write that he saw a star falling. He writes that he saw a star that *had* fallen — a perfect participle in Greek, describing a fall already completed. By the time the vision opens, the fall is in the past. If the star is an angel, this is an angel who has already fallen: a fallen angel. That fits the sequence the study has established. By the time of the trumpets, the Antichrist has been revealed, Satan has taken over, and the fallen angels have already been expelled from heaven. Revelation does not narrate everything in strict order; it goes back at points to fill in events, and the expulsion itself is told in chapter 12. *(The expulsion of the fallen angels from heaven will be explored when the study reaches Revelation 12.)* The picture is consistent with the world the study has been building since the [[Glossary#Divine Council|divine council]]: fallen spiritual beings are real, active, and answerable to God.

The key is the detail that matters most. Jesus holds the keys of death and Hades (Revelation 1:18). The key in this scene is his, and he is giving it to a fallen angel for a specific purpose: releasing the inhabitants of the abyss. Which angel receives it matters less than what the act shows. The Lord is sovereign and directing, and rebellious powers act only within the limits he sets. It is the same pattern as Ezekiel 38, where the Lord says he will put hooks in the jaws of Gog and pull him out (Ezekiel 38:4). The powers of rebellion are not free agents. The Lord is putting a box around what they can do.

---

## 10. The Abyss and Its Inhabitants

The [[Glossary#Abyss|abyss]] is a prison: a dark place, a place of pain and torment, a place of separation from God. The abyss, the lake of fire, hell, and Hades overlap in Scripture's imagery, and the concepts tend to run together, but the common thread in all of them is confinement and judgment.

Who is confined there now, and who would be there in this period? The [[Glossary#Nephilim|Nephilim]] — more precisely, the spirits of the Nephilim, which are [[Glossary#Demons|demons]]. Scripture is the first witness. When Jesus confronts the man with the legion inside him, the demons ask whether he has come to torment them before the appointed time (Matthew 8:29), and they beg not to be sent into the abyss (Luke 8:31). They know it exists, and they fear it. The Second Temple literature gives the same picture in more detail. 1 Enoch and Jubilees describe a portion of the demons confined and a portion left on the earth, and Jubilees 10 puts the proportions at nine-tenths imprisoned and one-tenth remaining. There are more in the abyss than on the earth.

That proportion matters because of what the fifth trumpet does with it. The shaft is about to be opened.

---

## 11. The Locust Army — Revelation 9:2–11

When the shaft opens, smoke rises like the smoke of a gigantic furnace, and the sun and sky are darkened by it. Out of the smoke come locusts, given power like that of scorpions.

A biblical plague of locusts is a known thing: a swarm arrives like a cloud that blocks the sun, lands on everything, and strips every green plant in sight. These are described as locusts, but they behave nothing like them. They are told not to harm the grass, the plants, or the trees — an instruction no insect could receive or obey. They are told to harm only the people who do not have the seal of God on their foreheads. Something that is told what to do, and does it, has a mind and a will and stands under authority. These are sentient beings, not insects.

They are permitted to torture but not to kill, and only for five months. The agony is like the sting of a scorpion when it strikes. *"During those days people will seek death but will not find it; they will long to die, but death will elude them"* (Revelation 9:6).

The description in verses 7–10 grows stranger. They look like horses prepared for battle. On their heads is something like crowns of gold. Their faces resemble human faces. Their hair is like women's hair, and their teeth are like lions' teeth. They wear breastplates that look like iron, and the sound of their wings is like the thundering of many horses and chariots rushing into battle. They have tails and stings like scorpions, and in their tails is the power to torment.

Over them stands a king: the angel of the abyss, whose name in Hebrew is [[Glossary#Abaddon|Abaddon]] and in Greek Apollyon, which means Destroyer. *"The first woe is past; two other woes are yet to come"* (Revelation 9:12).

A question hangs over this passage and has to wait until the sixth trumpet has been read: why would the Lord release a demonic army on the earth?

---

## 12. Who Is Sealed, and Why

The seal of God appears again here and does exactly what it was given to do. The locusts may harm only those who lack it. The 144,000 are untouched.

That raises questions that surfaced in the room. Are the 144,000 believers? And if they belong to God, why do they need protection? The answer lies in the timing. If the 144,000 were Christians at the return of the Lord, they would have been gathered to heaven with the church. They are not in heaven; they are on the earth. They are Jews who do not yet believe in Jesus at that moment, and that is precisely why the sealing is necessary. They have to be kept alive through the wrath. They are not saved initially; they come to faith before the Millennial Kingdom begins. Believers in Christ, by contrast, are not on the earth at this point at all. The church has already been gathered. *(The 144,000 will be revisited when the study reaches Revelation 14.)*

The region where the locusts operate also matters. Israel sits physically at the center of the Antichrist's domain, so Israelites who lack the seal are exposed to the torment along with everyone else in the region. The seal protects the 144,000 specifically. It does not shield the nation as a whole.

Some found the sealing of a specific number hard to accept, as though certain people were chosen and others passed over. From a human standpoint that can sound unjust. But God knows from the beginning of time who will be written in the Book of Life. The objection rests on a human view of the situation; God works from complete knowledge of how the story ends. The same objection could be raised over the Egyptians who suffered under the plagues. Justice, in the full sense, will not exist on this earth until the Lord comes.

## 13. The Sixth Trumpet — The Army of Two Hundred Million — Revelation 9:13–19

The sixth angel sounds, and a voice comes from the horns of the golden altar before God: *"Release the four angels who are bound at the great river Euphrates"* (Revelation 9:14). The four angels had been kept ready for this very hour and day and month and year, and they are released to kill a third of mankind. Four angels lead, but the army itself numbers two hundred million mounted troops.

The riders and horses wear breastplates of fiery red, dark blue, and sulfur yellow. The horses' heads resemble the heads of lions, and fire, smoke, and sulfur pour from their mouths. A third of mankind is killed by these three plagues. Their power is in their mouths and in their tails, and their tails are like snakes with heads, with which they inflict injury.

Are these real horses? Are they ridden by real men? The text answers within itself. Men riding creatures that breathe fire and sulfur would be dead. These are described as horses, but with features no horse has: lion heads, fire, snake-headed tails. They are a second supernatural army. The first torments and cannot kill; this one kills. Both consist of condemned beings, supernatural beings who have rebelled against God, and both are released on the earth by the Lord.

---

## 14. Half the World

The scale can be measured. By the fourth seal, a fourth of mankind had been killed (Revelation 6:8). Using the same rough figure of eight billion people, that is two billion dead and six billion remaining. The sixth trumpet kills a third of those six billion — two billion more — leaving four billion. Half the earth's population is gone from these two events alone, and other deaths in the sequence, such as those caused by the bitter waters of the third trumpet, come on top of that.

---

## 15. The Rest of Mankind — Revelation 9:20–21

*"The rest of mankind who were not killed by these plagues still did not repent of the work of their hands; they did not stop worshiping demons, and idols of gold, silver, bronze, stone and wood — idols that cannot see or hear or walk. Nor did they repent of their murders, their magic arts, their sexual immorality or their thefts."*

The passage is built to make this point. Those who survived, including those outside the target region who watched, saw all of it. They did not repent.

The text records this because it shows what God's intent has been and what the response was. God wants repentance. As long as a person is alive, repentance remains possible; the last opportunity for any individual is the moment of death. He is willing to put people through hell on the earth to bring them to it. And even that does not move the majority.

---

## 16. Giving Them What They Worship

This brings the question the fifth trumpet raised to its answer: why would a loving God release fallen angels and demons to torture and kill human beings? The answer is in verse 20. Look at who the survivors are worshiping. They are worshiping demons. God is giving them exactly what they chose.

He is giving them a taste of hell on earth before they are cast into it permanently. That is not cruelty. Satan has never been overt about what he is. He has worked through deception, persuading people that spiritual beings are guides and helpers, or that they themselves are gods. The Lord is now stripping that away, and he does it in two steps. When the Antichrist stands in the temple, the lawless one is revealed (2 Thessalonians 2:8), stripped of every lie and seen for what he is. Now the demonic armies and the angels leading them are shown for what they are, too. This is not a spirit guide arriving with an offer to help through the spiritual realm and fulfill your desires. These beings torture and kill. The gloves are off. The facade has been removed, and humanity is being shown who the enemy really is.

For those who make it through, the next event is the Millennial Kingdom. They will have the chance to live on the earth under the kingship of Jesus Christ, to see him on the throne for a thousand years, and, if still alive, to repent and receive him. A loving God is showing people hell before they are sent to it for eternity, so that some might turn.

> **Key Principle:** Judgment is also revelation. God is not only punishing what people have worshiped; he is exposing it, so that anyone who can still turn knows exactly what they are turning from.

## 17. The Order of the Trumpets — Romans 1

The question of fairness surfaced in the room more than once, and Romans 1 is the answer.

*"The wrath of God is being revealed from heaven against all the godlessness and wickedness of people, who suppress the truth by their wickedness, since what may be known about God is plain to them, because God has made it plain to them"* (Romans 1:18–19). Since the creation of the world, God's invisible qualities — his eternal power and divine nature — have been clearly seen, understood from what has been made, so that people are without excuse. Although they knew God, they neither glorified him as God nor gave thanks to him. Their thinking became futile, their foolish hearts were darkened, and though they claimed to be wise, they became fools and exchanged the glory of the immortal God for images (Romans 1:20–23). Paul then traces the perversion that follows from the rejection.

With that in view, the order of the trumpets reads differently. What does God strike first? The trees and the green things. Then the sea and everything in it. Then the rivers and springs. Then the sun, moon, and stars. He strikes creation. Why? Because creation is what people refused to thank him for. They did not worship him as Creator or give thanks to him. So he takes away the gifts they took for granted: if they will not thank him for what he made, they will live without it. Only after the gifts of creation have been struck does he release the demons and fallen angels they have been worshiping. First the gifts they ignored are removed; then the beings they served are given to them.

The people in view were not worshiping creation itself. They were not worshiping the trees and the rivers. They were failing to worship the God who made them, and worshiping demons instead. The sequence moves from the gifts they refused to acknowledge to the beings they chose.

> **Key Principle:** The trumpets follow a deliberate strategy: a target, an audience, and an order. Creation is withdrawn first from those who would not thank the Creator, and then the demons they worshiped are revealed. There is a rhyme and a reason for what he does, the sequence he does it in, and to whom.

## 18. Why the Detail? Knowing God, Not Only the Plan

Why did the Lord put so much detail in the Bible? So that we could know him. Not only so that we could understand his plan, but so that we could understand him and know what he is going to do. Reading Revelation as a puzzle of events misses the point. The point is to see him revealed in his Word, and it is not possible to do that honestly and not see him differently.

Once the picture is clear, there is rest in it. He has it covered. What is asked is not that anyone fix this, solve that, or hold the world together. What is asked is to be about his kingdom and bring as many people into it as possible.

---

## 19. One Choice, Two Kinds of Revelation

Stripped down, life on this earth comes down to one choice. From the widest strategic view, this life is a test, and the test is a decision about the Lord. Everything turns on how a person looks at him. Does the person acknowledge him as Creator? If so, that person will want to know more about him, and will search him out. If not, the person is separated.

Two kinds of revelation make that search possible. [[Glossary#General Revelation|General revelation]] is creation itself: everything around us points to a designer and a creator, and this is why people are without excuse (Romans 1:20). Specific revelation comes in the form of words. General revelation is where the search begins; the Word is where it is answered.

The scale of the choice is worth keeping in view. This life, less than a hundred years, is a blip when set against the eternity that follows. None of the promises God has given were designed to be fully delivered on this earth in this lifetime. There are things that come from a relationship with God, and things experienced along the way, but no one is meant to go through this life without pain, suffering, or sickness. Sin is the reason. The promises are fully fulfilled in the next life. Those who expect no pain and full justice on this side of the equation are guaranteed to experience pain, disappointment, and frustration. That frustration will be aimed at God, not because he deserves it, but because they do not understand him or his plan.

> **Key Principle:** The promises of God were never designed to be fully delivered on this side of eternity. Expecting them here guarantees disappointment aimed at the one who made them.

## 20. The Antichrist Operates in the Shadows

A question raised near the end of the session: is what is happening now, on a small scale, the groundwork for the Antichrist to arrive as a savior? Yes. He arrives as a conquering hero, not as an obvious villain. Peace has not been brought to the Middle East in two thousand years, and he will bring it. He will also allow the Jews to rebuild the temple. Hostility toward Israel is already rising, including in places, such as the United States, that have treated Israel as an ally.

The four events Jesus grouped as [[Glossary#Birth Pains (Eschatological)|birth pains]] in Matthew 24 — the rise of a deceiver, wars and rumors of wars, conflict between nations with its economic fallout, and natural disasters — have been occurring periodically for two thousand years. What makes them birth pains is that they intensify and come closer together. Their presence now is expected.

How will he be recognized? He will not announce himself. He operates in the shadows, as a power broker rather than an obvious king, and there will be no moment when someone declares that this is the date or this is the man. What will be unmistakable is the effect of the covenant with many: peace, and with peace, Israel resuming sacrifices on the Temple Mount. That is the sure sign that the covenant has been confirmed. It begins the [[Glossary#Seventy Weeks (Seventy Sevens)|seventieth week]], and at its midpoint, three and a half years later, the shadows end and he is revealed.

He is human, not a robot, and ultimately human, though with demonic influence. Whether he has special capabilities is speculation the text does not invite.
