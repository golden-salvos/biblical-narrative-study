## Babylon in the Prophets

Revelation 17–18 is built almost entirely out of the Old Testament's oracles against Babylon, with one against Tyre. Nearly every line has a source.

The prostitute *"seated on many waters"* (Revelation 17:1) comes from Jeremiah's address to historical Babylon: *"O you who dwell by many waters, rich in treasures, your end has come"* (Jeremiah 51:13). The waters there were literal. Babylon sat on the Euphrates and was laced with canals. Revelation reinterprets them as peoples and nations (17:15). The golden cup comes from the next chapter of the same book: *"Babylon was a golden cup in the Lord's hand, making all the earth drunken"* (Jeremiah 51:7). The cry *"Fallen, fallen is Babylon"* (18:2) is Isaiah 21:9. The ruined city as a haunt of wild animals and unclean creatures (18:2) echoes Isaiah 13:19–22 and Jeremiah 50:39. *"Come out of her, my people"* (18:4) repeats Jeremiah 51:45, and *"repay her double"* (18:6) draws on Jeremiah 50:29. Her boast, *"I sit as a queen, I am no widow, and mourning I shall never see"* (18:7), is taken from Isaiah 47:7–8, where Babylon says, *"I shall not sit as a widow or know the loss of children."* Isaiah answers that both will come upon her *"in a moment, in one day"* (Isaiah 47:9), which becomes Revelation's *"in a single day"* and *"in a single hour."*

The millstone (18:21) reenacts a scene from Jeremiah's own ministry. Jeremiah wrote the disasters coming on Babylon in a book and told Seraiah to read it in Babylon, then tie a stone to it and throw it into the Euphrates, saying, *"Thus shall Babylon sink, to rise no more"* (Jeremiah 51:63–64). The silencing of music, craftsmen, the mill, the lamp, and the voice of bridegroom and bride (18:22–23) reverses Jeremiah 25:10, where God pronounces the same silence over Judah.

The lament of the kings, merchants, and sailors is modeled on Ezekiel 26–28, the dirge over Tyre, the great maritime trading city of the Phoenicians. Ezekiel 27 is a long inventory of Tyre's trading partners and goods: silver, iron, tin, and lead, slaves and bronze, horses and mules, ivory, fine linen, coral, wheat, honey, oil, wine, wool, spices, and precious stones. When Tyre falls, *"the mariners and all the pilots of the sea"* stand on the shore, cast dust on their heads, and cry, *"Who was ever destroyed like Tyre in the midst of the sea?"* (Ezekiel 27:29–32). Revelation's sailors cry, *"What city was like the great city?"* (18:18). Babylon's lament is a merchant city's lament. It is one reason the text's picture of a seaport is so consistent.

## The Cargo List and the Trade of Solomon

The twenty-nine items in Revelation 18:12–13 run from precious metals and stones, through luxury textiles, fine woods and ivory, building materials, spices and aromatics, foodstuffs and livestock, to horses, chariots, and finally *"slaves, that is, human souls."* The final phrase echoes Ezekiel 27:13, where Tyre's partners *"exchanged human beings"* for her wares. It is the only item the text pauses to explain.

The list is a catalog of ancient luxury trade. Historians note that nearly every item on it was a prized import into first-century Rome, which is one reason many interpreters read Babylon as Rome. In this study's reading, its closest biblical parallel is the reign of Solomon. Solomon *"built a fleet of ships at Ezion-geber, which is near Eloth on the shore of the Red Sea, in the land of Edom"* (1 Kings 9:26). Working with the sailors of Hiram of Tyre, the fleet brought gold from Ophir (9:27–28), and every three years *"the fleet of ships of Tarshish used to come bringing gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks"* (10:22). Solomon also traded horses and chariots from Egypt and Kue (10:28–29), and the queen of Sheba, from southern Arabia, brought spices, gold, and precious stones in quantities never seen again (10:10). Ezion-geber lay near modern Eilat and Aqaba, at the head of the Gulf of Aqaba, the northeastern arm of the Red Sea. It is the same coastline where Saudi Arabia is now building NEOM.

## Interpretations of Babylon

The identity of Babylon the Great is one of the most debated questions in Revelation, and five main answers have been given.

**Rome.** The oldest and most widely held reading. The seven heads are *"seven mountains on which the woman is seated"* (17:9), and Rome was famous as the city of seven hills. Rome was the great city that ruled the kings of the earth in John's day, and it had shed the blood of the saints under Nero. Peter appears to use "Babylon" as a code name for Rome (1 Peter 5:13), and Jewish writings after AD 70 did the same (4 Ezra, 2 Baruch, Sibylline Oracles 5). Since the Reformation, some Protestant interpreters have extended this reading to the Roman Catholic Church.

**Jerusalem.** Some interpreters, especially those who date Revelation before AD 70, identify the prostitute as apostate Jerusalem, on the strength of the prophets' charge that Jerusalem had become a harlot (Isaiah 1:21; Ezekiel 16) and Revelation 11:8's description of *"the great city... where their Lord was crucified."*

**A rebuilt Babylon in Iraq.** Some futurists read the name literally and expect the ancient city on the Euphrates to be rebuilt. Saddam Hussein's reconstruction of parts of the site in the 1980s drew attention to the idea.

**A symbolic world system.** Many modern commentators take Babylon as the archetype of every godless economic and political order, the city of man set against the city of God, embodied in Rome in John's day and in other powers since.

**Mecca or Saudi Arabia.** A smaller group of modern interpreters, among them those who identify the Antichrist's kingdom as a revived Islamic caliphate, argue that Babylon must sit at the religious and economic center of that kingdom, and point to Saudi Arabia. This is the reading this study develops. Its strongest supports are the requirement that Babylon be the center of the Antichrist's kingdom, the global reach of Saudi wealth, and the Red Sea setting of Solomon's trade. Its chief difficulty is that neither Mecca nor Medina is a seaport, which is why the study looks to the kingdom's Red Sea coast.

### "Seven Mountains"

The Roman reading depends heavily on the seven mountains. But the angel immediately adds that the heads *"are also seven kings"* (17:10), and in prophetic language a mountain is a common image for a kingdom. The stone that destroys the statue in Daniel *"became a great mountain and filled the whole earth"* (Daniel 2:35), which is the kingdom God sets up (2:44). Jeremiah calls historical Babylon a *"destroying mountain"* that God will make *"a burnt mountain"* (Jeremiah 51:25). Isaiah 2:2 describes the kingdom of the Lord as *"the mountain of the house of the Lord"* raised above the hills. Read that way, the seven mountains and the seven kings are a single statement: seven kingdoms, each with its king.

## "Was, and Is Not, and Is to Come"

The beast's description, *"was and is not and is to come"* (17:8), is a deliberate parody of God's own title in Revelation, *"who is and who was and who is to come"* (1:4, 8; 4:8). God's existence is unbroken. The beast's has a gap in it: it was, it ceased to be, and it returns. It then *"goes to destruction"* (17:8, 11). The pattern repeats the parody of Revelation 13, where one head of the beast had *"a mortal wound"* that was healed (13:3, 12, 14), a counterfeit of the death and resurrection of the Lamb who *"was slain"* and lives (5:6).

Ancient readers knew a story that fit the pattern: the *Nero redivivus* legend. After Nero's suicide in AD 68, rumors spread that he had not really died or would return from the east, and several impostors claiming to be Nero appeared in the following decades. Interpreters who identify the beast with Rome often read 17:8–11 against this legend. The study reads the same pattern at the level of a kingdom rather than a single ruler: a caliphate that existed, was abolished, and is restored.

## The Ten Kings and the Monarchies of the Middle East

The phrase *"for one hour"* (17:12) signals a brief and coordinated reign. The same expression marks the suddenness of Babylon's fall in 18:10, 17, and 19.

The region the study identifies as the Antichrist's kingdom is one of the few places left on earth where hereditary kingship still carries real power. Saudi Arabia is an absolute monarchy ruled by the house of Saud. Jordan, Morocco, Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, and the seven emirates of the UAE are all ruled by royal families, several with near-absolute authority. Egypt is a republic governed since 2013 by Abdel Fattah el-Sisi, the general who came to power when the military removed the Muslim Brotherhood's elected president, Mohamed Morsi. Turkey has been a presidential republic since a 2017 referendum abolished the office of prime minister; Recep Tayyip Erdogan has led the country since 2003, first as prime minister and then as president. Iran is a theocratic republic under a Supreme Leader. Ten rulers of the region exercising royal authority at once would require political change, but not as much as it would anywhere else in the world.

## The Caliphate, 632–1924

The word *khalifa* means "successor," and the caliph was the successor of Muhammad as ruler of the Muslim community, a role that was at once political and religious. After Muhammad's death in 632, the first four caliphs, the Rashidun, ruled from Medina (632–661), and within a generation Arab armies had conquered Syria, Egypt, Iraq, and Persia. Jerusalem surrendered to the caliph Umar around 637–638. The Umayyad caliphate (661–750), ruling from Damascus, extended Islamic rule across North Africa and into Spain in 711, until its advance into Francia was turned back at the Battle of Tours in 732. The Umayyad caliph Abd al-Malik built the Dome of the Rock in 691–692. The Abbasid caliphate (750–1258) ruled from Baghdad until the Mongols destroyed the city; a shadow Abbasid line continued in Cairo under the Mamluks. In 1517 the Ottomans conquered Egypt, and the Ottoman sultans came to claim the title of caliph.

The Ottoman Empire sided with Germany in the First World War and was dismembered after its defeat. The Turkish Grand National Assembly abolished the sultanate in November 1922. The Republic of Turkey was proclaimed in October 1923 with Mustafa Kemal (later Atatürk) as its president. On 3 March 1924, the Assembly abolished the caliphate itself and exiled the last caliph, Abdülmecid II, and the Ottoman dynasty. Several attempts to revive the office elsewhere in the 1920s failed. In 2014 the terrorist group ISIS proclaimed a caliphate in parts of Iraq and Syria; it was rejected by nearly every Muslim authority and had lost its territory by 2019.

In Turkey, the idea of a restored caliphate surfaces periodically around Erdogan's government. In 2020, after Hagia Sophia was reconverted into a mosque, the pro-government magazine *Gerçek Hayat* called on the country to prepare for the caliphate's return; the ruling party publicly rejected the call and said Turkey would remain a secular republic. Erdogan has described the Republic as a continuation of the Ottoman Empire, and the centenary of the caliphate's abolition in March 2024 drew renewed commentary on his ambitions.

## Jerusalem, the Crusades, and the Temple Mount

Jerusalem was under Roman and then Byzantine Christian rule from AD 70 until the Arab conquest in the 630s. From then until the British captured the city in December 1917, it was ruled almost continuously by Muslim powers: the caliphates, the Fatimids, the Seljuks, the Ayyubids, the Mamluks, and from 1517 the Ottomans.

The exception was the Crusades. In 1095, Pope Urban II called on the knights of Europe to aid the Byzantine Empire and recover the Holy Land. The First Crusade captured Jerusalem in July 1099 and established the Latin Kingdom of Jerusalem. Saladin retook the city in 1187. The Third Crusade (1189–1192), led by Richard the Lionheart of England, Philip II of France, and Frederick Barbarossa of the Holy Roman Empire, recovered much of the coast but not Jerusalem. The city returned briefly to Christian control by treaty from 1229 to 1244 and was lost for good thereafter. The Latin East's last major stronghold, Acre, fell in 1291.

In Islam, the Temple Mount is the Haram al-Sharif, "the Noble Sanctuary." The Quran opens its seventeenth surah: *"Glory be to Him who carried His servant by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque (al-Masjid al-Aqsa)."* Islamic tradition identifies the Sacred Mosque with Mecca and the Farthest Mosque with Jerusalem. From the rock on the Mount, Muhammad is said to have ascended through the heavens (the *Mi'raj*). Jerusalem was also the first direction of Muslim prayer before it was changed to Mecca. The Dome of the Rock is a shrine built over the rock, which Jewish tradition calls the Foundation Stone and associates with Mount Moriah (2 Chronicles 3:1). Its interior inscriptions include Quranic verses rejecting the divinity of Christ. The al-Aqsa Mosque stands at the southern end of the platform.

Islamic tradition holds that the son Abraham was commanded to sacrifice was Ishmael, and generally places the sacrifice at Mina, near Mecca. The event is commemorated each year at the Hajj and in the festival of Eid al-Adha, the "Feast of the Sacrifice."

The modern arrangement dates from 1967. When Israel took the Old City from Jordan in the Six-Day War, Defense Minister Moshe Dayan left day-to-day administration of the Mount to the Islamic Waqf, under the custodianship of the Jordanian king. Israel kept security control. Jews could visit but not pray. That arrangement, known as the status quo, has eroded steadily since Itamar Ben-Gvir became National Security Minister in 2022. In January 2026, police for the first time allowed visitors to bring printed prayer pages onto the Mount. By mid-2026, Jewish prayer was being permitted de facto on the eastern side of the platform while arrests continued for prayer elsewhere on it, and on August 16, 2026, Jewish men were allowed to bring full prayer books onto the Mount for the first time, in what was described as an initial experiment. Netanyahu's office has repeatedly stated that Israel's policy of maintaining the status quo has not changed. Jordan's role is formalized in Article 9 of the 1994 Israel–Jordan peace treaty, in which Israel undertook to "respect the present special role" of Jordan in the Muslim holy shrines of Jerusalem.

The Crusades are often remembered today as unprovoked Christian aggression, but their immediate cause was defensive. By the 1090s, Islamic armies had conquered the Christian heartlands of Syria, Palestine, Egypt, and North Africa over the preceding four and a half centuries. After the Seljuk Turks routed the Byzantine army at Manzikert in 1071 and overran most of Anatolia, the Byzantine emperor Alexios I appealed to the West for help. Urban II's call at Clermont answered that appeal and added the recovery of Jerusalem as the goal. The Crusades also produced real atrocities, including the massacre that followed the capture of Jerusalem in 1099 and the sack of Christian Constantinople in 1204.

## Arabs, Ishmael, and Islam

Genesis promises that Ishmael will become *"a great nation"* (Genesis 21:18) and the father of twelve princes (17:20; 25:12–16). His sons, among them Nebaioth, Kedar, and Tema, are associated with tribes of northern Arabia, and Arab genealogical tradition traces the northern Arabs, including Muhammad's tribe, the Quraysh, to Ishmael. The Quran presents Abraham and Ishmael together raising the foundations of the Kaaba in Mecca (Surah 2:127).

Arab identity and Islam overlap but are not the same. About one in five of the world's roughly two billion Muslims is Arab. The most populous Muslim-majority country is Indonesia, followed by Pakistan, India's Muslim population, and Bangladesh. Turkey is Turkic and Iran is Persian. Arab Christian communities, among them Egypt's Copts and the churches of Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, and Iraq, predate Islam.

## Sunni and Shia

The division goes back to the succession after Muhammad. The majority accepted the leadership of the caliphs chosen from among his companions, beginning with Abu Bakr. A minority, the *Shi'at Ali* ("party of Ali"), held that leadership belonged to Muhammad's cousin and son-in-law Ali and his descendants. The breach hardened with the killing of Ali's son Husayn at Karbala in 680. Today Sunnis are roughly 85 to 90 percent of the Muslim world. Shia Muslims are the majority in Iran, Iraq, and Bahrain and form large communities in Lebanon, Yemen, and elsewhere. Most Shia belong to the Twelver branch, which holds that the twelfth Imam went into hiding in the ninth century and will return as the Mahdi. Iran has been the state center of Shia power since the Safavids made Twelver Shi'ism the state religion around 1501, and the Islamic Republic established after the 1979 revolution has positioned itself as the rival of Saudi Arabia for leadership of the Muslim world.

## The Petrodollar

The postwar monetary system established at Bretton Woods in 1944 pegged the world's currencies to the dollar and the dollar to gold at $35 an ounce. On 15 August 1971, facing a run on American gold reserves, President Nixon suspended the dollar's convertibility into gold. By 1973 the major currencies were floating. After the 1973 Arab oil embargo, the United States and Saudi Arabia agreed in 1974 to a set of economic and security arrangements, negotiated by Treasury Secretary William Simon under Secretary of State Henry Kissinger. Under them, Saudi oil continued to be priced in dollars and Saudi surplus revenues were recycled into U.S. Treasury securities, in a partnership that included American security support for the kingdom. Because OPEC's oil was priced in dollars, every oil-importing nation needed dollar reserves. This is the arrangement popularly known as the petrodollar.

Its erosion is real but gradual. After Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the United States and its allies froze roughly $300 billion of Russian central bank reserves and cut major Russian banks off from the SWIFT messaging network. Central banks then bought gold at a record pace. Saudi Arabia has discussed pricing some oil sales to China in yuan and took part in the BIS-backed mBridge digital-currency project before withdrawing from it. Most of the world's oil trade is still settled in dollars.

## Saudi Influence: What Is Documented

Several of the session's claims about Saudi reach are matters of public record.

**Media and entertainment.** Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, one of the world's largest sovereign wealth funds, has taken a series of stakes in Western media and entertainment. They include a minority stake in Penske Media (owner of *Variety* and *Rolling Stone*) in 2018, investments in live entertainment and sports, and in 2026 a reported $10 billion share of the Gulf financing behind Paramount Skydance's acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, held as a non-voting stake.

**Land.** Saudi and other Gulf investors own American farmland and real estate. The best-known case is Fondomonte, a subsidiary of the Saudi dairy company Almarai, which grew water-intensive alfalfa in the Arizona desert for export to Saudi Arabia, where such farming had been restricted to conserve groundwater. In 2023 Arizona terminated Fondomonte's leases on state land.

**Universities.** U.S. Department of Education disclosure data rank Saudi Arabia among the five largest foreign funders of American higher education since reporting began, with roughly $4 billion reported. Qatar is the largest.

**Religious funding.** Over several decades, Saudi Arabia spent heavily on mosques, schools, and religious literature abroad, exporting its Wahhabi interpretation of Islam. Since about 2017, Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman has publicly distanced the kingdom from parts of that legacy.

**September 11.** Fifteen of the nineteen hijackers were Saudi nationals. The four pilots were from Egypt, the UAE, Lebanon, and Saudi Arabia. Osama bin Laden was the son of Mohammed bin Laden, a Yemeni-born construction magnate whose company built much of modern Saudi Arabia, and he was stripped of Saudi citizenship in 1994. His organization grew out of the networks of foreign fighters who joined the Afghan mujahideen against the Soviet Union in the 1980s. The United States, with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan, funded and armed the Afghan mujahideen in that war. The twenty-eight-page section of the 2002 congressional joint inquiry dealing with possible Saudi government support for the hijackers remained classified until July 2016.

**Human trafficking.** In the State Department's annual Trafficking in Persons Report, Saudi Arabia was downgraded to Tier 3, the lowest ranking, in 2019, alongside China, Iran, North Korea, and Russia. The report cited widespread abuse of the foreign workers bound to employers under the kingdom's sponsorship (*kafala*) system. Saudi Arabia was raised to the Tier 2 Watch List in 2020 and to Tier 2 in 2021, and it remained there in the 2025 report. In July 2013, Princess Meshael Alayban, a wife of a Saudi prince, was arrested in Irvine, California, and charged with human trafficking. Prosecutors said she had brought a Kenyan domestic worker to the United States, held her passport, confined her, and paid her about $220 a month. Four Filipina workers in the household were also interviewed as possible victims. She was released on $5 million bail, and the charges were dismissed later that year. Members of the royal family do not automatically hold diplomatic immunity, but their wealth, legal resources, and the diplomatic weight of the kingdom have meant that such cases rarely end in convictions. The session's claim that the royal family runs the largest trafficking and sex ring in the world is the study's own assessment; no public investigation has established it.

**Islamic law in the United States.** Islamic arbitration and mediation bodies operate in several American cities, handling disputes among Muslims, chiefly marriage, divorce, custody, and inheritance. The best known, the Islamic Tribunal in the Dallas–Irving area, was founded in 2014 and was reported in 2025 to be hearing roughly 300 cases a year. Like Jewish rabbinical courts (*beit din*) and Christian conciliation panels, such bodies are legal only as voluntary arbitration: their decisions bind no one unless a civil court confirms them, and a court will refuse to enforce a ruling that violates U.S. or state law or public policy. Critics argue that the voluntariness is often nominal for women and recent immigrants under community pressure, and that the proceedings have no transcripts and no appeal. In November 2025, Texas Governor Greg Abbott called for law-enforcement investigations of the Dallas tribunal as what he described as a rival court system, and in April 2026 Attorney General Ken Paxton opened an investigation; the tribunal maintains it is a voluntary, non-binding mediation service. Several states, among them Oklahoma, Kansas, Tennessee, Louisiana, Arizona, Alabama, and Texas, have passed laws barring their courts from applying foreign or religious law that conflicts with constitutional rights.

**Prisons.** Islam has long been one of the most common religions of conversion inside American prisons, and prison chaplains have reported active proselytizing.

## The Mecca Joint Defense Agreement

In September 2025, Saudi Arabia and Pakistan signed a Strategic Mutual Defense Agreement, the first formal defense pact between Saudi Arabia and a nuclear-armed state. On 7 August 2026, in Mecca, Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, President Erdogan, and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif signed a trilateral agreement extending that commitment to Turkey: an attack on any one of the three is treated as an attack on all. The three governments described it as collective deterrence rather than a military bloc, and said it was not directed at any specific country. It joins the Arab world's largest economy and the custodian of Islam's two holiest cities, the Muslim world's only nuclear power, and a NATO member with the alliance's second-largest army. It was signed in the middle of the United States–Israel war on Iran, during which Saudi oil infrastructure came under attack. Saudi Arabia stated that the agreement does not affect its existing partnerships, including with the United States.

Egypt is not a party. Relations between Egypt and Turkey broke down after the Egyptian military removed the Muslim Brotherhood government in 2013, and Erdogan, a supporter of the Brotherhood, called Sisi a tyrant. The two countries restored full diplomatic relations in 2023, and Erdogan visited Cairo in 2024.

## Iran and the Strait of Hormuz

The Strait of Hormuz, between Iran and Oman, carried roughly a fifth of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas before the current war. Since the United States and Israel went to war with Iran in late February 2026, Iran has kept the strait largely closed or restricted, routing permitted traffic along corridors it designates. It has used control of the strait as its main leverage in negotiations, while the United States has blockaded Iranian ports. The strait has become the central sticking point in efforts to end the war. The Red Sea has its own chokepoint at its southern end, the Bab el-Mandeb, where Yemen's Iranian-backed Houthi movement has attacked shipping since late 2023.

## NEOM, Oxagon, and Jeddah

NEOM was announced by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman in 2017 as the centerpiece of Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 plan: a development zone of some 26,500 square kilometers in the kingdom's northwest, on the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aqaba. Its regions included The Line, a 170-kilometer linear city; Trojena, a mountain ski resort; Sindalah, a luxury island; and Oxagon, an octagonal port and industrial city partly built out over the water. After years of cost overruns, the project has been repeatedly cut back. The Line was reduced to an initial phase of a few kilometers and in 2026 was put on hold until after 2030, and Trojena lost the 2029 Asian Winter Games. Investment has been redirected toward Oxagon, with its Red Sea port, green hydrogen production, and large AI data-center developments.

Jeddah, on the Red Sea about forty-five miles from Mecca, has long been the kingdom's principal port, its commercial capital, and the gateway for pilgrims arriving for the Hajj. The Jeddah Tower, designed to exceed one kilometer and become the tallest building in the world, resumed construction in January 2025 after a long halt and has since passed the height of the Petronas Towers. Completion is targeted for 2028. The tallest building in Mecca, the Abraj Al-Bait clock tower, overlooks the Grand Mosque.

## The King of the South

In Daniel 11:40–43, at "the time of the end," the king of the South attacks the king of the North, who responds by sweeping through the lands *"like a flood,"* entering *"the glorious land,"* and stretching out his hand against the countries: *"the land of Egypt shall not escape. He shall become ruler of the treasures of gold and of silver, and all the precious things of Egypt, and the Libyans and the Cushites shall follow in his train."* Edom, Moab, and the main part of the Ammonites, the lands of modern Jordan, escape his hand (11:41). Earlier in the chapter, the king of the South is consistently the Ptolemaic dynasty of Egypt. The study reads the end-time king of the South as Egypt again, the first nation to make war on the Antichrist.
