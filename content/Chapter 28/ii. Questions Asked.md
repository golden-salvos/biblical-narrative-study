### Q: What are the "many waters" the prostitute is seated on?

The angel answers it directly in Revelation 17:15: *"The waters that you saw, where the prostitute is seated, are peoples and multitudes and nations and languages."* The waters are every people on earth. The image means her influence reaches the entire planet. Since no queen holds royal authority over the whole world, that dominion has to be something other than a throne, and Revelation 18 shows what it is: commerce, wealth, and the kings and merchants who have grown rich alongside her.

### Q: Are the ten horns kingdoms, like the seven heads?

No. The seven heads are kingdoms, the empires spread across history that Satan has used to try to destroy Israel. The ten horns are kings (Revelation 17:12), and they exist together at one time and place, in the future. When those ten rulers arise in the Middle East, the little horn, the Antichrist, rises among them, subdues three (Daniel 7:8, 24), and the rest hand him their power. He becomes the sole ruler over all of it.

### Q: Are the seven heads the seven kingdoms that are left after the ten?

No. The seven heads come first and span all of history. They are the empires Satan has used to try to destroy Israel: Egypt, Assyria, Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome, and the Islamic caliphate. The ten horns come at the end, as ten kings who arise together within the last kingdom. The Antichrist's kingdom is simply the last in that line. The seventh head is Islam, and the eighth, *the beast that was and is not*, is also Islam: the caliphate abolished in 1924 and revived (Revelation 17:11).

### Q: Is Satan driving the ten kings that become one, or the beast, or both?

Both. Satan is driving the ten kings who come together into one, and he is driving the beast they hand their power to. There is some nuance in how he works through each, but for the purposes of this session, both. That is also why the eighth *belongs to the seven*: the common denominator among all eight is who is running them. The Antichrist cannot be behind all of them, because he is a man. Satan is behind every one.

### Q: Will the ten kings take power by military force?

The text points elsewhere. The ten kings *have not yet received royal power, but they are to receive authority as kings* (Revelation 17:12). Military power is power taken by force. Royal power is inherited through a family line. These are kings, not generals, presidents, or prime ministers, which fits a region where monarchies such as Saudi Arabia and Jordan still rule.

### Q: Which three kings does the Antichrist conquer?

Egypt, the king of the South, and the nations on either side of it: Libya to the west and Sudan, biblical Cush, to the south. Daniel 11:42–43 says the land of Egypt will not escape him, *"and the Libyans and the Cushites shall follow in his train."* Ezekiel 38:5 names Cush and Put (Libya) among the forces in the end-time coalition.

### Q: Was Muhammad a real historical person?

Yes. The claim that Muhammad never existed circulates online, but it is not a serious historical position. Muhammad is a documented historical figure, attested both archaeologically and in written sources, just as Jesus is. He was born in Mecca around AD 570 and died in Medina in 632.

### Q: When you say "Muslims," do you mean Arabs?

They are not the same thing. Arabs are a people, a bloodline descended in biblical terms from Ishmael. Islam is a religion. Most of the Arab world follows Islam, but there are Arab Christians, and most of the world's Muslims are not Arab. The largest Muslim-majority country by population is Indonesia. Iran is overwhelmingly Muslim but ethnically Persian, not Arab.

### Q: Why is the Temple Mount the third holiest site in Islam?

An audience member answered that it is where Muhammad went, and that answer was right. The Temple Mount, which Muslims call the Haram al-Sharif, is honored in Islam as the destination of Muhammad's Night Journey. The Quran (Surah 17:1) speaks of his being carried by night to "the farthest mosque," which Islamic tradition identifies with Jerusalem, and from the rock on the Mount he is said to have ascended to heaven. The Dome of the Rock, a shrine rather than a mosque, was built over that rock in AD 691–692. Jewish tradition identifies the same rock with Mount Moriah, where Abraham bound Isaac (Genesis 22:2; 2 Chronicles 3:1). Islam tells that story with Ishmael, Abraham's firstborn, as the son who was offered, though Islamic tradition generally places the sacrifice near Mecca rather than in Jerusalem. Both the Dome of the Rock and the al-Aqsa Mosque stand on the platform today. *(See the Context note for this session for the Night Journey and the Dome of the Rock.)*

### Q: When were the Crusades, and what were they for?

The Crusades were a series of military campaigns launched by the Christian kingdoms of Europe in the Middle Ages, beginning in 1095. They had one central aim: to take back Jerusalem and the Holy Land from Islamic rule. The First Crusade captured Jerusalem in 1099, and the crusader kingdom held it until Saladin retook the city in 1187. The Third Crusade, led in part by Richard the Lionheart, failed to recover it. These events took place centuries before the caliphate's abolition in 1924. Jerusalem did not finally pass out of Islamic hands until the British took it in 1917, after the Ottoman defeat in the First World War.

### Q: Were the Crusades a response to Muslim aggression, or an unprovoked Christian attack?

An audience member raised a point here. Growing up in a military family, they had been taught that the Crusades were a response to the Muslims, while almost everyone else they knew believed they were an unprovoked Christian attack. The Crusades were a response. Islam had conquered the Holy Land, along with the rest of the Middle East and North Africa, and the Crusades were Europe's effort to take Jerusalem back. The immediate trigger was an appeal from the Byzantine emperor for help against the Seljuk Turks, who had overrun most of Anatolia after 1071, and Pope Urban II's call in 1095 to aid him and recover Jerusalem.

### Q: Would Sharia law be illegal in the United States?

Sharia is the body of Islamic law drawn from the Quran and Muslim tradition. It is fundamentally different from the Mosaic law and from the Ten Commandments, the Bill of Rights, and the American legal order built on them. Imposing it as law would violate the Constitution federally. Locally and at the state level, there is some wiggle room, especially where a majority holds power. And it is already operating in some places through Islamic arbitration bodies, such as the Dallas-area Islamic Tribunal, which handle family disputes among Muslims. People gain power through campaigns, campaigns run on money, much of that money comes from lobbying, and the lobbying money comes from the countries with the most wealth.

### Q: When did Israel get Jerusalem back, and does it control the Temple Mount now?

Israel declared independence in 1948, after the period of British rule that followed the Ottoman collapse. But Jordan held the Old City of Jerusalem, including the Temple Mount, until the Six-Day War in 1967. Since then Israel has controlled security on the Mount, but the site itself is administered by the Waqf, an Islamic trust overseen by the king of Jordan. Under the arrangement known as the status quo, Jews could visit but not pray there. That has begun to erode: police now permit Jewish prayer on parts of the site. Israel still does not have full control over it. Jordan's special role over the Muslim holy sites in Jerusalem is written into the 1994 Israel–Jordan peace treaty.

### Q: Could Mecca or Medina be Babylon?

They are the obvious first candidates, since they are the religious heart of Islam. But Revelation 18 describes Babylon as a seaport. The shipmasters, sailors, and everyone whose trade is on the sea mourn her (Revelation 18:17–19). Neither Mecca nor Medina is on the sea. Mecca lies roughly forty-five miles inland, and its port is Jeddah, on the Red Sea. The text points to a trading port.

### Q: What is the difference between Sunni and Shia, and how does Iran fit in?

Islam split into two main branches early in its history over who should lead the community after Muhammad. Sunni Muslims are the large majority worldwide: Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Turkey, and most of the Islamic world. Shia Muslims are a smaller share, concentrated in Iran, which is overwhelmingly Shia, and Iraq, which is majority Shia even though Saddam Hussein was a Sunni. Iran has served as the counterweight to Sunni power in the region. As its strength is diminished by war, that counterweight weakens and the Sunni powers are free to rise.

### Q: Is the caliphate the same thing as the Mahdi?

No. The caliphate is the political entity, a government. It is the single state that unites the nations and peoples of the Islamic world under one ruler, as it did under Muhammad's successors and later the Ottomans, until it was abolished in 1924. The Mahdi is the figure Islamic tradition expects at the end of the age to unite the Muslim world. In this study's reading, the Mahdi corresponds to the Antichrist, and the revived caliphate is his kingdom: as he rises, all those kings hand him their power, and one man rules all that land, united by religion.

### Q: Has Turkey's leadership talked about bringing the caliphate back?

Yes, openly enough to make headlines. Erdogan has called the Turkish Republic a continuation of the Ottoman Empire. In 2020, after Hagia Sophia was reconverted into a mosque, a pro-government Turkish magazine called on the country to prepare for the caliphate's return. A provincial official of Erdogan's own party has publicly urged the country to "get ready for the caliphate" of Erdogan. The centenary of the caliphate's abolition in 2024 drew renewed attention to the idea. The ruling party has also formally said Turkey will remain a secular republic.

### Q: Why would Saudi Arabia align with Turkey instead of relying on the United States?

Because in the Middle East, military strength is everything, and Saudi Arabia does not have it. Saudi forces have excellent equipment and years of American training, but they would not stand against Israel. Turkey has by far the most powerful military in the region apart from Israel, NATO's second-largest army, and a rapidly growing defense industry. Saudi Arabia also no longer trusts the United States the way it once did. Despite its American security agreement, it signed the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement with Turkey and Pakistan in August 2026. That is a sea change from the last fifty years.

### Q: You've never put it in terms of "a couple of years" before. Why now?

Because the chess pieces have never been in the places they are now. Everything is ready for the temple and for the sacrifices. The rules on the Temple Mount are loosening. The alliances in the region are forming. No date can be set. But this is not decades away, and when sacrifice resumes in Jerusalem, the seven years will already be underway.

### Q: Where does this information come from, if it isn't on the news?

From knowing what to look for and searching for it directly: the latest on the Temple Institute and the rebuilt temple, developments in specific countries, and sources embedded in the region, including missionaries and Israeli sources. Mainstream outlets rarely report on the temple preparations at all. No single source tells the whole story, so the work is connecting several of them. Much of it is simply history, and once the history is understood, current events begin to make sense.

### Q: Why are nations stockpiling gold instead of dollars?

Because gold is the only money that carries no counterparty risk to another sovereign country. Dollars held in reserves, or in American treasuries, move through a network the United States controls, and the United States can sanction a country and freeze those assets at any time. It froze hundreds of billions of dollars of Russia's reserves in 2022. Physical gold in a nation's own vaults cannot be frozen that way short of war. That is why central banks around the world have been buying gold at a record pace and why so many nations are trying to reduce their dependence on the dollar.
