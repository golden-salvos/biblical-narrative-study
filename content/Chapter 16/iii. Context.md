## Antiochus IV Epiphanes

Antiochus IV (reigned 175–164 BC) was the eighth king of the Seleucid Empire, which controlled Syria, Mesopotamia, and significant portions of Asia Minor and the Levant following the breakup of Alexander's empire. He was not the rightful heir; his ascension was secured through political manipulation while the legitimate heir was held hostage in Rome. His adopted title, *Epiphanes* ("God Manifest"), was mocked even in his own day; the Greek historian Polybius records that people called him *Epimanes*, "the Madman."

His campaign against Judaism was not simply religious hostility. It was part of a broader Hellenizing program aimed at cultural unification of his kingdom. He required Hellenistic practices and the worship of Greek gods throughout his territory. In Jerusalem, he sold the high priesthood to the highest bidder, installed his own candidates, and corrupted the temple cult. His direct desecrations of the temple, most infamously an altar to Olympian Zeus erected over the altar of burnt offering and the sacrifice of swine, occurred in 167 BC (1 Maccabees 1:47, 54; 2 Maccabees 6:2). He outlawed Sabbath observance, Torah reading, and circumcision, making Jewish religious practice a capital offense.

His actions correspond precisely to Daniel 11:21–35, which describe a contemptible king who seizes power through intrigue, desecrates the temple, removes the daily sacrifice, and sets up "the abomination that causes desolation." Later interpreters, including the author of 1 Maccabees, understood these passages as referring to Antiochus. Jesus' reference in Matthew 24:15 to a future abomination of desolation indicates that Antiochus's actions, while fulfilling a near reading of the prophecy, were not its terminal fulfillment.

## The Maccabean Revolt and Hanukkah

The priestly family at the center of the revolt is called the Maccabees, probably from the Hebrew *maqqevet* ("hammer"), a nickname attached to Judas, the most prominent military leader among the sons of Mattathias the priest. When Antiochus's enforcers arrived in the village of Modi'in to compel pagan sacrifice, Mattathias killed both the Seleucid officer and a Jewish collaborator who stepped forward to comply. The family fled to the hills and launched a guerrilla campaign.

The revolt lasted from approximately 167 to 160 BC. In 164 BC, three years after the desecration, the Maccabees retook Jerusalem and cleansed the temple. The rededication festival (*Hanukkah* means "dedication") lasted eight days. Later rabbinic tradition (Babylonian Talmud, Shabbat 21b) explains the eight days by the miracle of a one-day supply of ritually pure olive oil burning for the full eight days needed to prepare a new supply; 2 Maccabees 10:6–8 instead compares the celebration to the eight-day Feast of Tabernacles. Counted as 1,150 days (evening and morning sacrifices tallied separately), the 2,300 evenings and mornings of Daniel 8:14 come close to the three years and ten days between the desecration (1 Maccabees 1:54) and the rededication (1 Maccabees 4:52); counted as 2,300 days, they span roughly the whole persecution.

Four books bear the name Maccabees. First and Second Maccabees are canonical for Catholics and Orthodox (the Orthodox also accept Third Maccabees), and most Protestants value them as history without treating them as Scripture. First and Second Maccabees provide detailed accounts of the revolt, the desecration, and the rededication, and they are the primary historical sources for the period Daniel 8 describes.

## The Seleucid and Ptolemaic Empires

Of Alexander's four successor kingdoms, two dominate the prophetic material in Daniel 8 and 11: the Seleucid Empire (Syria and the East) and the Ptolemaic Empire (Egypt and the South). Their location on either side of the land of Israel made the Jewish people an unavoidable flashpoint in their long rivalry.

The **Seleucids** (kings of the North) controlled the region stretching from present-day Turkey and Syria through Mesopotamia and into Persia. Their chief capitals were Antioch on the Orontes (near the present Syrian-Turkish border) and Seleucia on the Tigris. The dynasty produced Antiochus IV and is the context in which the little horn of Daniel 8 most naturally fits.

The **Ptolemies** (kings of the South) controlled Egypt, Libya, and at times the southern Levant. Their capital was Alexandria, which became one of the foremost centers of Hellenistic culture, scholarship, and Jewish diaspora life. The Septuagint, the Greek translation of the Hebrew Scriptures, was begun in Alexandria, according to tradition under the patronage of Ptolemy II, a work of enormous significance for the early church.

Daniel 11 traces the conflicts between these two powers in extraordinary detail, including military campaigns, political marriages, betrayals, and sieges, most of which can be verified from Greek and Egyptian historical sources. This level of verifiable historical correspondence is a primary reason that critical scholars often argue Daniel was written after the fact rather than before, while traditional interpreters see it as evidence of genuine predictive prophecy.

## The Seventy-Weeks Calculation

The mathematical precision of Daniel 9:24–27 has been scrutinized extensively. The most detailed modern treatment along the lines this study follows is Harold Hoehner's *Chronological Aspects of the Life of Christ* (Zondervan, 1977), which refines the earlier calculation of Sir Robert Anderson's *The Coming Prince* (1894). The key variables are:

**The starting decree:** Artaxerxes's decree to Nehemiah, authorizing the rebuilding of Jerusalem's walls, is recorded in Nehemiah 2:1–8 and dated in the text to the twentieth year of Artaxerxes, the month of Nisan, which Hoehner places in March 444 BC (Anderson had used 445 BC).

**The calendar conversion:** The sixty-nine sevens (483 years) are calculated as prophetic years of 360 days, the twelve thirty-day months implied by the "1,260 days" and "time, times and half a time" of Revelation and Daniel, rather than solar years of 365.24 days. 483 prophetic years equal 173,880 days, which is about 476 solar years.

**The terminal event:** Counting 173,880 days forward from 5 March 444 BC, Hoehner arrives at 30 March AD 33, which he identifies as 10 Nisan, the day of the Triumphal Entry, when Passover lambs were selected, four days before they were slaughtered on 14 Nisan, the day of the crucifixion. (Anderson's older calculation ended on 6 April AD 32.)

The calendar mechanics are complex, but the claim is verifiable for anyone willing to work through Hoehner's reconstruction. On his reckoning, the alignment is exact to the day. Readers should know that interpreters differ on the starting decree (some begin with Cyrus in 538 BC or Artaxerxes's earlier decree to Ezra in 458/457 BC, often ending the count at Jesus' baptism around AD 27), on the length of the year, and on whether a gap belongs between the sixty-ninth and seventieth weeks at all.

## The Islamic Caliphate as the Seventh Kingdom

The traditional Protestant reading of the Revelation 17 seven kings identifies them as Rome and the Roman imperial line, or as seven successive forms of Roman government. The traditional Catholic reading also centers on Rome. The reading offered in this study, Egypt, Assyria, Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome, and the Islamic Caliphate as the seven, with the revived Caliphate as the eighth, is a minority position. Writers associated with this view include Joel Richardson (*The Islamic Antichrist*, 2009) and Walid Shoebat, among others who argue from the geographic and ethnic consistency of the prophetic material. This position is sometimes called the "Middle Eastern Antichrist" or "Islamic Antichrist" view.

The historical case for the Caliphate as the seventh king rests on several pillars. First, chronological fit: it is the dominant political-religious power that arose as Roman rule collapsed in the West and the eastern empire lost its southern provinces, not another form of Roman governance. Second, geographic fit: it controlled the same territories (Turkey, Syria, Mesopotamia, Egypt, North Africa) that Daniel's northern and southern empires occupied. Third, structural fit: the caliphate is a singular theopolitical entity unlike Western Christian kingdoms in that it combined absolute religious and governmental authority in one office, which matches the character of the beast more closely than any European state. Fourth, the dissolution: the caliphate was formally ended in 1924, creating the historical state of dormancy that the "once was, now is not, and yet will come" language of Revelation 17:8 describes.

The traditional Roman interpretation has a strong ancient pedigree and should not be dismissed casually. The claim here is not that Rome is irrelevant to prophecy, since it is clearly the sixth kingdom, but that the seventh and eighth are distinct from it and that the geographic data in Daniel, reinforced by Isaiah's use of "the Assyrian" as a prophetic title for the end-times enemy, points consistently to the Middle Eastern north rather than to Europe.
